## AI News Digest

### The AI Arms Race: New Models and Corporate Maneuvers

**Anthropic Launches Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5, Sparking Both Awe and Controversy**
Anthropic has released its new flagship model in two forms: Claude Fable 5, a publicly available version with strong safety guardrails, and Claude Mythos 5, an unrestricted version for trusted partners. This dual-release strategy aims to balance access and safety, but the heavy restrictions on Fable 5 have drawn significant backlash from developers and cybersecurity researchers who find it too constrained for their work. Despite the controversy, Fable 5 has demonstrated impressive long-term autonomy and capabilities, including playing complex video games like Factorio and Slay the Spire.
(Source: MarkTechPost, TechCrunch, Wired, The New Stack, WSJ, Towards AI)

**Apple Unveils "Apple Intelligence" and a Revamped Siri at WWDC 2026**
Apple announced a major push into AI with "Apple Intelligence," a suite of features integrated across iOS 27 and its other operating systems. The update brings a significantly more capable and context-aware Siri, which can take actions within and across apps, powered by on-device processing and private cloud compute. Apple is emphasizing a privacy-first approach to personal AI, and has also announced a partnership to integrate Google's Gemini for more complex queries when needed.
(Source: TechCrunch, Wired, WSJ)

**OpenAI Confidentially Files for IPO, Intensifying Rivalry**
Following in the footsteps of rival Anthropic, OpenAI has confidentially filed paperwork for an Initial Public Offering, marking a pivotal moment for the AI industry leader. The move signals a transition towards public markets as the company faces soaring compute costs and intense competition. In a related development, OpenAI is reportedly considering drastic price cuts for its API tokens to compete more aggressively with Anthropic and other rivals, potentially triggering a price war for AI services.
(Source: Wired, WSJ)

**Google Releases Experimental DiffusionGemma and Gemini Live Translate**
Google's DeepMind released DiffusionGemma, an experimental 26B open model that uses a text diffusion architecture, promising up to 4x faster generation on GPUs. The company also launched Gemini 3.5 Live Translate, a streaming speech-to-speech audio model that can translate over 70 languages in near real-time. This technology is being rolled out across Google Meet, the Translate app, and a new Gemini Live API for developers.
(Source: MarkTechPost)

**Amazon Borrows $17.5B as AI Spending Soars**
Amazon has secured a $17.5 billion loan from banks, following a recent bond sale, to fuel its continued massive investment in AI infrastructure and development. This move highlights the exorbitant capital required to compete in the AI arms race, with major tech companies burning through cash to build data centers and secure chips. A new report indicates the most "AI-pilled" firms are now spending roughly $7,500 per employee each month on AI services.
(Source: TechCrunch)

**OpenAI Models GPT-5.5 and Codex Arrive on Amazon Bedrock**
In a significant shift from its exclusive Azure arrangement, OpenAI's frontier models, including GPT-5.5, GPT-5.4, and Codex, are now generally available on Amazon Bedrock. This makes OpenAI's most advanced models accessible to AWS customers, with pricing matching OpenAI's direct rates and usage counting toward AWS commitments. GPT-5.4 is notably the first OpenAI model to be available in the AWS GovCloud region.
(Source: InfoQ)

**Cohere Launches "North Mini Code" for Developers**
Cohere has released its first open-weight coding model, North Mini Code, aimed at developers building agentic workflows. It is a 30B parameter Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model with 3B active parameters, a 256K context length, and is designed to run efficiently on a single GPU. The model is available on Hugging Face for developers to experiment with and build upon.
(Source: Hugging Face, MarkTechPost)

### AI Infrastructure and Hardware

**Meta Signs First India AI Data Center Deal with Reliance**
Meta has partnered with Reliance to build its first AI-focused data center in India, a 168-megawatt facility designed to support the company's global AI computing needs. This move is part of a broader trend of tech giants expanding their infrastructure footprint in key international markets. The deal comes as Opendoor's exit from India sparks a wider conversation about the role of AI in global outsourcing.
(Source: TechCrunch)

**KKR Launches $10 Billion AI Infrastructure Company**
Private equity giant KKR is launching Helix Digital Infrastructure, a $10 billion venture aimed at serving the comprehensive needs of hyperscale data centers. With backing from Nvidia and power company Vistra, Helix will act as a single coordination point for data centers, power, connectivity, and related services, addressing the massive infrastructure demands of the AI boom.
(Source: WSJ)

**China Opens World's First Wind-Powered Underwater Data Center**
China has launched an innovative underwater data center that is powered by wind and uses seawater for natural cooling, significantly reducing its environmental footprint. The facility, with an initial capacity of 24 megawatts, represents a novel approach to making the energy-intensive data center industry more sustainable. This follows news of an MIT-led startup, Ferveret, developing a nuclear-inspired cooling system for the same purpose.
(Source: Wired, MIT News)

**Anti-Nvidia Startup TensorWave Raises $350M for AMD-Powered Data Centers**
TensorWave, a data center startup, has raised $350 million in a new funding round, valuing the company at $1.55 billion. The company is positioning itself as an alternative to the Nvidia-dominated market by building its data centers with chips from competitor AMD, which is also an investor. This reflects a growing market desire for diversification in the AI chip supply chain.
(Source: WSJ)

**SpaceX IPO Fueled by "Space Data Center" Ambitions**
Much of the valuation in SpaceX's highly anticipated IPO is reportedly tied to its ambitious plans for hard-tech moonshots, including the development of space-based data centers. These orbital facilities would offer unique advantages for global AI computation. The IPO is also expected to have a transformative effect on Brownsville, Texas, the region accommodating the company's expansion.
(Source: TechCrunch, WSJ)

### AI Safety, Ethics, and Societal Impact

**xAI Sued by Engineer Fired After Raising Grok Safety Concerns**
A former engineer is suing xAI and SpaceX, alleging he was wrongfully terminated for raising AI safety concerns about the Grok model. The lawsuit claims the engineer was fired just days before SpaceX's historic IPO after he flagged potential risks, highlighting the ongoing tension between rapid development and safety protocols within the AI industry.
(Source: TechCrunch)

**ACLU Sues Police Over Wrongful Arrest from Flawed Face Recognition**
The ACLU is suing two Florida police departments after a man was wrongfully arrested in a child-abduction case based on a flawed face-recognition match. The lawsuit argues that officers treated the output of one of the oldest police face-recognition tools in the US as a near-certain identification, exposing the dangers of over-reliance on the technology in law enforcement.
(Source: Wired)

**Meta Removes Face-Recognition Code from Smart Glasses App**
Following a report from WIRED, Meta has deleted face-recognition system code from the latest version of Meta AI, the companion app for its smart glasses. The company has not commented on why the code was removed or if it plans to reintroduce the feature, but the move follows significant privacy concerns about the potential for real-time facial identification.
(Source: Wired)

**Warner Music Acquires AI Attribution Startup Sureel AI**
Warner Music Group (WMG) has acquired Sureel AI, a startup specializing in AI-powered music attribution. The acquisition aims to help WMG better track when its artists' music is used to train AI models or included in AI-generated content, a critical step for ensuring proper compensation and credit in the age of generative AI.
(Source: TechCrunch)

**MIT Study Warns AI Can Weaken Human Ability to Detect Fake News**
A new study from the MIT Media Lab suggests that over-reliance on AI tools for information can impair our natural ability to detect fake news. The research draws a parallel to how GPS has weakened human navigation skills, cautioning that using AI as a crutch for news verification could make society more vulnerable to misinformation in the long run.
(Source: MIT News)

### AI in Business and Consumer Applications

**DoorDash Launches "Ask DoorDash" AI Chatbot for Ordering**
DoorDash has introduced a new AI-powered chatbot, "Ask DoorDash," that allows users to find and order food and other items using natural language prompts and even photos. Instead of manually browsing menus, customers can describe what they want (e.g., "spicy chicken wings near me") and the AI will provide relevant options, streamlining the ordering process.
(Source: TechCrunch)

**Nvidia and Abridge Partner on AI Model for Healthcare**
Nvidia is collaborating with healthcare AI startup Abridge to develop a new foundation model specifically trained for clinical conversations. Abridge, known for its AI-powered medical note-taking technology, will work with the chip giant to create a model that can summarize and structure information from doctor-patient interactions, aiming to reduce administrative burdens on clinicians.
(Source: WSJ)

**Visa to Secure Payments on ChatGPT in OpenAI Partnership**
Visa has announced a partnership with OpenAI to secure payments made through AI agents and chatbots powered by OpenAI's models. When users make purchases via ChatGPT, their transactions will be protected by Visa's security network and credentialing capabilities, a move that could significantly boost consumer trust in AI-driven commerce.
(Source: WSJ)

**Decart Launches World Model for Photorealistic Driving Simulation**
AI startup Decart is launching Oasis 3, a real-time world model capable of generating hours of photorealistic driving environments for autonomous vehicle testing. Now available via an API, the platform allows developers to simulate a wide range of driving scenarios, accelerating the training and validation of self-driving systems with some caveats on its current capabilities.
(Source: TechCrunch)

### The Developer Ecosystem: Tools, Startups, and Research

**Harvard and Perplexity Study Reveals AI Agents' Massive Autonomy Gains**
A new study from Harvard and Perplexity found that AI agents can perform an average of 26 minutes of autonomous work per session, compared to just 33 seconds for traditional search assistants. The research used matched-pair sessions to demonstrate that agentic AI offers significant gains in autonomy, time, and cost-efficiency, while enabling users to tackle much broader and more complex tasks.
(Source: MarkTechPost)

**Datadog Veterans Launch AI Coding Startup Niteshift with $7M Seed Round**
A team of veterans from Datadog has launched Niteshift, an AI coding agent startup, after raising a $7 million seed round from prominent angel investors. Niteshift is betting that companies will prefer flexible, powerful AI coding tools that avoid vendor lock-in with major model providers, giving them more control over their development stack.
(Source: TechCrunch)

**Jedify Raises $24M to Arm AI Agents with Business Context**
Startup Jedify has raised a $24 million funding round led by Norwest, with strategic investment from Snowflake Ventures. The company provides a platform to help enterprises equip their AI agents with deep, relevant context about their specific business operations, data, and workflows, making the agents more effective and reliable.
(Source: TechCrunch)

**Nous Research Ships Hermes Agent Profile Builder**
Nous Research has released the Hermes Agent Profile Builder, a new dashboard that streamlines the creation of complete AI agent profiles. The tool consolidates identity, model selection, skills, and server configuration into a single, user-friendly workflow, replacing what was previously a complex, multi-step command-line process for developers.
(Source: MarkTechPost)

**Mathematician Terry Tao Becomes an Evangelist for AI in Math**
Famed mathematician and Fields Medalist Terry Tao is increasingly advocating for the use of AI in mathematical research. He highlights the power of automated proof-checkers and formal verification systems, which allow mathematicians to break down complex problems into smaller, verifiable chunks, heralding a new era of collaboration between human and machine intelligence in pure mathematics.
(Source: Quanta Magazine)

### Global Economy &amp; Geopolitical Tensions

**Markets Rattled by Iran Conflict, Soaring Inflation, and Central Bank Hikes**
Global markets experienced significant volatility, with the Dow suffering its worst day of 2026, driven by escalating military conflict between the U.S. and Iran. President Trump's threats of renewed attacks caused oil prices to surge, fueling fears of sustained high inflation. The World Bank warned that a prolonged conflict and blockage of the Strait of Hormuz could slash global economic growth to just 1.3%, half its 2025 pace. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Economy)

**Central Banks Respond to War-Driven Inflation**
In response to rising energy prices and inflation, the European Central Bank raised interest rates for the first time since 2023, marking a significant policy shift. Meanwhile, U.S. data showed both consumer and wholesale prices accelerating at their fastest pace in three years, with the Consumer Price Index rising quickly and the Producer-Price Index up 1.1% in May, diminishing prospects for a Federal Reserve rate cut. Elsewhere, Bank Indonesia executed a surprise rate hike to support its currency, while the Bank of Canada held its rates steady. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Economy)

**U.S. Economic Indicators Show Mixed Signals**
Despite inflation pressures, U.S. home sales in May saw their largest rise of the year, increasing 3.2% as easing mortgage rates and more inventory attracted buyers. However, the labor market showed potential signs of softening, as initial jobless claims rose to 229,000, surpassing analyst expectations. President Trump stated "I love the inflation," asserting his administration's agenda is working despite prices outpacing wage growth for many workers. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy, NYT &gt; Business)

**Citigroup to Offer Tokenized Shares of Private Companies**
Citigroup is launching a new service to create and manage tokenized securities representing shares in private companies, using blockchain technology to bring liquidity to traditionally illiquid assets. The bank hopes this initiative will attract other financial institutions to participate in building out the infrastructure for tokenized private equity. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**Major US Banks Probed for "Debanking" Practices**
The U.S. Attorney’s Office in D.C. has subpoenaed major financial institutions, including JPMorgan and Bank of America, as part of an investigation into alleged "debanking." A deep dive into the microfinance industry revealed that hundreds of billions of dollars in small loans have largely failed to lift borrowers out of poverty. **New "Net Zero" Corporate Climate Rules Established**
The Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi), a key standard-setter for corporate climate goals, has released new rules for companies pursuing "net zero" emissions. The updated framework will now allow companies to use carbon removals and environmental credits as part of their decarbonization strategies, a move that could significantly alter corporate climate accounting. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)
