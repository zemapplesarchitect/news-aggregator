## AI News Digest

### AI Business, Funding, and Legal Battles

**OpenAI and Microsoft Renegotiate Partnership for Greater Flexibility**
OpenAI and Microsoft have restructured their high-profile partnership, granting OpenAI significantly more operational freedom. The new agreement allows OpenAI to sell its products and services on any cloud platform, including competitors like Amazon Web Services, resolving potential legal issues for Microsoft related to a separate $50B deal with Amazon. In return for this flexibility, Microsoft will reportedly receive a more favorable revenue-sharing agreement, marking a new chapter in one of the AI boom's most critical alliances. (Source: TechCrunch, WSJ.com)

**DeepMind Pioneer David Silver Raises $1.1B for New AI Lab**
David Silver, a key researcher behind DeepMind's AlphaGo, has secured $1.1 billion in funding for his new UK-based AI lab, Ineffable Intelligence. The company, now valued at $5.1 billion, aims to pursue a different path to advanced AI by focusing on reinforcement learning to create "superlearners" that do not depend on massive human-generated datasets. This represents a major investment in an alternative research direction to the dominant approach of scaling large language models. (Source: TechCrunch, Wired)

**Cohere and Aleph Alpha Merge to Create "Sovereign AI" Alternative**
Canadian AI startup Cohere is merging with Germany-based Aleph Alpha in a major consolidation move backed by German retail and tech giant Schwarz Group. The deal is explicitly positioned as an effort to create a "sovereign alternative" for enterprises in Europe and beyond, providing a non-US-centric option in an AI market dominated by American companies. The merger has reportedly received the blessing of both the Canadian and German governments, highlighting its geopolitical significance. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Elon Musk's Lawsuit Against OpenAI Proceeds to Trial**
The legal battle between Elon Musk and OpenAI is moving forward, with jury selection beginning for the trial. Musk's lawsuit alleges that OpenAI and its CEO Sam Altman have betrayed the company's founding non-profit mission by becoming a closed-source, for-profit entity closely tied to Microsoft. The proceedings have been marked by public maneuvering, with Musk promoting a critical article about Altman on X, and the jury selection process revealing that some potential jurors hold negative opinions of Musk himself. (Source: Wired, WSJ.com)

**OpenAI Reportedly Misses Key Revenue and User Growth Targets**
According to a Wall Street Journal report, OpenAI has failed to meet its internal targets for revenue and user growth, raising concerns ahead of a potential future IPO. The slowing growth has reportedly prompted the company's CFO and board members to question the scale of its massive spending on data center infrastructure. This comes as OpenAI faces increasingly stiff competition in the enterprise market from rivals like Anthropic. (Source: WSJ.com)

**GitHub Copilot Switches to Usage-Based Billing**
GitHub announced it is overhauling the pricing model for its popular AI coding assistant, Copilot, shifting from a flat-rate subscription to usage-based billing. This change reflects the significant and climbing computational costs required to run powerful AI models at scale. The move will directly impact how companies budget for developer tools, making costs more variable and potentially higher for teams that rely heavily on the assistant. (Source: The New Stack)

**Bloomberg Terminal Gets a Major AI Upgrade**
Bloomberg is integrating generative AI capabilities directly into its iconic Terminal, a ubiquitous platform in the financial industry. The AI makeover will introduce chatbot-style features, allowing traders and analysts to query data, summarize information, and automate workflows using natural language. This represents a significant modernization of a critical piece of financial infrastructure, driven by the advancements in large language models. (Source: Wired)

### Geopolitics and Regulation

**China Blocks Meta's $2B Acquisition of Robotics Firm Manus**
Chinese regulators have officially blocked Meta's planned $2 billion acquisition of Manus, a company specializing in AI-powered robotics and haptic technology. Citing national security grounds, Beijing has ordered the deal to be unwound, a move widely interpreted as an effort to prevent Chinese AI talent and intellectual property from being acquired by Western tech giants. The decision is a major setback for Meta's strategic push into AI agents and the metaverse. (Source: TechCrunch, WSJ.com)

**Maine Governor Vetoes Moratorium on New Data Centers**
The governor of Maine has vetoed a legislative bill that would have imposed the country's first statewide moratorium on the construction of new data centers. The bill, L.D. 307, would have halted new projects until November 2027, reflecting growing local concerns over the immense energy and water consumption of the facilities that power the AI industry. The veto highlights the ongoing tension between economic development and the environmental impact of AI's infrastructure boom. (Source: TechCrunch)

### New Models and Research Frontiers

**Meta Releases Sapiens2, a High-Resolution Human-Centric Vision Model**
Meta AI's Reality Labs has launched Sapiens2, a new foundation model for human-centric computer vision that achieves state-of-the-art results. The model can perform high-resolution pose estimation, body segmentation, and 3D geometry reconstruction (including normals and albedo) from a single image, all from a single model backbone. This technology is a significant step forward for creating realistic virtual avatars and enhancing human-computer interaction in AR/VR. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**OpenMOSS Debuts MOSS-Audio, a Unified Open-Source Audio Model**
The OpenMOSS project has released MOSS-Audio, an open-source foundation model designed to unify multiple audio domains. The model can process and reason about speech, environmental sounds, and music within a single architecture. According to the project, it outperforms every other open-source model on general audio benchmarks, including systems that are more than four times its size, making it a powerful new tool for audio-based AI applications. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**"Talkie-1930" LLM Created for Historical Reasoning Research**
Researchers have developed and released Talkie-1930, a 13-billion parameter open-weight LLM trained exclusively on a dataset of English text published before 1931. By intentionally excluding all modern knowledge, the model acts as a unique "time capsule" for studying historical reasoning and LLM generalization. It cannot comprehend concepts like World War II, smartphones, or the internet, providing a clean baseline for historical and linguistic analysis. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Anthropic Creates Test Marketplace for Agent-on-Agent Commerce**
In a novel experiment, AI safety and research company Anthropic built a classified marketplace where AI agents autonomously conducted business. The agents, representing both buyers and sellers, successfully negotiated and executed transactions for real goods using real money. This research provides a practical look into the future of an "agentic economy," where autonomous systems could manage commercial tasks on behalf of humans. (Source: TechCrunch)

**MIT Develops "EnergAIzer" for Rapid AI Power Consumption Estimates**
A new method from MIT researchers, dubbed "EnergAIzer," can estimate the power consumption of AI models in seconds, a dramatic speed-up from previous techniques. This tool is significant because it enables data center operators to allocate computational resources more efficiently and reduce wasted energy. Accurately predicting power draw is a critical challenge in managing the growing energy footprint of the AI industry. (Source: MIT News - Artificial intelligence)

### AI Agents and Robotics

**Kakao Mobility Details Roadmap for Level 4 Autonomous Driving**
South Korean technology firm Kakao Mobility has announced its strategic roadmap to develop Level 4 autonomous driving technology in-house. As part of its broader "physical AI" strategy, the company aims to create autonomous driving services that can operate without any human intervention in most conditions. This signals a major commitment to real-world robotics and automation from a key player in the Asian tech market. (Source: AI News)

**OpenAI Rumored to be Developing an AI-Centric Smartphone**
According to industry analyst Ming-Chi Kuo, OpenAI may be working on its own smartphone in collaboration with hardware manufacturers MediaTek, Qualcomm, and Luxshare. The rumored device would reportedly center its user experience around AI agents, potentially replacing the traditional app-based paradigm. This follows a growing trend of new hardware ventures aiming to create devices built from the ground up for an AI-first world. (Source: TechCrunch)

**New Class of "Physical AI" Models Powering Real-World Robots**
A new category of foundation models, designed specifically for physical action rather than text generation, is beginning to be deployed in robotics. These "physical AI" models are closing the gap between the abstract reasoning of LLMs and the practical demands of operating in the real world. They are currently being used to power more advanced robots in factories, warehouses, and research labs. (Source: MarkTechPost)

### Infrastructure, Hardware, and Energy

**Lightelligence IPO Soars on Bet That Optics Will Solve AI Bottleneck**
Lightelligence, a startup specializing in optical interconnects, saw its market capitalization briefly surge to $10 billion following its public debut. The massive valuation reflects a strong investor belief that using light (optics) to transmit data between chips is the key to solving the next major AI infrastructure bottleneck. Conventional copper wiring is struggling to handle the immense data-transfer demands of large-scale AI clusters, creating an opening for new hardware solutions. (Source: AI News)

**Meta Signs Deal for Space-Based Solar Power for Data Centers**
In a forward-looking move to secure clean energy, Meta has signed a contract with startup Overview Energy to purchase solar power generated in orbit and beamed down to Earth. While the initial deal is small, it represents a pioneering effort to tap into a constant source of renewable energy that is not subject to weather or the day-night cycle. This could be a long-term solution for powering the energy-intensive data centers required for AI. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Chip Startup Emerges to Tackle AI's "Memory Wall"**
A new chip startup, founded by veterans from Google and Meta, is developing technology to solve the "memory wall" problem that plagues AI hardware. This bottleneck occurs when powerful, expensive AI processors are left idle while waiting for data to be fetched from memory, severely limiting real-world performance. Overcoming this is a critical challenge as AI models continue to grow in size and complexity, overwhelming current server architectures. (Source: WSJ.com)

### Global Economy &amp; Geopolitical Tensions
**Iran Conflict Disrupts Global Economy and Spikes Oil Prices**
Stalled peace talks between the U.S. and Iran over the blockage of the Strait of Hormuz are causing significant global economic turmoil. Oil prices have jumped on the news, fueling inflation concerns and prompting a trade group for budget airlines to seek a $2.5 billion government bailout to offset soaring jet fuel costs. While the U.S. economy has remained largely insulated, the conflict is creating major headwinds for international partners. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Markets)

**China and Europe Feel the Economic Strain**
The ongoing war in the Middle East is beginning to show its effects on major economies, with China’s manufacturing sector starting to falter despite its strategic energy reserves. In Europe, German consumer sentiment has plunged to a three-year low, and Eurozone inflation expectations jumped to 4% in March. Meanwhile, Spain's unemployment rate climbed back above 10% in the first quarter, highlighting the region's vulnerability to energy shocks. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Economy)

**Moody's Upgrades China's Outlook to Stable**
In a contrasting development, Moody's Ratings has upgraded China's credit outlook from negative to stable. The agency cited the country's economic and fiscal strength as sufficient to navigate both domestic and international challenges, suggesting underlying resilience despite the pressures from global conflicts. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)
