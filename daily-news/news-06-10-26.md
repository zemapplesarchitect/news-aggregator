## FINANCEANDTECHNOLOGY News Digest

### Macroeconomics &amp; Central Bank Policy

**U.S. Inflation Rises, Dampening Rate Cut Hopes**
The U.S. Consumer Price Index (CPI) rose 4.2% in May, marking the fastest pace in three years, largely driven by surging energy costs linked to geopolitical conflict. This persistent inflation has put investors on edge and makes it highly unlikely that the Federal Reserve, under its new chair Kevin M. Warsh, will consider cutting interest rates in the near future, challenging bond markets. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Economy)

**Global Central Banks Diverge on Inflation Response**
Central banks worldwide are taking different approaches to rising inflation. The European Central Bank is expected to raise its inflation forecast and hike interest rates, while China's factory-gate inflation also accelerated due to commodity costs. In contrast, the Bank of Canada held its policy rate steady at 2.25% to balance a weak economy against higher energy prices, while Bank Indonesia executed a surprise rate hike to defend its currency. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy, WSJ.com: Markets)

**Bank of Japan Faces Uncertainty Amid Economic Slowdown**
The Japanese economy grew at a slightly slower pace than initially estimated in the first quarter, complicating its monetary policy outlook. Adding to the uncertainty, Bank of Japan Governor Kazuo Ueda has been hospitalized and is expected to miss the central bank's upcoming policy meeting. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**U.S. Social Security Fund Faces Depletion Risk**
A new report warns that the trust fund for Social Security is on a path to be depleted within the next six years. If Congress does not act, the 68 million Americans receiving benefits could face an average cut of 22%, creating significant fiscal pressure. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)

**U.S. Home Sales Rebound in May**
Sales of existing homes in the U.S. saw their largest monthly increase of the year, rising 3.2% in May. The rebound was driven by a combination of slightly easing mortgage rates and an increase in the number of homes for sale, which attracted buyers back to the market. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

### Geopolitics &amp; Energy Markets

**Mideast Conflict Drives Oil Prices and Market Volatility**
A wave of strikes in the Middle East, straining a cease-fire between the U.S. and Iran, caused oil prices to jump and global stock markets to fall. The conflict has become a primary driver of market volatility and has tested the economic stability of trade-dependent hubs like Dubai. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Markets)

**Nations Rush to Stockpile Oil, Keeping Prices Elevated**
In response to energy shocks from the ongoing war, countries are moving to build larger emergency crude oil reserves to protect themselves from future supply disruptions. This global rush to stockpile is expected to keep oil prices higher for a longer period. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business)

### The AI Revolution

**AI Startups Secure Massive Funding Amid Investment Frenzy**
The investment boom in artificial intelligence continues with two major funding rounds. AI-focused cybersecurity firm Cyera raised $600 million, achieving a $12 billion valuation. Meanwhile, data center startup TensorWave, which uses AMD chips as an alternative to Nvidia, secured $350 million in a new round, valuing the company at $1.55 billion. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Markets)

**AI's Impact on Jobs and Industries Expands**
Analysis suggests that AI poses a significant threat to millions of middle-class, back-office jobs in fields like HR, billing, and payroll, which are predominantly held by women. In a more positive application, Johnson &amp; Johnson's CEO stated that AI is already accelerating the company's drug development, making a cure for certain cancers a "realistic goal." (Source: NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Markets)

**Tech Giants Face AI Hurdles and Government Scrutiny**
Apple's planned major AI upgrade for its Siri assistant will be indefinitely delayed in Europe due to regulatory disputes. In the U.S., President Trump has publicly floated the idea of the government taking ownership stakes in major AI companies, as OpenAI reportedly tests investor appetite for a potential IPO. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)

**AI Gains Traction as a Tool for Advanced Mathematics**
Fields Medalist mathematician Terry Tao has become a vocal proponent of using AI-powered automated proof-checkers in mathematical research. This technology allows researchers to break down complex problems into smaller, verifiable parts, potentially heralding a new era in the field. (Source: Quanta Magazine)

### Markets, IPOs &amp; Investments

**SpaceX Prepares for Landmark Initial Public Offering**
The highly anticipated IPO for SpaceX is expected to be a record-setting event, marking a new high point for retail investor participation in Wall Street. The offering is designed to attract a large base of individual investors, with CEO Elon Musk's potential to become a trillionaire tied to its success, while also creating significant wealth for many company employees. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business)

**Wall Street Uses SpaceX IPO to Court the Super Rich**
Major banks are offering their wealthiest clients exclusive access to the SpaceX IPO, underscoring the growing importance of their wealth management divisions. However, historical analysis suggests that the largest IPO returns are typically reserved for the select few who receive shares at the initial offer price. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Markets)

**Investors Seek Shelter from Chip Stock Turbulence**
Amid recent volatility in high-flying semiconductor stocks, investors are rotating into other market sectors to reduce risk. Popular alternatives include transportation stocks, the use of options for hedging, and a renewed focus on profitable, non-tech companies. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

### Corporate Deals &amp; Strategy

**Major Deals Set to Reshape Banking and Energy Sectors**
In Italy's banking sector, Intesa has launched a $35 billion bid for rival Monte dei Paschi, continuing a wave of industry consolidation. In the U.S., a mega-merger is underway in the utility sector, with the nation's largest utility poised to acquire Dominion Energy, a peer with significant data-center exposure. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**Private Equity and Pharma M&amp;A Activity Remains Strong**
Irish conglomerate DCC announced its board intends to accept a revised $7.6 billion takeover offer from a private-equity consortium led by KKR and Energy Capital Partners. In the healthcare sector, GSK is moving forward with a $10.6 billion acquisition of Nuvalent. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**GM Pivots to Energy Storage as EV Sales Weaken**
General Motors is shifting its strategy to develop and manufacture large-scale energy storage batteries for utilities and data centers. This move follows the lead of Tesla and comes as GM faces flagging sales of its consumer electric vehicles. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)

**Dutch Government Blocks U.S. Tech Acquisition on Security Grounds**
The Netherlands has blocked a $115 million deal for U.S.-based Kyndryl to acquire Dutch firm Solvinity, which manages the country's national ID system. The government cited the deal as a "threat to the public interest," highlighting growing transatlantic tensions over technology and data sovereignty. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)

### Regulation &amp; Governance

**New U.S. Rules Proposed for Prediction Markets**
The Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) has proposed clearer regulations for what is permissible on prediction markets like Kalshi. The new rules aim to provide clear parameters for event-based contracts without issuing outright bans, while Kalshi is also developing internal plans to combat potential insider trading. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**New Paramount CEO Pledges Editorial Independence for '60 Minutes'**
Following a change in corporate control, new Paramount CEO David Ellison has personally assured correspondents at the news program '60 Minutes' that he will respect the show's long-standing editorial independence and decision-making processes. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)
