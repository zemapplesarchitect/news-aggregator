## AI News Digest

### Policy, Governance, and National Strategy
**Trump Administration Reportedly Discusses Taking Equity Stakes in AI Companies**
The Trump administration is reportedly in discussions with AI industry leaders, including OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, about the U.S. government taking financial stakes in major AI companies. The stated purpose of such a deal would be to ensure that the American public can benefit financially from the success and growth of the artificial intelligence sector. (Source: TechCrunch, WSJ)

**Anthropic Reportedly Blacklisted by Pentagon for Refusing Domestic Surveillance Role**
A report from Towards AI claims that the U.S. government designated Anthropic a national security supply-chain risk on February 27, effectively blacklisting it from federal contracts. This action was allegedly taken after the company refused to allow its AI models to be used for the purpose of spying on American citizens, highlighting a major conflict between AI safety principles and government intelligence demands. (Source: Towards AI)

**Report Alleges Anthropic is Aiding NSA Hackers**
In a seemingly contradictory development, a separate report from Wired alleges that Anthropic is actively helping NSA hackers. This claim, combined with the report of the Pentagon blacklisting, paints a complex and murky picture of the AI safety company's relationships with different arms of the U.S. national security apparatus. (Source: Wired)

**Canada Launches $360 Million Fund for Sovereign AI**
The Canadian government has announced a new $360 million tech growth fund as a key component of its national artificial intelligence strategy. The initiative is designed to provide critical support to promising domestic AI companies, with the goal of fostering a robust and sovereign Canadian AI industry. (Source: WSJ)

**Estonia Offers Free ChatGPT Access to Citizens**
The nation of Estonia is reportedly providing its citizens with free access to ChatGPT in a national initiative. The government has framed the program as a measure to combat "brain rot" and enhance digital literacy across its population, representing a novel state-level adoption of generative AI for public education. (Source: WSJ)

**White House AI Advisor Sriram Krishnan Departs Role**
Sriram Krishnan is leaving his position as an AI advisor to the White House. He is reportedly planning to establish a new institution to continue shaping and influencing the Trump administration's policies on artificial intelligence from outside the government. (Source: TechCrunch)

**OpenClaw Incident Highlights AI Agent Accountability Problem**
The autonomous use of developer Gavriel Cohen's code by the AI agent "OpenClaw" has ignited a debate about legal and ethical accountability. The case exposes the significant gray area that exists when AI agents independently find, use, and modify open-source code without human oversight, raising questions of liability and ownership. (Source: The New Stack)

Apple is widely expected to unveil its comprehensive AI strategy at its upcoming Worldwide Developers Conference (WWDC). The centerpiece is anticipated to be a fundamentally revamped and more capable Siri, alongside a new suite of on-device and cloud-based AI features branded as "Apple Intelligence." Anthropic President Daniela Amodei announced that the company's annualized revenue surpassed $47 billion in May, a massive jump from approximately $9 billion at the end of 2025. At its Build 2026 conference, Microsoft declared a strategic shift, positioning Windows as an "AI Operating System." (Source: Towards AI)

**Airbnb Announces Plans to Launch a New, Dedicated AI Lab**
CEO Brian Chesky has revealed that Airbnb will be launching its own AI lab. ### Hardware and Infrastructure
**Google Signs Massive $920M/Month Compute Deal with SpaceX**
In a landmark agreement, Google will pay SpaceX nearly $11 billion annually, or $920 million per month, for cloud computing capacity. The deal is a direct response to the unexpectedly high demand for Google's new AI products and provides a significant new revenue stream for SpaceX's own AI business as it reportedly heads towards an IPO. (Source: TechCrunch, WSJ)

**AirTrunk Commits $30B to Build 5GW of AI Data Centers in India**
Australian data center operator AirTrunk has announced a monumental $30 billion investment plan to construct 5 gigawatts of AI-ready data center capacity in India. This project underscores the colossal global infrastructure investment required to meet the surging demand for specialized computing power to train and run advanced AI models. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Meta Adopts Tents for Faster Data Center Construction**
To accelerate its infrastructure build-out, Meta is reportedly using large tents to house data centers, an unconventional method also used by Tesla. This tactic aims to drastically reduce construction time and help the company meet the urgent, ever-growing demand for compute power driven by its AI initiatives. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Ireland Mandates "Bring Your Own Power" for New Data Centers**
The Irish government has implemented a new policy requiring tech companies to source their own power for any new data centers built in the country. The move is a test case for how nations can attract massive AI investment without straining their national power grids or causing energy prices to rise for their citizens. (Source: WSJ)

**Perplexity AI Debuts Hybrid On-Device and Cloud Inference Orchestrator**
Perplexity AI has announced a hybrid inference system for personal computers that automatically routes AI tasks between the user's local hardware and more powerful cloud models. This orchestrator aims to optimize performance by balancing speed, privacy, and capability, running simpler tasks on-device and sending more complex ones to the cloud. (Source: MarkTechPost)

## FINANCEANDTECHNOLOGY News Digest

### US Economy &amp; Market Performance

**Strong Jobs Report Rattles Markets, Stokes Rate Hike Fears**
The U.S. economy added 172,000 jobs in May, significantly beating expectations and keeping the unemployment rate steady at 4.3%. This strong labor market data, which the White House celebrated, weakened stocks and sent bond yields soaring, as investors now see a reduced chance of the Federal Reserve cutting interest rates and a renewed possibility of rate hikes to combat resurgent inflation. The S&amp;P 500 suffered its worst one-day drop of the year, ending a nine-week winning streak, while the 2-year Treasury yield jumped to its highest level in a year. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy, NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Markets)

**Tech Rally Drives Stock Fund Gains Despite Volatility**
Despite recent downturns, U.S. stock funds are up an average of 11.5% year-to-date, largely thanks to a powerful rally in a small group of big technology companies. However, this dependence on a few megacap tech stocks creates vulnerability, as demonstrated by a recent "carnage" in chip stocks that hit the top-heavy market particularly hard. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**Trump's Market Influence Proves Potent**
An analysis suggests that when former President Trump "jawbones" the market on topics from oil to interest rates, he often succeeds in moving it in his desired direction. This raises questions about whether such influence serves the broader economy, but indicates that betting against his market pronouncements can be a perilous strategy for investors. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, WSJ.com: Economy)
