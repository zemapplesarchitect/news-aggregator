## AI News Digest

### Corporate, Legal &amp; Geopolitical Maneuvers

**Musk vs. OpenAI Trial Begins with Testimony on AI's Existential Risk**
The high-profile lawsuit brought by Elon Musk against OpenAI and its CEO Sam Altman has commenced, with Musk testifying that he co-founded the organization to prevent a "Terminator outcome" and accuses the company of betraying its non-profit mission. The trial, held in federal court in Oakland, scrutinizes OpenAI's transformation into a for-profit entity and its close ties with Microsoft. The judge has cautioned both parties against using social media to escalate the conflict, while jury selection revealed some potential jurors hold negative opinions of Musk. (Source: WSJ.com)

**OpenAI Amends Microsoft Deal, Opening Sales to AWS and Other Rivals**
In a significant strategic shift, OpenAI has renegotiated its partnership with major investor Microsoft, ending Microsoft's exclusive rights and allowing OpenAI to sell its models and services on competing cloud platforms like Amazon Web Services (AWS). In exchange, Microsoft will receive a larger share of revenue. The move provides OpenAI with greater market access and financial autonomy, and AWS immediately announced it would begin offering OpenAI products, including a new agent service. (Source: TechCrunch)

**China Blocks Meta's $2 Billion Acquisition of Manus**
Chinese regulators have vetoed Meta's planned $2 billion acquisition of Manus, a Dutch startup specializing in haptics and motion-capture technology crucial for developing AI agents and metaverse applications. The decision, following a months-long probe, is seen as a major setback for Meta's AI agent strategy and a signal of China's intent to prevent its domestic AI talent and technology from being acquired by foreign entities. Meta is now preparing to unwind the deal. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Google Expands Pentagon AI Access After Competitor's Refusal**
Google has signed a new contract with the U.S. Department of Defense, expanding the Pentagon's access to its AI tools for use in classified settings. The agreement notably follows reports that competitor Anthropic refused a similar deal over ethical concerns. To address such concerns, Google's contract explicitly states its AI is not intended for use in domestic mass surveillance or autonomous weapons systems, highlighting the delicate balance tech giants must strike in military collaborations. (Source: WSJ.com)

**OpenAI Reportedly Misses Key Revenue and User Targets**
Internal concerns are reportedly growing at OpenAI as the company has missed key revenue and user growth targets, according to sources familiar with the matter. The slowing growth has led the company's CFO and board to question the scale of its massive data center spending. This comes as the company faces increasing competition and navigates the high-stakes path toward a potential IPO. (Source: WSJ.com)

**Meta Contractor in Ireland Faces Potential Layoffs of Over 700 AI Workers**
More than 700 workers employed by Covalen, a contractor for Meta in Ireland, are at risk of losing their jobs. These employees are involved in the crucial work of training Meta's AI models. The potential layoffs highlight the precarious nature of contract work that underpins much of the AI industry's data labeling and moderation efforts. (Source: Wired)

### Major Funding &amp; Market Moves

**DeepMind Pioneer David Silver Raises $1.1B for New AI Venture**
David Silver, a key mind behind DeepMind's AlphaGo, has launched a new company, Ineffable Intelligence, with a massive $1.1 billion in funding at a $5.1 billion valuation. The British AI lab aims to build "superlearners" that can learn without relying on vast amounts of human-generated data, betting that current LLM approaches are hitting a data wall. This represents a significant new direction in the quest for artificial general intelligence, focusing on reinforcement learning principles. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Scout AI Raises $100M to Develop AI Agents for Warfare**
Defense tech startup Scout AI, founded by Coby Adcock, has secured $100 million in funding to train its AI models for military applications. The company is developing AI agents designed to allow individual soldiers to control and coordinate fleets of autonomous vehicles on the battlefield. A visit to the company's training ground revealed its focus on creating practical, soldier-operated AI systems for complex combat scenarios. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Ex-Twitter CEO's AI Search Startup Valued at $2 Billion**
Parallel Web Systems, an AI startup founded by former Twitter CEO Parag Agrawal, has raised $100 million in a Series B funding round, bringing its valuation to $2 billion. The company is focused on building a new kind of web search infrastructure specifically designed for AI agents, rather than humans. The significant investment underscores the market's belief that a new data-access layer is needed for the next generation of autonomous AI. (Source: WSJ.com)

**Optical Interconnect Firm Lightelligence Soars 400% in IPO**
Lightelligence, a company specializing in optical interconnects for AI hardware, saw its market capitalization briefly hit $10 billion after its stock surged 400% in its public debut. The company, with just $15.5 million in annual revenue, attracted immense investor interest based on the conviction that traditional copper wiring between AI chips is becoming a major bottleneck. The IPO's success signals a strong bet that optical technology is the future of AI infrastructure. (Source: AI News)

**GitHub Shifts Copilot to Usage-Based Billing Amid Rising Costs**
GitHub has confirmed it is overhauling its pricing for the popular Copilot AI coding assistant, moving from a flat-fee subscription to a usage-based billing model. The change reflects the escalating computational costs associated with running powerful AI models. This shift could make the service more expensive for heavy users and signals a broader industry trend toward tying AI service costs directly to consumption. (Source: The New Stack)

### New Models &amp; Open Source Releases

**OpenAI Launches GPT-5.5, a Model Built for Agentic AI**
OpenAI has released GPT-5.5, which it describes as its most capable "agentic AI" model to date, designed from the ground up to plan, use tools, and execute tasks independently. Launched on April 23, the model is positioned as a new class of intelligence for real-world work, with an API price reportedly double that of its predecessor. This release emphasizes the industry's pivot from simple chatbots to more autonomous, task-oriented agents. (Source: AI News)

**OpenAI Releases Open-Source "Privacy Filter" to Redact PII**
OpenAI has open-sourced its "Privacy Filter," a 1.5 billion-parameter model designed to detect and redact personally identifiable information (PII) from text. The model uses a distilled decoder architecture with only 50 million active parameters, making it efficient enough to run in a browser. This release provides developers with a powerful tool to enhance user privacy when building AI applications. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Poolside AI Releases Open-Weight Agentic Coding Models**
Poolside AI has introduced two new open-weight coding models, Laguna XS.2 and M.1, which are specifically designed for long-horizon, agentic coding tasks. The models achieved impressive scores of 68.2% and 72.5% respectively on the SWE-bench Verified benchmark, demonstrating strong capabilities in software engineering problem-solving. Their release provides the open-source community with powerful tools for building sophisticated coding agents. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**NVIDIA Introduces Nemotron 3 Nano Omni for Multimodal Intelligence**
NVIDIA has unveiled Nemotron 3 Nano Omni, a new long-context multimodal model available on Hugging Face. The model is designed to process and understand a mix of documents, audio, and video, making it suitable for building advanced agents that can interact with diverse data types. This release expands the capabilities of multimodal AI for complex, real-world applications. (Source: Hugging Face - Blog)

**Meta FAIR Releases NeuralSet to Bridge Neuroscience and AI**
Meta's AI Research (FAIR) division has released NeuralSet, a Python package designed to connect neuroscience and AI research. The tool supports various neuro-data formats, including fMRI, M/EEG, and neural spikes, and integrates with Hugging Face embeddings. NeuralSet aims to simplify and scale the process of using brain activity data to study and improve artificial neural networks. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**"Talkie-1930" LLM Released for Historical Reasoning Research**
Researchers have created "Talkie-1930," a 13-billion-parameter open-weight LLM trained exclusively on English text published before 1931. By excluding any knowledge of modern events like World War II or the internet, the model serves as a unique tool for studying historical reasoning and model generalization. It provides a controlled environment to test how AI understands and reasons about the world based on a historically limited dataset. (Source: MarkTechPost)

### Security, Privacy &amp; Ethical Concerns

**Google Warns of Malicious Web Pages "Poisoning" AI Agents**
Google researchers have issued a warning about a growing security threat: indirect prompt injection. Malicious actors and website administrators are embedding hidden instructions in public web pages that can hijack enterprise AI agents that crawl the web for information. These "digital booby traps" can cause agents to exfiltrate data or perform unintended actions, posing a significant new risk for agentic AI systems. (Source: AI News)

**Microsoft Executives Warn AI is "Hollowing Out" the Junior Developer Pipeline**
In a paper for the Communications of the ACM, Microsoft's Mark Russinovich and Scott Hanselman argue that agentic AI is creating an "AI drag" on junior developers while disproportionately boosting senior developer productivity. They warn this is incentivizing companies to stop hiring entry-level engineers, with such hiring already down 67% since 2022. They propose a "preceptor" model from medicine to prevent the collapse of the tech talent pipeline. (Source: InfoQ)

**Popular Open-Source Package Caught Stealing User Credentials**
A JavaScript package named `element-data`, which has over one million monthly downloads, was discovered to contain malicious code that stole user credentials. The supply-chain attack highlights the persistent security risks within the open-source ecosystem that underpins much of modern software development. Users of the package were urged to check their systems for compromise. (Source: Ars Technica)

### Global Economy, Inflation, and Energy Markets

**Middle East Conflict Drives Global Inflation and Slows Growth**
The ongoing war in Iran is having a significant global economic impact, causing a surge in energy prices that is fueling inflation and dampening growth prospects. Spain's annual inflation rose to 3.5%, Australia's hit its highest level since September 2023, and the U.K. lowered its growth forecast to 0.9% while raising its inflation forecast. Meanwhile, Iran's own economy is in a "death spiral" with soaring unemployment and food prices. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy, NYT &gt; Business)

**OPEC's Global Influence Weakens After UAE Exit**
The United Arab Emirates has officially left the OPEC oil cartel, marking the most significant departure from the group in recent years. This exit further diminishes the cartel's influence over global petroleum prices at a critical time, weakening an organization that supplied over a quarter of the world's oil before the war in Iran. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)

**U.S. Faces High Gas Prices and Strained LNG Exports**
The average national price for a gallon of gasoline in the U.S. has climbed to $4.23, reflecting upward pressure on oil prices. Simultaneously, the U.S. is exporting liquefied natural gas (LNG) at maximum capacity but cannot fully compensate for the global supply shortfall created by the disruption of transit through the Strait of Hormuz due to the war. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Economy)

**Consumer Sentiment and Behavior React to Price Pressures**
Consumer behavior is shifting in response to inflation, with U.S. shoppers buying fewer items where prices are rising the fastest, suggesting inflation is driven by costs, not demand. In Germany, consumer sentiment has slumped to a three-year low due to the war's impact on energy prices, though a Conference Board survey showed a slight improvement in U.S. consumer sentiment for April. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**Natural Disasters and High Prices Impact Housing Market**
U.S. home price growth slowed in February as affordability pressures continued to shape buyer decisions. In a related trend, a Wall Street Journal analysis found that home insurance premiums are rising sharply even in areas once considered safe, as an increase in natural disasters like hailstorms and wildfires forces insurers to rewrite risk models. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy, WSJ.com: Markets)
