## AI News Digest

### Major Model and Product Releases

**Google Unveils Gemma 4, a Frontier Multimodal Model for On-Device AI**
Google has released Gemma 4, its latest open, frontier-class model designed for on-device multimodal intelligence. This release aims to challenge the "token tax" of cloud-based AI by enabling powerful, personalized, and always-on AI assistants to run locally on hardware ranging from NVIDIA RTX desktops to DGX Spark, reducing reliance on costly API calls for every action. (Source: Hugging Face - Blog, MarkTechPost)

**Google Launches 'Vids' for AI-Powered Video Creation**
Google has introduced Google Vids, a new AI-powered video creation app for Workspace that allows users to generate and edit videos using prompts. The tool can create storyboards, select stock footage, and add voiceovers, with new features allowing users to direct on-screen avatars through text-based instructions, aiming to simplify video production for business communications. (Source: AI, AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Netflix Open-Sources 'VOID' for Physics-Aware Object Removal in Video**
The Netflix AI team has open-sourced VOID, an AI model designed to erase objects from video footage while realistically accounting for the physical consequences. Unlike simple object removal, VOID can simulate how the scene should look without the object, addressing complex issues like gravity and object interactions, which traditionally require weeks of manual VFX work. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Microsoft Releases Three New Foundational Models for Audio and Images**
Microsoft's new AI division, MAI, has released three new foundational models just six months after its formation. The models are specialized for transcribing speech to text, generating audio, and generating images, marking Microsoft's continued effort to build its own powerful, in-house AI capabilities to compete with rivals like Google and OpenAI. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**IBM Releases Granite 4.0 3B Vision for Enterprise Document Extraction**
IBM has launched Granite 4.0 3B Vision, a 3-billion-parameter vision-language model specifically engineered for high-fidelity data extraction from enterprise documents. Rather than a monolithic multimodal model, it functions as a specialized adapter for the Granite 4.0 language backbone, designed to improve visual reasoning for complex business workflows. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Z.ai Launches Multimodal Vision and Language Models**
Zhipu AI (Z.ai) has released GLM-5V-Turbo, a native multimodal vision-coding model optimized for agentic engineering workflows, and GLM-5, a language model focused on production-ready agentic systems. These models emphasize advanced features like tool calling, streaming, and multi-turn workflows, positioning them for complex, real-world applications. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**TII and Arcee AI Release New Open-Source Models for Vision and Reasoning**
The Technology Innovation Institute (TII) released Falcon Perception, a 0.6B-parameter model for open-vocabulary segmentation from language prompts, challenging traditional modular vision architectures. Concurrently, Arcee AI released Trinity Large Thinking, an Apache 2.0 open-weight reasoning model designed for long-horizon agents and complex tool use, providing a transparent alternative to proprietary systems. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Anthropic Scrambles After Source Code Leak and Botched Takedown**

(Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, The New Stack, Feed: Artificial Intelligence Latest)

**Major Security Flaw Discovered in Popular AI Agent Tool OpenClaw**
A critical vulnerability was found in the viral agentic AI tool OpenClaw, which allowed attackers to gain unauthenticated administrative access to systems running the software. The flaw has raised significant security concerns, prompting experts to advise users to assume their systems may have been compromised due to the ease of exploitation. (Source: Biz &amp; IT - Ars Technica)

Meta has paused its work with AI data vendor Mercor following a security incident that may have exposed sensitive data about how major AI labs train their models. **New 'Rowhammer' Attacks Grant Full Control Over Machines with NVIDIA GPUs**
Security researchers have developed new variations of the "Rowhammer" attack, named GDDRHammer, GeForge, and GPUBreach, that target GPU memory. These attacks can flip bits in the GPU's memory to gain complete control over the machine's CPU, demonstrating a novel and serious threat vector for systems equipped with NVIDIA GPUs. (Source: Biz &amp; IT - Ars Technica)

**KiloClaw Launches to Combat 'Shadow AI' in the Enterprise**
A new tool called KiloClaw has been launched to help enterprises enforce governance over the growing use of unauthorized autonomous agents, a phenomenon known as "shadow AI." ### Global Markets &amp; Geopolitics

**Iran Conflict Roils Global Markets, Boosting Oil and Inflation Fears**
The ongoing war in Iran is having a significant impact on global markets, causing oil prices to rise and fueling inflation concerns among investors and retirees. President Trump's signals of further military strikes renewed fears of supply disruptions in the Middle East, leading to a surge in the U.S. oil benchmark and a fall in Asian equities. This volatility has caused investors to waver, pausing a recent stock market rally that was driven by a "fear of missing out" on war-related gains. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, MarketWatch.com - Top Stories)

**War-Related Energy Costs Impact Consumers and Airlines**
Surging fuel prices, exacerbated by the conflict in Iran, are affecting consumers globally and straining U.S. airlines. In the Philippines, high gasoline costs have forced some Catholics to scale back or cancel traditional Holy Week travel. In the U.S., carriers like United and JetBlue have responded to soaring fuel costs by raising checked bag fees by up to $10 on domestic flights, with more airlines expected to follow. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)

**U.S. Economic Dominance Strengthened by Role as Energy Exporter**
The conflict in Iran has inadvertently highlighted and strengthened America's economic dominance, largely due to its position as a major oil and gas exporter. This status gives the U.S. leverage over other nations dependent on Middle Eastern energy and tempts the Trump administration to be more aggressive in its foreign policy. Meanwhile, rival nations like China and Iran are reportedly searching for economic choke points to counter U.S. pressure. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy, NYT &gt; Business)

**Gulf Sovereign Wealth Funds Face Uncertainty**
The trillions of dollars held by Gulf sovereign wealth funds, previously a reliable source of financing for Wall Street deals, now face an uncertain landscape due to the regional conflict with Iran. These funds, built on oil wealth, are a major force in global finance, and any disruption to their investment strategy could have widespread implications. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

### U.S. Economy &amp; Labor Market

**Strong March Jobs Report Surpasses Expectations**
The U.S. labor market demonstrated surprising resilience, adding 178,000 new jobs in March, a figure that exceeded economists' forecasts. The robust growth, coupled with a drop in unemployment, suggests the economy remains healthy, giving the Federal Reserve more latitude to continue its fight against inflation without needing to stimulate a faltering job market. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Markets)

**Healthcare Sector Drives Job Growth**
A significant portion of the March job gains was concentrated in a few key sectors, with healthcare being the primary driver. The healthcare industry, including nursing, has been one of the most consistent sources of job growth since the 1980s. The strong report was also aided by better weather and the conclusion of a healthcare strike. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**Labor Market Reaches a Tense Equilibrium**
Despite strong headline numbers, the U.S. labor market is described as being in a "low-hire, low-fire" state, leaving many job-seekers in a bind. This balance between labor supply and demand, influenced by lower immigration, has created a slow-moving market that some economists worry is fragile. Amid this environment, job security is becoming a higher priority for workers than monetary compensation. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, MarketWatch.com - Top Stories)

**Economic Indicators Show Mixed Signals**
While the jobs report was strong, other data points to potential weakness, with the S&amp;P Global Services PMI showing its first contraction in over three years. The index fell to 49.8 in March from 51.7 in February, as rising energy prices linked to the Iran war eroded business confidence. This mixed data complicates the economic outlook, tempering the optimism from the labor market. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)
