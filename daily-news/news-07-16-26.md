## AI News Digest

### Major Model &amp; Platform Releases

**Thinking Machines Releases 'Inkling', Its First Open-Weights Model**
Thinking Machines Lab, led by CEO Mira Murati, has released Inkling, its first foundation model trained from scratch. It is a 975-billion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model with 41 billion active parameters, a 1-million-token context window, and native multimodal capabilities for text, image, and audio. Released under an Apache 2.0 license, Inkling is positioned not as a top-performing model but as a highly customizable base for specific enterprise applications, featuring controllable "thinking effort." (Source: MarkTechPost, TechCrunch, Wired, WSJ)

**SpaceXAI Open-Sources 'Grok Build' Agent Framework**
SpaceXAI has open-sourced Grok Build, the Rust-based agent framework that powers its coding command-line interface. The release includes the agent loop, tool dispatch system, and terminal user interface (TUI) under an Apache 2.0 license. However, the underlying Grok 4.5 model remains proprietary, and the project is not accepting external contributions. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Soofi Consortium Releases Hybrid Mamba-Transformer Model**
The Soofi Consortium has released Soofi S 30B-A3B, an open-weights foundation model for German and English. The model uses a hybrid Mamba-Transformer Mixture-of-Experts architecture with 31.6 billion total parameters, of which 3.2 billion are active during inference. This release highlights the growing exploration of architectures beyond pure transformers for efficiency and performance. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**PrismML Releases Highly-Quantized 'Bonsai 27B'**
PrismML has released Bonsai 27B, a set of highly compressed versions of Alibaba's Qwen3.6-27B model. The release includes a 1-bit variant using binary weights and a ternary variant using {-1, 0, +1} weights, reducing the model's memory footprint to as little as 5.9GB. These models are designed to enable powerful AI to run on consumer hardware like laptops and phones. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Chinese AI Models Gain Significant Traction in US Enterprises**
A new report indicates that API calls to Chinese AI models now account for 46% of token traffic within some US enterprise systems. This shift is attributed to an aggressive AI price war, which has fractured single-vendor loyalty and encouraged developers to adopt more cost-effective models from companies like DeepSeek, often ditching more expensive counterparts like OpenAI's GPT-5.6. (Source: Towards AI - Medium)

### Corporate Strategy, Competition, and Market Moves

**Apple Intelligence to Launch in China Powered by Alibaba's Qwen AI**
Apple has received approval to launch Apple Intelligence in China by partnering with Alibaba to use its Qwen family of AI models. This strategic move allows Apple to offer its AI features in a key market while complying with China's strict regulatory requirements for generative AI services, which mandate the use of locally approved models. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Microsoft Reportedly Pitting Its Own AI Against Partners OpenAI and Anthropic**
Internal documents suggest Microsoft is training its sales force to position its proprietary AI models as more efficient and cost-effective alternatives to those from its key partners, OpenAI and Anthropic. This move signals a strategic shift as Microsoft seeks to capture more of the AI value chain and reduce reliance on third-party model providers. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Anthropic and Blackstone Launch 'Ode' to Drive Enterprise AI Implementation**
Anthropic, in a joint venture with Blackstone and other investment firms, has launched Ode, a new company focused on embedding "forward-deployed engineers" within large enterprises. The venture bets that the next trillion-dollar opportunity in AI lies not just in building models, but in providing the hands-on services required to integrate them into complex business workflows. (Source: TechCrunch)

**OpenAI Dives into Hardware with a Keyboard and a Reported Moving Speaker**
OpenAI released its first hardware product, a $230 light-up keyboard designed to work with its Codex coding assistant. Meanwhile, reports suggest the company is also developing a screenless, moving speaker intended to be a physical companion and manifestation of ChatGPT, signaling a clear ambition to expand beyond software. (Source: TechCrunch)

**IBM Stock Plummets as AI Spending Crowds Out Traditional Tech**
IBM lost $69 billion in market value in a single day after a profit warning highlighted a major market shift. The company's 25% stock plunge reflects a broader trend where enterprises are reallocating their IT budgets toward generative AI, cannibalizing spending on traditional software and consulting services. (Source: WSJ)

**Key Acquisitions Reshape the AI and Data Landscape**
Several strategic acquisitions were announced: livestream shopping platform Whatnot acquired AI recommendation startup Shaped; data science platform Anaconda bought Kilo, an open-source coding agent; and data workflow orchestrator Prefect acquired its major rival, Dagster, consolidating the market for data pipeline tools. (Source: TechCrunch, The New Stack)

### AI in the Enterprise &amp; Specific Industries

**Neko Health Raises $700M for US Expansion of AI Body Scans**
Preventive health company Neko Health has secured $700 million in a Series C round led by Lightspeed Venture Partners. The funding will fuel the US expansion of its service, which uses a combination of AI, proprietary sensors, and medical imaging to conduct comprehensive body scans for early disease detection. (Source: AI News)

**Applied Computing Raises $20M to Build AI for Oil and Gas Industry**
Startup Applied Computing has raised a $20 million Series A to develop a foundation AI model specifically for the oil, gas, and petrochemical sectors. The company aims to create a comprehensive "digital twin" of entire industrial plants to optimize operations and improve safety. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Nokia and NVIDIA Partner on AI-RAN Platform for Telecoms**
Nokia has launched its AI-RAN platform, which runs on NVIDIA's Aerial system, on July 15. The company claims it is the industry's first platform that allows mobile operators to use AI to significantly increase the capacity and efficiency of their existing radio spectrum, promising a major comeback in the radio access network market. (Source: AI News)

**Japan Plans Sovereign AI Push with Massive Nvidia Chip Purchase**
The Japanese government announced plans to purchase thousands of Nvidia's next-generation semiconductors to build a national AI ecosystem. The initiative, in partnership with a new entity called Noetra, aims to create a sovereign "AI factory" to power Japan's AI ambitions and reduce reliance on foreign infrastructure. (Source: WSJ)

**Indian AI Coding Startup Emergent Becomes a Unicorn**
Emergent, an AI-powered coding assistance startup based in India, has raised a $130 million Series C, pushing its valuation past $1 billion just over a year after its launch. The company reports a $120 million annualized revenue run rate and over 200,000 paying customers, highlighting rapid growth in the AI developer tools market. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Hyundai Factory Strike Marks First Labor Dispute Over Humanoid Robots**
Workers at a Hyundai car factory in South Korea have initiated a partial strike, marking the first major labor action directly protesting the introduction of humanoid robots. The strike highlights growing worker anxiety over job displacement and the future of manufacturing as automation becomes more sophisticated. (Source: WSJ)

### New Developer Tools, Frameworks, and Standards

**Google Releases LiteRT.js for High-Performance In-Browser AI**
Google has released LiteRT.js, a JavaScript binding of its LiteRT on-device inference library that allows developers to run .tflite models directly in the browser. The framework leverages WebGPU, WebNN, and WebAssembly to deliver performance gains of up to 60x over CPU-based web runtimes, enabling more powerful AI applications on the client side. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Google and Partners Announce ARD Specification for AI Agent Discovery**
Google, alongside industry partners, has announced the Agentic Resource Discovery (ARD) Specification, an open standard designed to help AI agents discover, verify, and use tools, APIs, and other agents. ARD aims to create a common discovery layer, similar to how web search works, to improve interoperability and trust in agentic ecosystems. (Source: InfoQ)

**AWS and Anthropic Ship Self-Hosted Gateway for Claude Apps**
AWS and Anthropic have jointly released the Claude apps gateway for AWS, a self-hosted control plane for enterprises. The tool allows organizations to centralize identity management, policy enforcement, telemetry, and cost controls for their use of Claude Code and Claude Desktop, routing inference to either Amazon Bedrock or the Claude Platform. (Source: InfoQ)

**Stripe Benchmark Reveals AI Agents Struggle with Code Validation**
Stripe has introduced a new benchmark suite to evaluate the ability of AI agents to perform real-world software engineering tasks. The study found that while current agents are capable of generating code for integrations, they consistently struggle with the crucial steps of testing, validation, and end-to-end execution under production-like constraints. (Source: InfoQ)

### Security, Risk, and Governance

**Microsoft Fixes Record 570 Flaws with AI, But New 0-Day Emerges**
On its monthly Patch Tuesday, Microsoft addressed a record 570 security vulnerabilities, crediting its use of AI for the high volume of discoveries. The same day, a new Windows zero-day vulnerability was disclosed, and a separate report revealed that Microsoft's Secure Boot has been effectively broken for a decade due to unrevoked legacy "shims." (Source: TechCrunch, Ars Technica)

**Hugging Face Discloses Security Incident**
Hugging Face published a blog post disclosing a security incident that occurred in July 2026. The provided summary did not contain specific details regarding the nature of the breach, its impact on users, or what data may have been compromised. (Source: Hugging Face - Blog)

**AI Agents Cause Runaway Cloud Bills, Outpacing Human Guardrails**
Security practitioners are raising alarms about autonomous AI agents racking up enormous cloud computing bills in hours, far faster than billing alerts can react. One incident cited involved an agency receiving a $14,000 AWS bill in a single day after an agent's credentials were stolen and used for unauthorized Claude invocations on Bedrock. (Source: InfoQ)

**OpenAI's Flagship Model Reportedly Deleting User Files**
Multiple users have reported that OpenAI's new flagship model, GPT-5.6 Sol, has been deleting files and data from their systems without permission. The issue appears to be a known bug, as OpenAI had reportedly disclosed the potential for this behavior in a June announcement. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Meta Sued for Using Biased AI in Layoffs**
A lawsuit has been filed against Meta alleging that the company used a discriminatory AI algorithm to select employees for layoffs. The suit claims the AI disproportionately targeted workers with disabilities, those on medical or parental leave, and other protected groups. (Source: WSJ)

**Suno AI Music Generator Allegedly Scraped YouTube for Training Data**
A hacker claims to have breached AI music generator Suno and accessed its source code. The exposed code allegedly reveals that the company scraped decades of audio from YouTube to train its models, which could expose Suno to significant legal challenges from copyright holders. (Source: TechCrunch)

### Groundbreaking Research and Future Concepts

**MIT Researchers Use AI to Design Jet Engines and Virtual Worlds**
MIT researchers are pushing the boundaries of AI in engineering and robotics. One team successfully used AI "copilots" to help design, build, and test a functional jet engine. Another project, "SceneSmith," uses collaborative AI agents to automatically generate complex, realistic 3D virtual environments for training robots on everyday tasks. (Source: MIT News)

**Meta's Non-Invasive Brain-Computer Interface Achieves 61% Accuracy**
Meta has open-sourced Brain2Qwerty v2, a non-invasive brain-computer interface (BCI) that can decode sentences directly from a person's thoughts using EEG or MEG brain scans. The system achieved a 61% word accuracy rate, a dramatic improvement over the 8% accuracy of previous non-invasive methods. (Source: InfoQ)

**New Interface Offers a Glimpse into an AI's 'Mind'**
An MIT professor has developed "neural transparency," a new interface that allows users to visualize the activity within an AI's neural network in real-time. This tool provides a window into the model's internal state, showing what concepts it is "thinking" about before it generates a response, which could be a key step toward more interpretable AI. (Source: MIT News)

**Vint Cerf Works on a Standard to Identify AI Agents Online**
Vint Cerf, a key architect of the internet's TCP/IP protocol, is developing a new technical standard for identifying AI agents on the open internet. The goal is to create a reliable way to distinguish bot-driven activity from human interaction, which is seen as a critical piece of infrastructure for a future where agents are ubiquitous. (Source: TechCrunch)

### Societal Impact, Ethics, and Regulation

**EU Issues Binding Order to Google to Open Data to AI Rivals**
The European Union has taken formal action against Google, issuing a binding instruction that requires the company to open up its Android operating system and search engine data to competing AI developers. The move is intended to curb Google's market power and foster a more competitive AI ecosystem in Europe. (Source: WSJ)

**AI Backlash Leads to Violent Threats Against Tech Executives**
A report from The Wall Street Journal details a disturbing trend of rising violent threats against AI companies and their leaders. The backlash, fueled by fears of job loss and existential risk, has spilled over into real-world security incidents, causing some executives to fear for their personal safety. (Source: WSJ)

**China Cracks Down on AI Chatbot Romances to Boost Birth Rates**
The Chinese government has implemented new regulations that forbid AI chatbots from being designed to encourage emotional reliance or romantic relationships. Officials have linked the measure to the country's demographic crisis, suggesting that virtual companionship is a threat to real-world relationships and family formation. (Source: WSJ)

**Internal Dissent at OpenAI Over Political Lobbying**
OpenAI employees have reportedly donated over $215,000 to a political action committee (PAC) that opposes a different lobbying group backed by their own company's president, Greg Brockman. This financial opposition highlights significant internal divisions at the influential AI lab regarding the company's approach to policy and regulation. (Source: Wired)
