## AI News Digest

### The New Frontier of Large Models

**Alibaba Previews 2.4 Trillion-Parameter Qwen3.8-Max Model**
Alibaba has previewed Qwen3.8-Max, a massive 2.4 trillion-parameter multimodal Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model. The company claims it is "second only to Fable 5," highlighting China's accelerating efforts to compete with top US AI labs. While a preview is available on platforms like Token Plan, Alibaba has not yet released benchmarks, licensing details, or the active-parameter count. (Source: MarkTechPost, Technology - WSJ.com)

**Moonshot AI Releases 2.8 Trillion-Parameter Kimi K3 Open-Weight Model**
Chinese startup Moonshot AI has released Kimi K3, a 2.8 trillion-parameter open-weight model, making it the largest of its kind to date. The release has intensified the US-China tech race and raised concerns about the implications of such powerful, openly available models. Kimi K3 is seen as a strategic bet on model memory and capability over raw compute efficiency. (Source: AI News, AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, Technology - WSJ.com)

**China's Trillion-Scale Open Models Spark Direct Comparisons**
The recent releases of Moonshot's Kimi K3, DeepSeek's V4 Pro, and Zhipu AI's GLM-5.2 have sparked direct comparisons of China's top open-weight MoE models. Analysis is focusing on their benchmark performance, differing open-source licenses, and the practical costs of serving these massive models. This highlights a new phase of competition centered on large, openly accessible foundation models from Chinese firms. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Feyn AI Releases SQRL for Advanced Text-to-SQL**
Feyn Labs has launched SQRL, a family of text-to-SQL models designed to improve query accuracy by first inspecting a database's structure with read-only probes. The flagship 35B model reportedly outperforms Claude Opus 4.6 on the BIRD benchmark with 70.6% execution accuracy. The release includes smaller, self-hostable 4B and 9B versions, making advanced database interaction more accessible. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Community Fine-Tunes Compact "Thinking" Model from Claude Traces**
A developer has fine-tuned OpenBMB's MiniCPM5-1B model using traces from Anthropic's Claude Fable 5, creating a compact model with visible reasoning capabilities. The resulting model is just 657MB, runs locally, and features a 128K context window. This demonstrates the power of the open-source community to adapt and enhance smaller models with capabilities distilled from larger, proprietary ones, though it raises licensing questions. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Murati's Startup Unveils Self-Improving Inkling Model**
A new startup, reportedly led by former OpenAI CTO Mira Murati, demonstrated a 975-billion-parameter model named Inkling. During its launch demo, the model fine-tuned itself on new information in real-time, showcasing a significant step towards models that can continuously learn and adapt. This live self-improvement capability allowed it to quickly achieve a top spot on a US open-weight leaderboard. (Source: Towards AI - Medium)

### Corporate Maneuvers and Legal Showdowns

**Apple Sues OpenAI for Trade Secret Theft**
Apple has filed a major lawsuit against OpenAI, alleging a "pattern of misconduct" and the theft of trade secrets. The suit claims that over 400 former Apple employees now work at OpenAI, with two named as defendants, and that they brought confidential information with them. This legal battle could significantly disrupt OpenAI's reported IPO plans and its ambitions in the hardware space. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, Towards AI - Medium)

**Databricks Valuation Soars to $188 Billion**
Databricks has successfully rebranded itself as a key player in the AI space, achieving a new valuation of $188 billion. The company has extended its focus from data analytics to a full-fledged AI platform, capitalizing on the demand for enterprise AI solutions. This valuation cements its status as a major beneficiary of the current AI boom. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Patreon Actively Blocks AI Data Scrapers**
Shifting its strategy from passive requests to active enforcement, Patreon is now using Cloudflare to block bots that scrape creator content for AI model training. This move addresses creator concerns about unauthorized use of their work and marks a more aggressive stance against data scraping than relying on the voluntary `robots.txt` protocol. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**IBM Struggles to Find its Footing in the AI Era**
Despite its long history in AI, IBM is facing significant challenges in the current generative AI boom, with CEO Arvind Krishna under pressure. The company's position in the new tech cycle is seen as uncertain as it competes against more agile startups and tech giants who have captured the market's attention. This raises questions about the ability of legacy tech firms to adapt to the rapid pace of AI innovation. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

## FINANCEANDTECHNOLOGY News Digest

### AI &amp; Technology Investing

**Retail Investors Shift AI Focus Beyond Magnificent Seven**
Everyday investors, who were once major fans of the "Magnificent Seven" tech stocks, are now shifting their capital towards newer and smaller companies perceived as the next big AI winners. This trend indicates a broadening of the AI investment landscape and a search for higher growth potential outside of the established tech giants. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**AI Supply Deals Face Scrutiny Amid Market Frenzy**
While chip stocks are climbing on AI enthusiasm, the massive, long-term supply agreements fueling the boom are not as secure as they seem. Companies like SK Hynix have touted major deals, but these arrangements are often non-binding, posing a risk to the long-term stability of the AI hardware supply chain and the valuations built upon it. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**Prysmian Inks $6.29 Billion Data Center Cable Deal with Molex**
Italian cable maker Prysmian secured a 10-year, $6.29 billion deal to supply data-center cables to Koch-owned Molex, highlighting the massive infrastructure investment required to support the AI boom. This agreement is part of Prysmian's strategy to increase its fiber capacity in the U.S., with a potential U.S. stock listing being considered. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

### Mergers, Acquisitions, and Corporate Deals

**Brookfield and CPP to Acquire LXP Industrial for $5.2 Billion**
Brookfield Asset Management and Canada Pension Plan Investment Board have agreed to an all-cash deal to purchase LXP Industrial Trust for approximately $5.2 billion. The acquisition adds a significant portfolio of modern warehouse and logistics facilities to their real estate holdings, signaling continued high investor demand for industrial properties. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**UK's Segro Rejects $18.2 Billion Takeover Bid from Prologis**
U.K. warehouse operator Segro has turned down a third, improved offer from its U.S. competitor Prologis, valued at $18.2 billion. While the bid was rejected, Segro's board indicated it remains open to further improved offers, underscoring the intense competition and high valuations within the global logistics real estate sector. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

### Global Markets and Geopolitical Tensions

**Middle East Conflict Roils Global Markets, Boosting Oil and Yields**
Escalating hostilities in the Middle East, particularly between the U.S. and Iran, are causing significant market volatility, with oil prices oscillating near $90 a barrel. The conflict has pushed U.S. Treasury yields higher as inflation concerns grow, and has caused European airline stocks to drop due to the prospect of higher fuel costs. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**China Deploys State Funds to Stabilize Stock Market**
In an effort to support its faltering stock market, Chinese state-owned funds have purchased nearly $9 billion worth of shares. This significant intervention aims to restore investor confidence amid a deepening global selloff in technology and semiconductor stocks. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**Markets Brace for Key Economic Data Amid Geopolitical Uncertainty**
Investors are closely watching for upcoming economic data, including provisional Purchasing Managers' Index (PMI) surveys from the U.S. and Europe, as well as a key interest rate decision from the European Central Bank (ECB). These indicators will provide a snapshot of economic health just as renewed military conflict in the Middle East adds a layer of uncertainty and inflationary pressure. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

### Economic Indicators and Consumer Trends

**US Inflation Metrics Undergo Changes as Import Prices Rise**
The Bureau of Economic Analysis is updating its methodology for measuring several components of the Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index, a key inflation gauge monitored by the Federal Reserve. The changes come as U.S. import prices unexpectedly rose 0.3% in June, adding to concerns about persistent inflationary pressures in the economy. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**Consumer Sentiment Improves, But Spending Habits Show Strain**
The University of Michigan's preliminary survey for July showed that U.S. consumer sentiment has climbed, largely due to cooling gasoline prices. However, other data reveals financial strain, with the average cost of a "date night" reaching $189, forcing many younger couples to opt for more frugal alternatives like coffee dates. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**World Cup Boosts Local Economies While Big Food Struggles**
The World Cup has provided a surprise economic stimulus for host cities across the U.S., with soccer fans boosting spending on tourism and hospitality. In contrast, major food conglomerates like General Mills and Kraft Heinz are facing significant headwinds from investors and shoppers, squeezed by inflation, the rise of GLP-1 weight-loss drugs, and shifting consumer preferences. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, WSJ.com: Economy)

### Corporate Governance and Investment Strategy

**Justice Department Eases Prosecution of Corporate Crime**
A new analysis suggests the U.S. Justice Department is pulling back on prosecuting corporations, having declined to press charges in a number of recent cases despite evidence of wrongdoing by senior employees. This trend indicates a potential shift in enforcement priorities and could reduce a significant deterrent for corporate misconduct. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**Debate Grows Over Passive Investing's Impact on Fund Managers**
The continued rise of passive investing strategies is raising questions about its effect on the performance of active fund managers. The massive flows into index funds and ETFs may be altering market dynamics, making it more challenging for stock-pickers to outperform the broader market. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**Generosity Varies Widely in Corporate 401(k) Retirement Plans**
Not all 401(k) plans are equal, with some U.S. employers offering far more generous retirement benefits than others. Companies like Costco are highlighted for providing superior plans with better matching contributions and lower fees, underscoring the importance for employees to scrutinize their retirement options. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

### Science and Technology Frontiers

**New Theory Proposes Mitochondria as the Foundation of the Mind**
Biologist Martin Picard has put forth a "mitochondrial theory of mind," a bold new perspective suggesting that mitochondria—the energy-producing organelles in our cells—are the crucial link between cellular activity, physical health, and consciousness. This "energetic view of life" posits that these organelles are fundamental to our experience of being alive. (Source: Quanta Magazine)
