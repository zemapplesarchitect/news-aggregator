## AI News Digest

### Major AI Labs &amp; Corporate Strategy

**OpenAI Unveils Custom "Jalapeño" Chip and Open Source Security Initiative**
OpenAI announced its first custom-designed chip, an ASIC named "Jalapeño," co-developed with Broadcom to optimize AI inference and reduce operational costs. This move signals a strategy to build a full vertical stack and lessen dependence on third-party hardware. Concurrently, OpenAI launched "Patch the Planet," an initiative using an improved GPT-5.5-Cyber model to find and fix vulnerabilities in open-source software. (Source: TechCrunch, AI News, WSJ.com, Wired)

**Anthropic Integrates "Claude Tag" into Slack and Faces Geopolitical Scrutiny**
Anthropic launched a beta of "Claude Tag" for its Enterprise and Team tiers, allowing users to pull its AI model directly into shared Slack channels by typing `@Claude`. The feature aims to capture institutional knowledge and streamline workflows. Separately, Anthropic accused Chinese tech giant Alibaba of a "brazen" campaign to illicitly access and harvest the capabilities of its Claude model. (Source: AI News, TechCrunch, The New Stack, WSJ.com)

**Samsung Rolls Out ChatGPT Enterprise to Global Workforce**
Samsung Electronics is providing widespread access to ChatGPT Enterprise and Codex for its employees in Korea and its global Device eXperience (DX) division. The move comes after previous restrictions on AI tool usage and is intended to boost productivity and innovation in both technical and non-technical roles across the company. (Source: AI News)

**Google Loses More Top AI Talent as Researchers Depart for Anthropic**
The trend of AI talent migration from Google continues, with top researchers Jonas Adler and Alexander Pritzel leaving to join rival Anthropic. Their departure follows other high-profile exits, including Noam Shazeer and John Jumper, highlighting the intense competition for leading AI experts among major labs. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Meta Pauses Employee Keystroke Tracking Program After Internal Data Leak**
Meta has halted a controversial internal program that collected employee keystroke data to train AI models. The decision followed an incident where this potentially sensitive data was accidentally exposed internally, raising further concerns among employees about privacy and the nature of the data collection. (Source: Wired)

**Amazon Pledges an Additional $13B for AI Infrastructure in India**
Amazon is significantly increasing its investment in India with a new $13 billion commitment toward AI and cloud infrastructure. This move is part of a broader race among global tech giants to expand their AI capabilities and data center presence in the rapidly growing Indian market. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Oracle's AI Ambitions Fueled by Major Layoffs**
Oracle is reportedly laying off 21,000 employees as part of a strategic shift to fund massive, debt-fueled investments in AI data center infrastructure. The company is spending billions to build out its capacity to support the growing demand for AI workloads on its cloud platform. (Source: Ars Technica)

### AI Hardware, Chips, and Infrastructure

**China's "LineShine" System Becomes World's Fastest Supercomputer**
China has reclaimed the top spot in supercomputing, with its LineShine system being named the world's fastest in the latest Top500 ranking. The achievement overtakes the previous US leader and underscores the escalating global competition for high-performance computing dominance, which is critical for training large-scale AI models. (Source: WSJ.com)

**Data Center Boom Drives Inflation and Memory Chip Crunch**
The intense demand for AI infrastructure is creating a "third wave" of inflation, primarily driven by soaring prices for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) chips. One unnamed US memory chip company reported its revenue quadrupled to $41.45 billion and profit surged to $28.2 billion year-over-year, illustrating the massive financial impact of the AI hardware race. (Source: WSJ.com, TechCrunch)

**Qualcomm Acquires Chip Software Startup Modular for Nearly $4 Billion**
Qualcomm has acquired Modular, a promising AI chip software startup, in a deal valued at nearly $4 billion. The acquisition gives Qualcomm access to Modular's technology, which is designed to unify AI software development across different hardware, a critical component for competing in the diverse AI accelerator market. (Source: Wired)

**Cerebras Stock Plummets After Post-IPO Earnings Report**
In its first earnings report since going public, AI chipmaker Cerebras saw its stock price plunge after its gross margin outlook was misunderstood by investors. The company's CEO stated that the forecast for its core business was misinterpreted, leading to a sharp negative market reaction. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Europe Pushes Back on US-Led Chip War Against China**
European leaders and industry figures are showing resistance to Washington's efforts to further restrict semiconductor technology sales to China. ASML's CEO noted that proposed US legislation would block sales of older-generation DUV tools, a move that could harm European business interests without significantly halting China's technological progress. (Source: TechCrunch)

**MIT Researchers Design Power-Efficient Chip for Tiny Robots**
A new chip developed at MIT combines a novel algorithm with dedicated hardware to enable small, power-constrained robots to generate 3D maps for navigation. The system uses minimal memory and power, a key breakthrough for deploying autonomous robots in complex, real-world environments. (Source: MIT News - Artificial intelligence)

### New Models and Open Source Developments

**Baidu and Mistral AI Release Advanced OCR Models**
Baidu open-sourced "Unlimited OCR," a 3B parameter model that uses a novel "Reference Sliding Window Attention" to parse long documents with constant memory usage. Meanwhile, Mistral AI launched OCR 4, which moves beyond simple text extraction to provide structured, citation-ready JSON output with bounding boxes and confidence scores in 170 languages. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Datalab Releases "lift," a 9B Open-Weights Model for Structured Data Extraction**
Datalab has released "lift," a 9B parameter open-weights vision model designed to extract structured JSON from PDFs and images based on a provided schema. The model uses schema-constrained decoding to ensure valid output and is trained to abstain from hallucinating fields that are not present in the source document. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**DFlash Speculative Decoding Promises Major Speedups for LLMs**
Researchers from UC San Diego introduced DFlash, a new speculative decoding method that drafts entire blocks of tokens in parallel using a lightweight block diffusion model. The technique reports up to a 6x lossless speedup on some models and NVIDIA claims it could enable up to 15x higher throughput on its Blackwell architecture. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Gradium Launches Real-Time Speech Translation Models**
Gradium has released two new real-time speech translation models, `stt-translate` and `s2s-translate`, covering 20 language pairs. The company claims its two-model cascade architecture provides a better accuracy-latency tradeoff than competing real-time translation services from major AI labs. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Nous Research Enhances Hermes Agent with Automated Skill Creation**
Nous Research has added a `/learn` command to its Hermes Agent Skills System, enabling the agent to automatically create new, reusable skills from conversations, documents, or URLs. This feature streamlines the process of expanding an agent's capabilities by eliminating the need for developers to manually write skill definition files. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Google Releases OpenRL for Self-Hosted LLM Fine-Tuning**
Google's GKE Labs has introduced OpenRL, an open-source project that provides a self-hosted API for post-training and fine-tuning large language models. The tool is designed to run on standard Kubernetes clusters, giving developers more control over the model customization process. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

### AI Agents, Robotics, and Automation

**Humanoid Robotics Firm Agility Robotics to Go Public in $2.5B SPAC Deal**
Agility Robotics, the startup behind the bipedal robot Digit, is planning to go public through a merger with a special-purpose acquisition company (SPAC). The deal values the company at $2.5 billion and is expected to generate $620 million in proceeds to scale production and deployment. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Sakana AI Launches "Fugu" to Orchestrate Multi-Agent Systems**
Japanese AI firm Sakana AI has launched Fugu, an orchestration language model designed to manage operations across a diverse pool of specialized AI models. The approach aims to mitigate the risks of vendor lock-in by allowing enterprises to build robust, multi-agent systems that are not dependent on a single monolithic AI provider. (Source: AI News)

**Linux Foundation Proposes "Agent Name Service" for AI Identity**
The Linux Foundation announced its intent to launch the Agent Name Service (ANS), an open standard designed to give AI agents a verifiable, DNS-like identity. The initiative aims to create a foundational layer of trust and interoperability for the growing ecosystem of autonomous and agentic systems. (Source: The New Stack)

**Cloudflare and Grab Build Platforms for Secure Agent Deployment**
Cloudflare released an open-source library of agent skills to automate the deployment and management of Zero Trust security environments. In a similar vein, Southeast Asian tech company Grab built "Palana," a secure, Kubernetes-native platform to contain and run unpredictable autonomous AI agents safely within its infrastructure. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**Fika Jobs Raises $4M for AI-Powered Hiring Platform**
Stockholm-based startup Fika Jobs has raised $4 million to build a video-first hiring platform where AI agents conduct initial candidate interviews. The platform combines short-form video profiles with automated screening, aiming to streamline the recruitment process for companies. (Source: TechCrunch)

### AI &amp; The Tech Sector

**Micron's Strong Earnings Quell AI Fears and Boost Tech Stocks**
Memory-chip maker Micron Technology reported blockbuster earnings and a strong sales forecast, driven by surging demand for its high-bandwidth memory chips used in AI servers. The massive energy requirements of AI are creating a new competitive front for big tech, with Amazon and Google emerging as leaders in securing power for their data centers. Meanwhile, companies like Tesla and Sunrun are exploring using networks of home solar panels and batteries to help meet the growing energy demand, potentially turning residential homes into distributed power plants for the AI industry. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business)
