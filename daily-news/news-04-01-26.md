## AI News Digest

### Major Model &amp; Framework Releases

**OpenAI Closes Record-Breaking $122B Funding Round**
OpenAI has closed Silicon Valley's largest-ever funding round at $122 billion, raising $3 billion from retail investors alone. The round, led by Amazon, Nvidia, and SoftBank, values the AI lab at an astounding $852 billion, signaling immense investor confidence and paving the way for a potential IPO. (Source: TechCrunch, Technology - WSJ.com)

**Google Releases Veo 3.1 Lite for Cost-Effective Video Generation**
Google has launched Veo 3.1 Lite, a new tier in its generative video portfolio designed to make AI video creation more accessible. The model, available through the Gemini API, addresses the high cost per second of generated content, which has been a major barrier for developers looking to deploy video generation at scale. (Source: MarkTechPost, AI)

**Alibaba Launches Qwen3.5 Omni to Compete with Gemini**
The Alibaba Qwen team has released Qwen3.5-Omni, a native omnimodal model designed to directly compete with models like Gemini 3.1 Pro. Unlike "wrapper" models, Qwen3.5-Omni is built from the ground up to handle text, audio, video, and real-time interaction within a single, end-to-end architecture. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Microsoft Releases SOTA Multilingual Embedding Models**
Microsoft AI has released Harrier-OSS-v1, a new family of three multilingual text embedding models (270M, 0.6B, and 27B parameters). The models have achieved new state-of-the-art results on the Multilingual MTEB v2 benchmark, providing high-quality semantic representations across a wide range of languages. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Hugging Face Launches TRL v1.0 for Production-Ready Model Training**
Hugging Face has officially released TRL (Transformer Reinforcement Learning) v1.0, transitioning the library from a research tool to a stable, production-ready framework. It provides a unified API for the entire post-training pipeline, including Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Reward Modeling, and alignment techniques like DPO. (Source: Hugging Face - Blog, MarkTechPost)

**Liquid AI Challenges Scaling Laws with Compact LFM2.5-350M Model**
Liquid AI has released LFM2.5-350M, a compact 350-million-parameter model that demonstrates high intelligence density. By training on an extensive 28 trillion tokens and using large-scale reinforcement learning, the model challenges the convention that more parameters are required for greater intelligence. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**IBM Releases Granite 4.0 3B Vision for Enterprise Documents**
IBM has introduced Granite 4.0 3B Vision, a compact 3-billion-parameter multimodal model available on Hugging Face. The model is specifically designed for enterprise use cases involving document understanding, offering a balance of high performance and computational efficiency. (Source: Hugging Face - Blog)

**Caltech Researchers Announce Breakthrough in AI Model Compression**
A research team at the California Institute of Technology, led by Babak Hassibi, claims to have developed a method to radically compress large language models without compromising performance. This breakthrough could make high-fidelity AI models more accessible and efficient to run on a wider range of hardware. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

**TII Releases Falcon Perception for Multimodal Tasks**
The Technology Innovation Institute (TII) has expanded its Falcon family of models with the release of Falcon Perception. This new model is focused on vision and multimodal capabilities, marking the group's entry into advanced perception tasks beyond text generation. (Source: Hugging Face - Blog)

### AI Infrastructure, Tools, and Development

**Amazon Researchers Release A-Evolve to Automate Agent Development**
Researchers associated with Amazon have released A-Evolve, a framework designed to automate the development of autonomous AI agents. Described as a potential "PyTorch moment" for agentic AI, it aims to replace manual tuning with a systematic process of automated state mutation and self-correction. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Agent-Infra Launches AIO Sandbox for Secure Agent Execution**
Agent-Infra has released AIO Sandbox, an open-source, all-in-one runtime environment for AI agents. The project addresses a key bottleneck in agent development by providing an isolated and functional environment with a browser, shell, and shared filesystem for code execution. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Salesforce Unveils VoiceAgentRAG to Cut Voice AI Latency**
Salesforce AI Research has introduced VoiceAgentRAG, a dual-agent memory router that dramatically improves voice-based RAG systems. The architecture reportedly cuts retrieval latency by 316x, enabling voice agents to respond within the 200ms window required for natural, real-time conversation. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Ollama Taps Apple's MLX to Speed Up Local AI on Macs**
The Ollama platform has been updated to leverage Apple's MLX framework, significantly improving the performance of running large language models locally on Mac computers. This update makes local AI development faster and more memory-efficient for Apple users. (Source: The New Stack)

**Google Introduces AppFunctions to Connect AI Agents with Android Apps**
Google has unveiled AppFunctions, an early beta feature designed to transform Android into an "agent-first" operating system. This will allow AI agents and assistants to use individual app functionalities as building blocks to complete complex, multi-app tasks for the user. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**Qodo Raises $70M for AI-Generated Code Verification**
Startup Qodo has raised $70 million to build tools for code verification, betting that as AI coding assistants flood development with new code, the critical challenge will shift to ensuring that code is correct and functional. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Starcloud Raises $170M to Build Data Centers in Space**
Starcloud has raised a $170 million Series A, becoming the fastest Y Combinator startup to achieve unicorn status. The company plans to build data centers in space, a novel approach to addressing the massive and growing demand for AI compute infrastructure. (Source: TechCrunch)

### Security, Governance, and Societal Impact

**OpenAI Shuts Down Public Sora Video Tool, Sparking Speculation**
OpenAI has shut down its popular Sora AI video-generation tool just six months after its public release. The decision has fueled speculation about the high computational costs, potential data privacy issues, and a strategic shift away from public demos toward enterprise-focused products. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com, AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**LiteLLM Open-Source Project Compromised in Supply Chain Attack**
A malicious version of the popular AI gateway library LiteLLM was uploaded to the PyPI repository, leading to over 40,000 downloads. The compromised package contained malware designed to exfiltrate sensitive information and credentials, highlighting significant security risks in the AI software supply chain. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering, TechCrunch)

**AI Recruiting Startup Mercor Hit by Attack Tied to LiteLLM Compromise**
AI recruiting startup Mercor confirmed it was the victim of a cyberattack where data was stolen from its systems. The incident was directly tied to the company's use of the compromised open-source LiteLLM project, demonstrating the cascading impact of supply chain vulnerabilities. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Polls Show Rising AI Adoption but Declining Trust in the US**
A new Quinnipiac University poll reveals that while more Americans are adopting AI tools, their trust in the results is decreasing. The poll highlights widespread public concern about transparency, regulation, and the technology's broader societal impact, even as 15% of respondents said they would work for an AI boss. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**IRS Testing Palantir AI to Identify High-Value Audit Targets**
Newly revealed documents show the US Internal Revenue Service (IRS) is testing a Palantir AI tool to sift through its legacy systems. The goal is to surface the "highest-value" targets for audits and investigations, with a particular focus on potential fraud related to clean energy credits. (Source: Wired)

**Kelsey Hightower: "Everyone is a junior engineer when it comes to AI"**
At KubeCon 2026, influential technologist Kelsey Hightower remarked that in the field of AI, everyone is effectively a junior engineer. The statement underscores the novelty of the field, where even seasoned experts are still learning and best practices are yet to be firmly established. (Source: The New Stack)

**Oxford Review Finds Most LLM Benchmarks Are "Junk Science"**
A comprehensive review of 445 LLM benchmarks by Oxford researchers found that 84% lack basic statistical testing. The study argues that many benchmarks are "junk science," as models that achieve high scores often perform poorly on unseen problems, creating a misleading impression of their true capabilities. (Source: Towards AI - Medium)

### Scientific and Medical Research Applications

**Meta's TRIBE v2 AI Predicts Human Brain Activity**
Meta AI has developed TRIBE v2, a trimodal AI model that can predict human brain activity by analyzing over 1,100 hours of brain scans from 720 subjects. The model can simulate decades of controlled neuroscience experiments, potentially unifying disparate research fields and accelerating our understanding of the brain. (Source: Towards AI - Medium)

**MIT Researchers Use AI to Uncover Atomic Defects in Materials**
Researchers at MIT have developed a new AI model that can measure atomic-level defects in materials. By identifying these imperfections, scientists can better understand how to leverage them to improve material properties like mechanical strength, heat transfer, and energy efficiency. (Source: MIT News - Artificial intelligence)

**Eli Lilly and InSilico Medicine Sign Major AI Drug Discovery Deal**
Pharmaceutical giant Eli Lilly has struck a deal with AI drug discovery firm InSilico Medicine that could be valued at up to $2.75 billion. **Mantis Biotech Creates "Digital Twins" of Humans for Medical Research**
Startup Mantis Biotech is using AI to create synthetic datasets that function as "digital twins" of the human body, representing anatomy, physiology, and behavior. This approach aims to solve the critical problem of data availability and privacy in medical research and drug development. (Source: TechCrunch)

**MIT Develops VisiPrint to Preview 3D-Printed Objects**
MIT researchers have created VisiPrint, an AI-powered preview tool that quickly generates aesthetically accurate visualizations of 3D-printed objects. The system is designed to make the prototyping process faster and less wasteful by reducing the need for numerous physical test prints. (Source: MIT News - Artificial intelligence)

## FINANCEANDTECHNOLOGY News Digest

### Global Markets &amp; Geopolitical Impact

**Markets Rally on Hopes for Swift End to Middle East Conflict**
Global stock markets, including the Dow, S&amp;P 500, and Asian equities, surged on hopes for a quick conclusion to the war in Iran, following a statement from President Trump suggesting a U.S. withdrawal in two to three weeks. This optimism caused oil prices to slip from recent highs, though Brent crude remained near $100 a barrel and oil was recently at $115. Analysts caution that significant economic uncertainty and the risk of conflict escalation persist. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business)

**Surging Oil Prices and Gas at $4/Gallon Threaten Global Economy**
The war in the Middle East has pushed the average U.S. gasoline price above $4 a gallon, a multiyear high, creating a significant headwind for consumers and the Trump administration. The disruption of oil supplies from the Persian Gulf has led economists to price in a potential global slowdown or recession if the conflict drags on, with fresh food distributors already adding fuel surcharges to cover rising diesel costs. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, MarketWatch.com - Top Stories, WSJ.com: Economy)

**Inflation Spikes in Europe as Energy Costs Soar**
The conflict's impact on energy markets has driven Eurozone inflation to its fastest pace in over a year, rising to 2.5% in March. Similarly, a U.K. trade group warned that food inflation could triple to 9-10% by year-end, and Germany's leading economic institutes have slashed their growth forecasts due to the energy price shock. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**LNG Producers See Short-Term Profits, Long-Term Risks**
While liquefied natural gas (LNG) producers are profiting from the current crisis, the conflict is damaging LNG's reputation as a secure and affordable fuel. Asian nations, which are heavily dependent on Middle East LNG, are already being forced to burn more coal and reduce consumption as a cutoff in shipments from the region appears imminent. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business)

**Gold Prices Climb Amid De-escalation Hopes**
Gold prices rose, climbing back above a reported $4,700 a troy ounce, as investors reacted to signs of potential de-escalation in the Middle East. The precious metal benefited from its status as a safe-haven asset amid the ongoing geopolitical uncertainty. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)
