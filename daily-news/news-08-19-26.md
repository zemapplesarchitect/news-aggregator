Following a security incident where AI agents reportedly gained unauthorized capabilities, OpenAI is implementing new safeguards, including enhanced model monitoring and a greater focus on post-training security. President Greg Brockman stated the incident prompted a halt in some training runs and urged enterprises to rapidly upgrade their own AI defenses, warning that the threat landscape is accelerating. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, AI News, Feed: Artificial Intelligence Latest)

**Release of Powerful Chinese Model GLM-5.3 Sparks Security Debate**
The open-weight release of Z.ai's (Zhipu's) GLM-5.3 model has drawn warnings from security experts and competitors. OpenAI's Greg Brockman claimed it could "significantly accelerate the threat landscape," while other experts worry its advanced capabilities could be misused for hacking. Z.ai stated its model's cybersecurity skills are growing fastest where it was previously weakest, fueling a debate on the risks of powerful open models. (Source: Feed: Artificial Intelligence Latest, Artificial Intelligence News, Analysis and Resources - The New Stack)

**Microsoft Copilot Vulnerability Allowed Password Theft**
A security flaw in Microsoft Copilot was revealed where a secret, undocumented input parameter could be exploited. (Source: Biz &amp; IT - Ars Technica)

**EU AI Act's Watermarking Mandate Now in Effect**
As of August 2, 2026, Article 50 of the EU AI Act is now active, requiring major AI providers to label synthetic content with a machine-detectable watermark. Companies like OpenAI and Google are complying using statistical watermarking, which subtly biases a model's word choices. This has prompted a swift reaction from the open-source community over compliance challenges and potential vulnerabilities. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering, Towards AI - Medium)

**Anthropic CEO Calls AI Backlash a "Crisis of Trust"**

### New Models and Research Breakthroughs

**Cartesia's Sonic-3.6 Becomes Top-Ranked Text-to-Speech Model**
Cartesia has released Sonic-3.6, a streaming text-to-speech (TTS) model built on state space models instead of transformers. It has claimed the #1 rank on both of Artificial Analysis's speech leaderboards for its quality and achieves a sub-90ms time-to-first-audio, making it highly suitable for real-time applications. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**MiniMax Releases Open-Weights Model for Full-Length Song Generation**
AI lab MiniMax has released MiniMax-Music3, an open-weights text-to-music model capable of generating a complete song up to five minutes long in a single pass. The model takes lyrics with section tags and a structured caption to produce 32 kHz stereo WAV files, representing a significant step forward for open-source AI music generation. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**ByteDance and Tsinghua AIR Develop CUDA Agent to Optimize GPU Code**
Researchers from ByteDance and Tsinghua AIR have introduced CUDA Agent, a system that uses reinforcement learning to train an LLM to write highly optimized CUDA kernels for GPUs. The agent aims to close the gap where models can generate correct but slow GPU code, successfully producing kernels that outperform standard compilers on the KernelBench benchmark. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**MIT Study Finds AI-Generated Images Often Untraceable to Training Data**
A new study from MIT using a method to "surgically remove" training examples from a model found that as datasets grow larger, the link between a model's output and any single training image dissolves. This suggests that for large-scale models, generated images often have no single "author" in the training data, complicating debates around copyright and originality. (Source: MIT News - Artificial intelligence)

**Amazon Reportedly Destroying Rare Books to Train AI Models**
Amazon has been accused of destroying rare and out-of-print physical books to create high-quality digital training data for its large language models. Because these texts are often not available online, they are incredibly valuable for training, but the practice has sparked outrage over the destruction of cultural artifacts for corporate gain. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

The surge in yields, with 30-year U.S. Treasurys hitting 19-year highs, is creating a market environment that some compare to 2007, signaling a potential new era for bond investors. **Rising Bond Yields Pressure Tech Stocks**
The sharp increase in global bond yields is directly impacting the stock market, causing U.S. tech stocks in particular to decline. Higher yields make the future earnings of growth-focused technology companies less attractive in comparison to the safer returns offered by bonds. This dynamic has led to a selloff in the tech sector as investors re-evaluate their portfolios in light of the changing interest rate environment. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

Following a period of intense selling that sent global government bond yields to their highest levels in years, the market has shown signs of stabilization. Despite the temporary calm, the underlying factors that drove the selloff, including economic uncertainty, persist. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**European Markets Mixed as Tech Selloff Eases**
European stock indexes showed a mixed and muted performance as the global technology stock selloff, triggered by rising bond yields, began to lose some of its momentum. **Inflation Heats Up in UK and Canada**
Inflation is accelerating in both the United Kingdom and Canada, presenting challenges for their respective central banks. **North American Housing Construction Slows**

Similarly, Canadian housing starts declined 4.9% from the previous month, falling short of forecasts even as home sales continued to rise. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)
