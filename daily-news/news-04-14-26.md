## AI News Digest

### Major Model Releases &amp; Research Breakthroughs

**Anthropic Releases Powerful but Restricted Claude Mythos Model**
Anthropic has introduced Claude Mythos Preview, its most advanced AI model, with powerful capabilities in reasoning, coding, and cybersecurity. Due to its effectiveness in discovering critical security flaws, public access is being withheld; it is only available to a select consortium of tech companies via Project Glasswing, highlighting the dual-use dilemma of powerful AI. The model's power has also attracted attention from the White House and industry leaders, who are racing to use such tools to find and fix bugs before they can be exploited. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering, Technology - WSJ.com)

**Google Launches Gemma 4 for On-Device AI**
Google has released Gemma 4, a new family of open-weight models designed to enable local, on-device AI, particularly for Android development. The models are optimized to support the entire software lifecycle, from coding assistance to production inference on mobile devices, and include new tool-calling capabilities. This focus on "edge AI" aims to make powerful, agentic AI more accessible and private by running it directly on user hardware. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering, MachineLearningMastery.com)

**NVIDIA and University of Maryland Release Audio Flamingo Next**
Researchers from NVIDIA and the University of Maryland have released Audio Flamingo Next (AF-Next), an open-source large audio-language model. The model aims to advance multimodal AI's ability to robustly understand and reason over various audio types, including speech, environmental sounds, and music. This work addresses a key area where AI development has lagged behind vision-language models. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**MiniMax Open-Sources Self-Evolving Agent Model M2.7**
AI company MiniMax has open-sourced its M2.7 model, making the weights publicly available on Hugging Face. This is significant as it is the company's first model to actively participate in its own development cycle, representing a step towards "self-evolving" AI agents. The model has demonstrated strong performance on coding benchmarks, scoring 56.22% on SWE-Pro. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Meta AI and KAUST Propose "Neural Computers"**
Researchers from Meta AI and King Abdullah University of Science and Technology (KAUST) have introduced the concept of Neural Computers (NCs). This novel machine architecture proposes that a neural network itself can act as the computer, folding computation, memory, and I/O into a single learned model. This contrasts with current systems where a neural network is a software layer running on top of traditional hardware. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Google Proposes "Vantage" to Measure Durable Skills**
Google AI Research has proposed Vantage, an LLM-based protocol for measuring "durable skills" such as collaboration, creativity, and critical thinking. This system aims to evaluate human capabilities that are not reliably captured by standardized tests. The protocol uses AI to assess how individuals handle tasks that require complex, real-world problem-solving. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Anthropic Paper Examines Emotion-Like Mechanisms in LLMs**
In a new research paper, Anthropic's interpretability team examined how large language models like Claude Sonnet 4.5 internally represent concepts related to emotions. The work analyzes the model's internal activations to understand how these representations influence its behavior. This research is a step toward better understanding the internal workings of complex AI models beyond just their outputs. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**AI Revolutionizes Mathematical Research**
Mathematicians are increasingly using AI as a collaborative tool to prove new theorems and generate novel results at an unprecedented pace. This marks a significant shift from AI being a mere calculator to becoming a partner in formal mathematical discovery. Experts believe this is just the beginning of AI's transformative impact on the field. (Source: Quanta Magazine)

## FINANCEANDTECHNOLOGY News Digest

### Geopolitics and Market Reaction

**Markets Rally on Hopes of U.S.-Iran De-escalation**
Global stock markets gained and oil prices slipped below $100 per barrel amid investor optimism for a potential peace deal between the U.S. and Iran. The S&amp;P 500 recovered its losses since the conflict began, and U.S. Treasury yields declined as appetite for riskier assets grew on hopes that talks to suspend Iran's nuclear activity could avert a wider war and ease crude supply disruption fears. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business, MarketWatch.com - Top Stories)

**U.S. Imposes Naval Blockade on Iran, Spooking Shipping and Markets**
The Trump administration initiated a naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz to choke off Iran's oil exports, creating significant economic risk and uncertainty. Shipping companies have expressed reluctance to sail through the critical chokepoint, and while some Iran-linked vessels passed through just before the blockade took effect, the move is seen as a test of which side can endure more economic pain. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Economy)

**Trump's Policy Reversals on Iran Rattle Markets**
Recent shifts in President Trump's stance on the conflict with Iran have caused significant volatility, or "whipsawing," in markets for various investments. The uncertainty surrounding potential peace talks versus escalatory actions like blockades has led to sharp swings in oil prices and equities as investors struggle to price in the geopolitical risk. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**Physical Oil Prices Detach From Financial Markets**
A significant gap has emerged between the price of physical barrels of oil and the prices seen in financial futures markets, with one article citing a difference between $133 and $99. This divergence highlights market stress and logistical challenges in the real world, which are not fully reflected in financial instruments that are more sensitive to geopolitical headlines and speculation. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

### Banking and Financial Sector Earnings

JPMorgan Chase announced increased first-quarter profits, driven by a surge in revenue from markets and investment banking. However, CEO Jamie Dimon issued a cautious outlook, warning of a "complex set of risks" that could derail the economy, and the bank's stock pulled back on a downbeat forecast for net interest income. **Goldman Sachs Posts Record Quarter on Dealmaking and Volatility**
Goldman Sachs reported a 19% rise in first-quarter profit, achieving a record quarter in its banking and trading divisions. **BlackRock Profits Surge on Investment Fee Growth**
BlackRock, the world's largest asset manager, saw its quarterly profit jump 46% due to growth in investment fees and a strong influx into its exchange-traded funds (ETFs). Despite the profit gain, the firm's total assets under management dipped slightly to just under $14 trillion in March. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, MarketWatch.com - Top Stories)

**Fund Manager Pessimism Grows, But Not to Capitulation Levels**
A Bank of America survey of fund managers revealed that pessimism is building due to geopolitical events and the oil price shock. However, sentiment has not reached the "close-your-eyes-and-buy" levels of extreme fear, indicating that investors are cautious but not yet fully cutting risk from their portfolios. (Source: MarketWatch.com - Top Stories)

### Global Economy and Trade

**China's Export Growth Slows Amid Geopolitical Tensions**
China's export momentum slowed sharply in March, indicating that the war in the Middle East is weighing on global demand and threatening a key driver of Beijing's economic growth. Separately, new Chinese regulations are making it harder for foreign firms to move their supply chains out of the country, raising concerns about potential penalties for executives and companies. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy, NYT &gt; Business)

**EU Agrees to Double Tariffs on Excess Steel Imports**
The European Parliament has reached a deal to protect its steel industry by cutting the amount of tariff-free steel imports to 18.3 million metric tons annually. Tariffs on any imports above that quota will be raised to 50%, a significant protectionist measure. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**Singapore Central Bank Tightens Policy on Inflation and Growth Risks**
Singapore's central bank tightened its monetary policy for the first time in over three years, bracing for slower growth and higher prices resulting from the war in the Middle East. The move is a proactive measure to combat inflation risks stemming from global instability. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**U.S. Home Sales Fall as Spring Season Starts Poorly**
Sales of existing homes in the U.S. declined by 3.6% in March to a nine-month low, marking a weak start to the crucial spring selling season. The slowdown is attributed to rising mortgage rates and broader economic uncertainty weighing on potential buyers. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)
