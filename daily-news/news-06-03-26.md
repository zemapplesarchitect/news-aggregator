## AI News Digest

### Corporate Strategy &amp; Major Moves

**Alphabet Plans $80B Fundraising for Massive AI Expansion**
Alphabet, Google's parent company, announced plans to raise $80 billion to fund a significant build-out of its AI infrastructure. The company cited strong enterprise and consumer demand for its AI services that is currently exceeding its available supply, signaling a major escalation in the AI spending race. This move highlights the immense capital required to compete at the highest level of AI development and deployment. (Source: TechCrunch, Technology - WSJ.com)

**Microsoft Unveils "Scout" and a Suite of AI Tools at Build 2026**
At its Build 2026 conference, Microsoft launched "Scout," a new personal AI assistant for work designed to integrate into Microsoft 365 and automate office tasks within platforms like Teams. The company also emphasized that the enterprise AI race will be won on data context, not just model power, and released numerous developer-focused tools, including an "Intelligent Terminal" and new frameworks for testing and controlling AI agent behavior. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, Wired, The New Stack)

**Anthropic Confidentially Files for IPO and Expands Enterprise Focus**
AI safety-focused startup Anthropic has confidentially filed paperwork for what could be one of the largest IPOs in history, following a period of rapid growth and high-profile funding. In parallel, the company is expanding its enterprise partner program and scaling access to its Claude Mythos model for cybersecurity in critical infrastructure, aiming to demonstrate revenue durability and a strong enterprise strategy ahead of going public. (Source: Wired, Technology - WSJ.com, The New Stack)

**Meta Rolls Out Business AI Globally, Envisions AI-Run Companies**
Meta has made its AI agent for WhatsApp Business available to businesses globally, with a pricing model based on token usage. CEO Mark Zuckerberg articulated a broader vision where Meta's AI agents could eventually handle entire business operations, moving the company's focus beyond its consumer social media core and into the lucrative enterprise AI market. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, Technology - WSJ.com)

**Nvidia Targets PC Market with New Chips and AI Agent Partnerships**
Nvidia is making a major play for the $200 billion CPU market with its new RTX Spark laptop chips, designed to make the "AI PC" a practical reality. The company is partnering with Microsoft, Dell, and HP to release a new generation of PCs equipped with powerful AI agent capabilities, aiming to bring advanced, locally-run AI to the mass market. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, Feed: Artificial Intelligence Latest)

**OpenAI Expands Codex with Tools for White-Collar Professionals**
OpenAI has launched a new set of tools for its Codex platform specifically aimed at non-coding knowledge workers. The release includes six plugins for jobs like data analytics, sales, product design, and investment banking, bundling integrations and instructions to allow Codex to approximate specific professional roles, reflecting a strategy to broaden its user base beyond developers. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, The New Stack)

**Uber Imposes Caps on Employee AI Spending After Exceeding Budget**
Ride-hailing giant Uber has implemented spending caps on employee use of AI tools after reportedly blowing through its annual budget in just four months. This move comes after the company had initially encouraged widespread experimentation with AI, highlighting the unexpectedly high operational costs associated with large-scale adoption of generative AI technologies in a corporate environment. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

### AI Funding, IPOs, and Market Valuations

AI monitoring and observability platform Coralogix has raised a $200 million Series F round, reaching a valuation of $1.6 billion. The funding will be used to build out its platform, which is designed to watch over and manage the behavior of autonomous AI agents, a growing need as enterprises deploy more complex AI systems. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Cybersecurity Firm Cyera Nears $300M Round at $12B Valuation**
AI-powered cybersecurity company Cyera is reportedly close to securing a $300 million funding round led by Evolution Equity Partners. The deal would value the company at an estimated $12 billion, an 80x multiple of its annual recurring revenue, demonstrating immense investor confidence in AI-driven data security despite the company's current operating losses. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Market Research Firm AlphaSense Reaches $7.5B Valuation**
AlphaSense, a market-research firm using AI to analyze corporate and financial documents, has raised $350 million in a new funding round, bringing its valuation to $7.5 billion. The round included investments from firms like Accenture and JPMorgan’s asset-management unit, underscoring the high value placed on AI platforms that can extract insights from vast amounts of unstructured data. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

**Spanish AI Software Group Factorial Valued at $2.5 Billion**
Factorial, a Spanish business-software company that uses AI to automate HR processes, has hit a $2.5 billion valuation in a new funding round led by General Catalyst. The fresh capital will fuel its expansion as AI continues to reshape the market for enterprise software and business management tools. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

**Rocket Startup Impulse Space Raises $500M, Prioritizing Human Engineers**
Impulse Space, a rocket engine startup, has raised $500 million in a new funding round. The company's president, Eric Romo, notably stated that the funds would be used to hire people, not AI, emphasizing that engineering complex physical systems still fundamentally depends on human talent and expertise. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

### Regulation, Policy, and Legal Battles

**Trump Signs Narrowed Executive Order on AI Oversight**
After facing industry objections, President Trump signed a revised executive order on AI that scales back government oversight. The new order requires only voluntary pre-release reviews of advanced AI models by the government, a significant softening from a previously shelved version that had proposed mandatory checks 30 days before public release. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, Technology - WSJ.com)

**UK Regulator Forces Google to Allow Publisher Opt-Out from AI Search**
Following pressure from publishers, UK regulators are requiring Google to offer a tool that allows websites to opt out of having their content used in its generative AI search features. This mechanism, set to be tested in the UK before a global rollout, aims to give publishers more control and a stronger negotiating position over how their content is used by AI systems. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, Technology - WSJ.com)

**Florida Sues OpenAI and Sam Altman Over Violent Incident**
The state of Florida has filed a first-of-its-kind lawsuit against OpenAI and its CEO, Sam Altman, alleging the company's technology played a role in a violent incident. The lawsuit centers on a shooting at Florida State University, claiming that ChatGPT was involved, setting a new legal precedent for holding AI companies liable for the use of their products. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Amazon Faces Class-Action Lawsuit Over Ring's Facial Recognition**
Amazon is facing a class-action lawsuit over the "Familiar Faces" feature in its Ring security cameras. The suit, filed in Seattle, claims the feature illegally stores biometric data and images of passersby without their consent, raising significant privacy concerns about mass-market surveillance technology. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

### New Models, Products, and Developer Tools

**Anthropic's Claude Code Gains Dynamic Workflows for Agent Coordination**
Anthropic has introduced "Dynamic Workflows" for Claude Code, a new capability that allows the AI to manage complex software engineering tasks by orchestrating large numbers of AI agents. The system can dynamically create scripts, break down work into parallel subtasks, and validate results, enabling it to tackle more sophisticated development projects autonomously. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**Nvidia Releases Cosmos 3, an Open Omni-model for Physical AI**
Nvidia has launched Cosmos 3, which it describes as the first open omni-model designed for physical AI reasoning and action. Released on Hugging Face, this model is built to understand and interact with the physical world, a critical step toward developing more capable robots and embodied AI agents. **Google Deploys AI-Powered Fake Call Detection**
To combat a rise in sophisticated scams, Google is rolling out a new feature that uses AI to detect fake calls. The system is designed to identify AI-generated deepfake voices and spoofed phone numbers used by scammers impersonating authority figures or family members, providing users with a real-time warning. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**GitHub Copilot Switches to Usage-Based Billing**
GitHub has officially retired its premium request model for Copilot and moved to a usage-based billing system. (Source: The New Stack)

**MIT Researchers Teach AI to Interpret Charts with ChartNet**
Researchers at MIT have developed ChartNet, a new training dataset designed to improve the ability of vision-language models to understand and interpret charts and graphs. This work is significant for enabling AI to more accurately analyze business trends, scientific figures, and other data visualizations. (Source: MIT News - Artificial intelligence)

### Security, Safety, and Vulnerabilities

**Anthropic Expands Claude Mythos Access to Secure Critical Infrastructure**
Anthropic is scaling its Project Glasswing security program by providing its Claude Mythos model to 150 organizations across 15 countries. The initiative targets critical infrastructure sectors like power, water, and healthcare, where a successful cyberattack could be "catastrophic," using AI to help defenders find and fix vulnerabilities. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, The New Stack)

**"BadHost" Vulnerability Exposes AI Agents and LLM Gateways**
A high-severity authentication bypass vulnerability, dubbed "BadHost," has been discovered in the widely used Python web framework Starlette. The flaw allows attackers to use malformed HTTP Host headers to bypass access controls, potentially exposing sensitive AI agent infrastructure, evaluators, and LLM gateways to unauthorized access. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**Red Hat Packages Backdoored Through Official NPM Channel**
A security breach has been identified where dozens of official Red Hat software packages were backdoored through the company's channel on the NPM registry. The incident represents a significant supply chain attack, and security experts have urged any developers who have downloaded the affected packages to conduct immediate investigations of their systems. (Source: Biz &amp; IT - Ars Technica)

**Study Finds Top AI Models from Major Labs Remain Vulnerable**
A study from Cisco has found that leading AI models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Amazon, and xAI all fail to defend against a specific type of attack. The finding suggests that current safety benchmarks used by enterprises to evaluate AI models may be measuring the wrong things and failing to capture critical vulnerabilities. (Source: The New Stack)

### Global Economic Pressures &amp; Geopolitics

**Middle East Conflict Escalates, Driving Oil Prices and Economic Risk**
An exchange of military strikes between the U.S. and Iran, including an Iranian drone attack on Kuwait's main airport and a projectile strike on an MSC-owned ship in an Iraqi port, has caused oil prices to surge. The escalating conflict is fueling fears of a prolonged disruption to global energy supplies and shipping lanes, particularly the Strait of Hormuz, with oil prices jumping over 6% on the news. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business)

**OECD Warns of Severe Global Slowdown from Prolonged Conflict**
The Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD) issued a stark warning that a protracted Middle East war could trigger a severe global economic slowdown. The Paris-based body projects that if disruptions to energy and shipping continue into next year, global growth could fall to just 2.1% in 2026, the weakest rate this century outside of major recessions. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy, NYT &gt; Business)

**Trump Administration Expands Tariff Agenda on Multiple Fronts**
The Trump administration is significantly expanding its trade protectionism, proposing new tariffs against dozens of countries. The plans include a 25% tariff on Brazil for unfair trade practices and levies up to 12.5% on 59 countries and the EU for failing to curb goods made with forced labor. This comes as the administration also fights a court order to refund some previously collected tariffs. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Economy)

**Chinese Subsidies Drive 60% of Market Share Gains, OECD Finds**
A new OECD report concludes that massive state subsidies are the primary driver of China's growing dominance in global markets. The research found that over the past two decades, Chinese businesses received three to eight times more government support than their international competitors, accounting for 60% of their market share gains and validating long-standing complaints about unfair competition. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**EU Unveils Plan for "Tech Sovereignty" from US Giants**
The European Union has outlined a strategic plan to reduce its dependence on American technology companies and bolster its "tech sovereignty." The initiative aims to strengthen the 27-nation bloc's control over its digital future by expanding its own data centers, semiconductor manufacturing, and cloud computing capabilities. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)

### U.S. Economy &amp; Central Bank Policy

**Strong Jobs Data and Inflation Concerns Rattle Markets**
U.S. private companies added 122,000 jobs in May, according to ADP, surpassing economist expectations of 110,000 and signaling a resilient labor market. This stronger-than-expected data, combined with persistent inflation worries, caused U.S. stocks to weaken and Treasury yields to rise as investors anticipated a more hawkish Federal Reserve. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, WSJ.com: Economy)

**Fed Officials Signal More Aggressive Stance on Inflation**
Recent comments from Federal Reserve officials suggest a growing willingness to tighten policy further. Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack, a voting member this year, stated that current policy may not be restrictive enough to reach the 2% inflation target. Separately, new Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh appointed two outside advisers, one of whom previously helped write a plan for a radical restructuring of the central bank. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**Global Central Banks Weigh Rate Hikes Amid Uncertainty**
Central banks in the U.K., Japan, and the Eurozone are all signaling potential interest rate hikes. A Bank of England official said the case for a rate increase is growing due to the Mideast conflict, while the Bank of Japan governor stated he could raise rates despite global uncertainty. Meanwhile, high household inflation expectations in the Eurozone are putting pressure on the ECB to act. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**U.S. Economic Activity Expands in Services and Manufacturing**
U.S. economic activity showed continued strength, with the ISM services index rising to 54.5 and the manufacturing index hitting 54 in May, its highest reading since May 2022. This expansion, along with a 0.4% increase in construction spending in April, points to underlying resilience in the economy despite higher interest rates. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)
