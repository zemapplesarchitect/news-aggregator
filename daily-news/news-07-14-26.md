## FINANCEANDTECHNOLOGY News Digest

### Corporate Earnings &amp; Market Performance
**Goldman Sachs Posts Record Quarter to Kick Off Bank Earnings**
Goldman Sachs reported a record-breaking quarter, providing a strong start to the bank earnings season. The positive results set an optimistic tone for the financial sector's health, though investors remain watchful for broader market trends and an upcoming inflation report. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**U.S. Stocks Fall as AI Selloff and Oil Jump Rattle Markets**
U.S. stock markets declined as investor confidence was shaken by a dual shock: a sharp selloff in artificial-intelligence chip stocks and a significant jump in oil prices. The drop highlights market sensitivity to both tech sector sentiment and geopolitical events that impact commodity prices and inflation. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**IBM Shares Tumble on Earnings Warning**
IBM's stock price fell sharply after the company issued an earnings warning related to its new AI-focused enterprise mainframe. The stumble occurred as customers shifted spending to other products like servers and storage, underscoring the execution risks and competitive pressures within the booming AI hardware market. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**Tech, Media &amp; Telecom Roundup**
A summary of market analysis provided insights on various companies in the technology, media, and telecommunications sectors. Key companies discussed included Ericsson, Victory Giant Technology, and Bilibili, offering a snapshot of current trends and performance in these industries. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

### The AI Economy: Investment and Impact
**Data-Center Builders Race to Offload Billions in Stakes**
Driven by the unrelenting demand for computing power to support artificial intelligence, data-center developers are rushing to sell stakes in their facilities, valued in the billions. This trend reflects intense investor appetite to own the physical infrastructure that underpins the AI revolution. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**AI's Role in Drug Discovery May Not Meet Wall Street's Timeline**
While AI technology is being integrated into pharmaceutical labs, there is skepticism about its short-term impact on creating better drugs. The analysis suggests that the timeline for tangible, AI-driven breakthroughs may be longer than what investors are currently pricing into company valuations. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**AI May Help Address Future Worker Shortages**
New economic research is beginning to shift the narrative around AI's effect on the job market. Rather than simply being a source of job displacement, AI could potentially help solve labor crises by filling gaps and picking up the slack in economies facing a shortage of workers. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

### Global Economy &amp; Central Bank Policy
**Economists See More Stubborn Inflation Post-War**
According to the latest WSJ survey of economists, the recent war with Iran did less damage to the U.S. economy than initially feared. However, a significant negative consequence is that the conflict has left inflation more persistent, prompting economists to lower recession probabilities but project that inflation will remain higher for longer. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**New Fed Chairman Faces Decision on Reversing Rate Cuts**
The new Federal Reserve chairman, Warsh, faces a critical first decision on whether to undo last year's interest-rate cuts as he prepares for testimony. A more resilient economy combined with stubborn inflation has put a potential rate increase back into consideration. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**China Exports Surged in June, Exceeding Expectations**
China’s exports grew unexpectedly in June, defying forecasts of a slowdown and providing a crucial boost to its economy. This strong export performance helps offset ongoing weakness in domestic demand that has been weighing on the country's overall growth. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**South Korea Raises 2026 Growth and Inflation Forecasts**
South Korea has revised its economic forecasts for 2026 upward, anticipating stronger growth and higher inflation. This optimism is fueled by a continued boom in its critical semiconductor industry and the effects of supportive government policy measures. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**Bank of Canada Expected to Keep Interest Rates Steady**
A survey of economists by the WSJ suggests the Bank of Canada will likely hold its policy rate steady for the sixth consecutive time. The central bank is navigating a difficult environment where inflation remains too hot to justify a rate cut, while economic activity is too tepid to warrant a rate hike. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**New Zealand Central Bank Warns Firms Are Passing on Costs**
The Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) has highlighted that businesses are now more willing to pass increased costs onto consumers, largely due to repeated global fuel price shocks. This trend is stoking concerns that the RBNZ may need to raise interest rates further to combat inflationary pressures. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

### Commodities &amp; Fixed Income Markets
**Oil Surges Most Since 2020 on Geopolitical Tensions**
Oil prices experienced their largest single-day surge since 2020, with the international crude benchmark climbing nearly 10%. The spike was triggered by a statement from President Trump that he would reimpose a U.S. blockade on a key strait, fueling market bets that supply disruptions will persist. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**U.S. Treasury Yields Rise on Renewed Inflation Fears**
Yields on U.S. Treasury bonds rose as the sharp increase in oil prices, driven by military escalation in the Middle East, reignited concerns about inflation. Higher yields reflect investor demand for greater returns to compensate for the rising risk of inflation eroding the value of future bond payments. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**Gold Prices Slide, Testing Investor Faith**
Despite a fresh round of military attacks between the U.S. and Iran, gold prices have continued to slide from their recent record highs. This unusual price action is causing even long-term gold investors to reconsider their positions, as the precious metal is not behaving as a typical safe-haven asset. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**Corporate Bond Yields Are High, But May Not Compensate for Risk**
While corporate bonds are currently offering their best yields in years, an analysis suggests they may still not be attractive enough. Investors are weighing whether the higher potential returns are sufficient to compensate for the increased risks in the current economic climate. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

### Investor Analysis &amp; Strategy
**S&amp;P 500 Valuation May Not Be as Cheap as It Appears**
An analysis of the S&amp;P 500's valuation warns investors to look closely at the details, as the index may not be as cheap as it seems. The widely used price/earnings (P/E) ratio can be misleading because its value depends heavily on which definition of "earnings" is being used in the calculation. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**Higher Spending on Investing May Not Guarantee Better Returns**
A commentary piece cautions investors that spending more on financial advice or investment services does not necessarily lead to superior, "all-star" returns. This serves as a reminder to focus on fundamental value and strategy, especially as bank earnings and inflation data come into focus. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)
