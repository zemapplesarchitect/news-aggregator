## AI News Digest

### The Anthropic-Government Standoff
**Anthropic Halts Powerful New Models Under Government Pressure**
Anthropic launched its new, highly capable models, Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5, designed for long-horizon tasks, but was forced to suspend access shortly after due to a U.S. government export control directive. The move has sparked a high-stakes conflict between the AI lab and the Trump administration over concerns about the models' potential for misuse, particularly in cybersecurity. Anthropic leaders have been in Washington D.C. for high-level talks to resolve the issue, stating both parties are working quickly on a resolution. (Source: InfoQ, TechCrunch, WSJ, Wired)

**Cybersecurity Community Protests Ban on Anthropic Models**
Dozens of cybersecurity experts have publicly protested the White House's export-control restrictions on Anthropic's Fable and Mythos models, calling the ban "dangerous." They argue the order limits the ability of cybersecurity defenders to use advanced AI for securing software and products, effectively handing an advantage to malicious actors. The controversy highlights the tension between preventing AI misuse and enabling AI-powered defense, with some analysts suggesting the ban was a reactionary or retaliatory measure unrelated to a specific jailbreak. (Source: TechCrunch, WSJ)

**Anthropic's "Safety" Marketing May Have Backfired**
Some analysts suggest that Anthropic's long-standing marketing emphasis on AI safety and the potential dangers of powerful models may have inadvertently created the political climate for the government's intervention. By ringing the alarm about AI's potential dangers, the company may have made its own advanced models a prime target for regulation. Despite the political turmoil, sales data from Ramp suggests Anthropic's popularity with business users is growing, and the feud could even boost its profile. (Source: WSJ, TechCrunch)

### Major Corporate Moves: M&amp;A, IPOs, and Pivots

The acquisition is a massive bet to bolster SpaceX's AI division as it seeks to compete in what it told investors is a $26 trillion addressable market. AI translation leader DeepL has acquired Mixhalo, a company specializing in live-event audio streaming, to integrate real-time translation into live events like concerts and conferences. This strategic acquisition marks DeepL's expansion into a new market vertical and includes the opening of a new San Francisco office to grow its U.S. presence. The move combines DeepL's translation technology with Mixhalo's low-latency audio streaming platform. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Footwear Brand Allbirds Pivots to AI, Rebrands as 'Smartbird'**
Struggling footwear company Allbirds is undergoing a radical transformation, changing its name to Smartbird and pivoting to become an AI business. The company has appointed Nadia Carlsten, a figure with a background in technology and venture capital, as its new CEO to lead the new direction. This dramatic shift represents a high-stakes attempt to find a new path to growth by tapping into the current investor enthusiasm for artificial intelligence. (Source: WSJ)

### AI Infrastructure and The Hardware Arms Race
**DOJ Cites National Security to Defend xAI's Unpermitted Power Generation**
The U.S. Department of Justice has intervened in a lawsuit against Elon Musk's xAI, arguing that the company's unpermitted gas turbines are "vital" for national, economic, and energy security. The DOJ claims the Pentagon and military operations, including those related to the Iran War, depend on xAI's continued operation, seeking to dismiss an NAACP lawsuit over the turbines' pollution. This development reveals the deep ties forming between AI companies and national security interests. (Source: TechCrunch, Wired)

**Google and Canadian Pension Fund Fuel Global Data Center Expansion**
The race to build AI infrastructure is accelerating with massive new investments. Google announced a $1.5 billion plan for 2026-2027 to expand its data center campus in Jackson County, Alabama. Meanwhile, a major Canadian pension giant is investing in India's data center boom by acquiring an 8.2% stake in CtrlS, which operates over 15 data centers across the country, highlighting the global demand for AI-powering facilities. (Source: AI, TechCrunch)

**AMD Under Fire for Removing Memory Encryption from Consumer CPUs**
AMD is facing criticism from users after it was discovered the company stripped the Transparent SME (TSME) memory encryption feature from its consumer-grade CPUs. The move, which appears to be a deliberate and unannounced decision, has raised security concerns among users who relied on the feature for an added layer of data protection. This decision highlights the ongoing tension between cost, features, and security in the competitive consumer hardware market. (Source: Ars Technica)

### Security, Safety, and Privacy
**Critical Vulnerability in GitHub Copilot Exposed User Data**
A critical security flaw, dubbed SearchLeak, was discovered in GitHub Copilot that allowed hackers to steal sensitive user information, including two-factor authentication (2FA) codes. The vulnerability highlights the persistent security challenges in Large Language Model (LLM) applications and the risks associated with how they handle and process user data. The exploit demonstrates a recurring failure point in the industry's current approach to LLM security. (Source: Ars Technica)

**Meta Prototyped Face Recognition for Smart Glasses with Pentagon Supplier**
It was revealed that Meta contracted Rank One Computing, a company with deep ties to U.S. intelligence and a Pentagon supplier, to prototype a face recognition app for its smart glasses. The technology was used for internal development, but the partnership with a firm whose board includes a former CIA deputy director and an FBI science chief raises significant privacy and ethical questions about the future of Meta's wearable technology. (Source: Wired)
