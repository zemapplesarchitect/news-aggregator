## AI News Digest

### Big Tech's AI Arms Race: Record Spending and Cloud Growth

**Big Tech Commits Over $630 Billion to AI Infrastructure**
Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, and Amazon collectively reported strong Q1 2026 earnings driven by AI demand, particularly in their cloud divisions. In response, they have announced a massive increase in capital expenditures, with combined commitments for AI infrastructure now estimated between $630 billion and $650 billion, signaling an intensifying arms race to build out capacity for future AI services. (Source: AI News, The Wall Street Journal)

**Microsoft's AI Bet Pays Off with Strong Cloud Growth and Copilot Adoption**
Microsoft reported strong Q3 2026 earnings, with sales reaching $82.9 billion, beating analyst expectations due to surging demand for its AI-infused cloud services. The company revealed it now has over 20 million paid users for its Copilot AI assistant, and CEO Satya Nadella stated Microsoft "fully plans to exploit" its new, non-exclusive partnership with OpenAI to bolster its offerings. (Source: TechCrunch, The Wall Street Journal)

**Google Cloud Surpasses $20B in Revenue Amidst Capacity Constraints**
Alphabet's Google Cloud division achieved a milestone, topping $20 billion in quarterly revenue for the first time, largely fueled by demand for AI workloads. However, the company noted that growth was constrained by available capacity, indicating that its revenue could have been even higher as it races to build out more data centers. (Source: TechCrunch, The Wall Street Journal)

**Amazon's Cloud Business Surges, Prompting Increased AI Spending**
Amazon's Q1 earnings showed a significant surge in its AWS cloud business, which it attributed directly to customer interest in generative AI. Following the strong results, the company announced it would substantially increase its capital spending to meet the growing demand for AI infrastructure and services. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Meta's Revenue Jumps, But AI and Metaverse Costs Continue to Climb**
Meta posted its largest quarterly revenue increase in recent history, but also announced it would spend even more than previously forecast on AI data centers this year. The company's Reality Labs division, focused on AR/VR, continues to lose billions of dollars each quarter, while its business-focused AI tools now facilitate 10 million conversations per week. (Source: TechCrunch, The Wall Street Journal)

**GitHub Shifts Copilot to Usage-Based Billing**
In response to the high computational costs of running its AI coding assistant, Microsoft's GitHub announced it is overhauling its pricing for Copilot. The company will move to a usage-based billing model, a shift from its previous flat-fee subscription for enterprise users. (Source: The New Stack)

### The Rise of Agentic AI: New Frameworks and Developer Tools

**OpenAI Launches GPT-5.5 as a Premier "Agentic AI" Model**
OpenAI has launched GPT-5.5, which it describes as its most capable "agentic AI" model to date, designed from the ground up to plan, use tools, and work through complex tasks independently. This new class of model comes at a premium, with an API price that is double that of its predecessor, reflecting its advanced capabilities for powering autonomous agents. (Source: AI News)

**Vercel Releases Open Agents for Background AI Workflows**
Vercel has launched Open Agents, an open-source application that allows developers to create and execute AI coding agents that run in the background. The framework provides a complete stack for building and deploying independent coding workflows without being tied to a local machine. (Source: InfoQ)

**Mistral AI Introduces "Workflows" for Enterprise Orchestration**
Mistral AI has launched Workflows, an orchestration layer designed to help enterprises reliably deploy, coordinate, and monitor complex AI models and agents in production. The platform, now in public preview, aims to solve the infrastructure challenges that hinder the deployment of advanced multi-agent systems. (Source: InfoQ)

**Cloudflare Announces "Agent Memory" for Persistent AI Context**
Cloudflare has entered the AI agent space with Agent Memory, a managed service that allows AI agents to have persistent memory. The service extracts structured memories from conversations and retrieves them on demand, enabling agents to maintain context over long-running interactions and share knowledge within teams. (Source: InfoQ)

**Cursor Launches TypeScript SDK for Building Coding Agents**
AI-native code editor Cursor has introduced a TypeScript SDK for developers to build and deploy programmatic coding agents. The SDK provides tools for creating agents that operate in sandboxed cloud VMs, can call subagents, and use a token-based pricing model for execution. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Google Cloud and Sauce Labs Launch New Agent Development Tools**
Google Cloud introduced its Agents CLI, a command-line interface designed to streamline the entire AI agent development lifecycle from local prototyping to production. Separately, Sauce Labs launched Sauce AI for Test Authoring, an agent that automates the creation of software test suites directly from business requirements. (Source: InfoQ)

### New Models and Open Source Contributions

**IBM Releases Granite 4.1 LLMs and New Speech Models**
IBM has open-sourced a family of its Granite code models and released details on the architecture of its Granite 4.1 LLMs. The company also launched two compact 2-billion-parameter Granite Speech 4.1 models, designed for enterprise-grade automatic speech recognition (ASR) and translation with fast inference. (Source: Hugging Face, MarkTechPost)

**NVIDIA Introduces Nemotron 3 Nano Omni for Multimodal Intelligence**
NVIDIA has unveiled Nemotron 3 Nano Omni, a new long-context multimodal model designed to power advanced AI agents. The model is capable of processing and understanding documents, audio, and video, enabling more sophisticated and context-aware agentic applications. (Source: Hugging Face)

**Poolside AI Releases High-Performing Open-Weight Coding Models**
Startup Poolside AI has released two open-weight agentic coding models, Laguna XS.2 and M.1, which have demonstrated state-of-the-art performance. The models achieved impressive scores of 68.2% and 72.5% respectively on the SWE-bench Verified benchmark for autonomous software engineering tasks. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**SenseTime Releases Fast Image Model Optimized for Chinese Chips**
Sanctioned Chinese AI firm SenseTime has released a new open-source image generation model built for speed. The model is notably optimized to run efficiently on Chinese-made chips, a strategic move as the company navigates US restrictions on its access to advanced semiconductor technology. (Source: Wired)

**Australian Regulator Flags "Poor" AI Agent Governance in Finance**

A targeted review found significant control gaps as firms expand their use of AI in both internal and customer-facing operations. (Source: AI News)

### AI in the Enterprise: Adoption and New Platforms

**IBM Launches "Bob" Platform to Govern Software Development Costs**
IBM has launched Bob, an AI platform designed to help enterprises manage the costs and governance of their software development lifecycle (SDLC). The platform aims to control the "technical debt" and unmanaged liabilities that can be generated by the rapid, unsupervised use of AI coding assistants. (Source: AI News)

**Lovelace Emerges from Stealth with "Context Engine" for Enterprise AI**
A new startup named Lovelace has launched its "Elemental" context engine, which it claims can provide a 1000x improvement in AI's investigative power for enterprises. The technology is designed to help AI systems better understand and connect disparate data sources to solve complex business problems. (Source: The New Stack)

**FDA Turns to AI to Accelerate Clinical Trials**
The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) is actively seeking to use artificial intelligence to speed up the development of new medicines. The agency aims to use AI to streamline the laborious process of collecting, submitting, and analyzing data from clinical trials. (Source: The Wall Street Journal)

**Small Language Models Gain Traction in Enterprise Analytics**
A notable trend is emerging in enterprise analytics, where companies are increasingly opting for smaller, specialized language models over large, general-purpose ones. The shift is driven by the need for faster, more cost-effective query responses, with "tiny" LLMs providing sufficient performance for many business intelligence tasks. (Source: Towards AI)

### AI Research and Technical Breakthroughs

**MIT Researchers Develop "WRING" to Better Debias AI Models**
Researchers at MIT have created a new technique called WRING to address bias in AI vision models, aiming to solve the "Whac-a-mole dilemma" where fixing one bias often creates or amplifies another. The method is designed to be a more holistic and effective approach to debiasing. (Source: MIT News)

**MIT and IBM Launch Joint Lab for AI and Quantum Computing**
Building on a long-standing collaboration, MIT and IBM have launched the new MIT-IBM Computing Research Lab. The lab will focus on charting the future convergence of artificial intelligence, advanced algorithms, and quantum computing. (Source: MIT News)

**Meta's FAIR Lab Releases "NeuralSet" to Bridge Neuroscience and AI**
Meta's Fundamental AI Research (FAIR) lab has released NeuralSet, an open-source Python package for neuro-AI research. The tool is designed to make it easier for researchers to work with various types of neural data, such as fMRI and EEG, in conjunction with modern AI models and embeddings. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Qwen Team Releases FlashQLA to Accelerate Linear Attention**
The team behind the Qwen language models has released FlashQLA, a high-performance kernel library for NVIDIA Hopper GPUs. The library dramatically accelerates a linear attention mechanism known as Gated Delta Network (GDN), achieving up to a 3x speedup for both model pretraining and inference. (Source: MarkTechPost)

### Central Banks and Monetary Policy
**Federal Reserve Holds Rates Steady in Contentious End to Powell's Chairmanship**
The U.S. Federal Reserve held interest rates steady in a divided decision that marked Jerome Powell's final meeting as chair, with four officials dissenting. Powell warned that inflation has not yet peaked and announced he will remain on the Fed's board as a governor after his term ends, a move seen as complicating potential policy shifts under the next administration. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy, NYT &gt; Business)

**Kevin Warsh's Nomination for Fed Chair Advances**
The Senate Banking Committee has advanced Kevin Warsh’s nomination to become the next Federal Reserve chair, setting up a full Senate vote. If confirmed, Warsh will inherit a divided central bank facing significant political pressure and an economic shock from surging energy prices. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Economy)

**European Central Banks Hold Rates Amid Economic Headwinds**
Both the European Central Bank and the Bank of England held their key interest rates steady as they confront a difficult mix of slowing economic growth and rising inflation. The economic pressure is largely attributed to the energy price shock stemming from the conflict in the Middle East. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy, NYT &gt; Business)

**Global Central Banks Diverge on Rate Policy**
Brazil's central bank cut its benchmark Selic lending rate to 14.5% but signaled uncertainty for future moves, citing the war in Iran. Meanwhile, the Bank of Canada kept its main interest rate unchanged at 2.25%, expecting inflation to peak in April. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)
