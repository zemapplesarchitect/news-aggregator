## FINANCEANDTECHNOLOGY News Digest

### Market Performance &amp; Economic Indicators
**Stock Markets End Week on a Downturn Amid AI Volatility**
Major U.S. stock indices, including the S&amp;P 500 and Nasdaq Composite, fell every session this week, capping a period of significant volatility. The downturn was largely driven by a selloff in high-flying AI-themed stocks, which also jolted Asian markets, overshadowing positive economic news and relief from falling fuel prices. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business)

**Consumer Sentiment Improves as Inflation Gauge Rises**
U.S. consumer sentiment improved in June, lifted by moderating gasoline prices, according to the University of Michigan's survey. However, the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation metric, the PCE index, rose 4.1% over the past year, its highest level since April 2023 and more than double the Fed's target, signaling persistent price pressures. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy, NYT &gt; Business)

**Oil Prices Fall to Pre-War Levels, Affecting Broader Markets**
Crude oil prices have returned to levels seen before the recent conflict in Iran, a drop attributed to easing geopolitical tensions. This plunge in oil has contributed to falling U.S. Treasury yields and a stronger dollar, while also impacting the performance of mining stocks, which are increasingly tied to global economic and tech sector volatility. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Markets)

**U.S. Housing Market Shows Signs of Strain**
Sales of new U.S. single-family homes fell to 580,000 in May, missing economists' expectations of 632,000. The decline highlights the strain on the housing market, which is grappling with high interest rates and a chronic shortage of inventory. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**Gold Prices Decline Sharply Over the Week**
Front-month Comex gold futures ended the week with a significant 3.44% loss, despite a modest rise in the final session to settle at $4078.70 a troy ounce. The weekly decline reflects broader market shifts and changing investor sentiment. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

### Central Banking &amp; Monetary Policy
**Federal Reserve Officials Offer Divergent Views on Rate Hikes**
Federal Reserve officials presented mixed signals on future monetary policy, with Minneapolis Fed President Neel Kashkari suggesting a rate hike may be needed this year due to inflation risks. In contrast, New York Fed President John Williams stated that the current policy stance is well-positioned to bring inflation back to the 2% target, though he acknowledged that risks remain. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**Atlanta Fed Leadership Search Stalls and Restarts**
The search for a new president for the Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta has stalled after an initial group of finalists did not result in a selection. The process has been restarted, and White House advisers have reportedly taken an interest, despite having no formal role in the regional bank's selection. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**New Fed Chairman Taps Veteran Economists as Advisers**
Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh has appointed two longtime Fed economists, Daniel Covitz and Eric Engstrom, to serve as his advisers. They will assist the new chairman with policy and analysis. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**International Central Banks Hold Rates Steady**
The Bank of Mexico's board voted unanimously to leave its benchmark interest rate unchanged at 6.5%, indicating a hold for the foreseeable future. Meanwhile, in the Eurozone, household inflation expectations cooled in May, providing some relief to European Central Bank policymakers. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

### Banking, Finance &amp; Regulation
**JPMorgan Reshuffles Leadership in Dimon Succession Race**
JPMorgan Chase named Troy Rohrbaugh and Doug Petno as co-presidents, positioning the two men as the leading internal candidates to eventually succeed CEO Jamie Dimon. The move sidelines previous female contenders who were once seen as front-runners for the top job at the nation's largest bank. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**Leon Black Subpoenaed by Congress Over Epstein Ties**
A House committee issued subpoenas for Apollo co-founder Leon Black, escalating its investigation into his financial connections with convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein. The move came after Black declined to answer questions about non-disclosure agreements with women during a closed-door hearing. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business)

**Big Banks Easily Pass Annual Fed Stress Tests**
The largest U.S. banks, including JPMorgan and Bank of America, passed the Federal Reserve's annual stress tests, which gauge their ability to withstand a severe recession. The tests were considered easier this year after the Fed began releasing key details of the hypothetical scenarios in advance. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**Lawmakers Demand Federal Probe of Prediction Market Polymarket**
Two U.S. senators have called on federal regulators to investigate the prediction market platform Polymarket for alleged deceptive advertising. The request follows reporting by The Wall Street Journal regarding the promotion of certain wagers on the platform. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**World Bank Watchdog Finds Harm from Cambodian Microfinance Investments**
The internal watchdog for the International Finance Corp. (IFC), a World Bank entity, concluded that microfinance lenders it funded in Cambodia harmed borrowers. The investigation found the lenders pressured clients to take on and repay loans they could not afford, leading to significant hardship. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

### Technology, AI &amp; The Chip Sector
**OpenAI Considers Delaying IPO Amidst AI Sector Turbulence**
OpenAI is reportedly leaning toward delaying its highly anticipated Initial Public Offering (IPO) until at least next year, as advisers urge CEO Sam Altman to proceed with caution. The potential delay comes as the broader AI sector faces a "rough summer" of stock volatility, rising memory chip prices, and other financial challenges. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)

**Memory Chip Boom Faces Long-Term Threats**
While chipmaker Micron has indicated the current memory chip shortage will last, the boom faces a long-term threat from innovation. Customers, driven by high prices, are actively developing workarounds and alternative technologies that could eventually reduce demand for traditional memory chips. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)

**Intel's Turnaround Shows Promise but Remains Incomplete**
Intel's chip manufacturing business is showing signs of life after years of struggle, a positive development for the U.S. initiative to increase domestic chip production. However, the company still has a long way to go before its turnaround can be considered a complete success. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)

**Advertisers Grapple with Reaching AI Chatbots**
At the Cannes Lions festival, marketing and advertising professionals discussed a new challenge: how to make their products and brands stand out to artificial intelligence. As AI-driven search and chatbots become more prevalent, companies are exploring strategies to influence these non-human gatekeepers of information. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)

### Corporate &amp; Company News
**Tesla Boosts European Sales with Price Cuts Despite Musk Controversy**
Tesla's sales are rising in Europe, driven by aggressive price cuts and low-interest financing offers. These strategies are successfully attracting buyers, including some who hold unfavorable views of CEO Elon Musk, demonstrating the power of pricing in the competitive EV market. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)

**Uber Implements Stricter Driver Background Checks**
Uber has enacted more stringent background checks for its drivers following a New York Times investigation. The report revealed that the ride-hailing company had previously approved drivers with various criminal convictions, including violent felonies. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)

**Saks Emerges From Bankruptcy as Exemplar Luxury Group**
Retailer Saks has emerged from bankruptcy protection with a new corporate name, Exemplar Luxury Group, and a refined strategy. The company plans to shed non-core assets and focus exclusively on its high-end department store business. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)

**Ryanair Ends Controversial Seating Fee for Families**
Budget airline Ryanair announced it will no longer charge parents an extra fee to be seated with their children. The company said it "reluctantly" changed its policy after a British regulator began an investigation into whether the fees were illegal. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)

### Global Economy &amp; Geopolitics
**Attacks Halt Shipping in Strait of Hormuz, Threatening Recovery**
Renewed attacks on cargo ships in the Persian Gulf have halted traffic through the critical Strait of Hormuz. The disruption, which prompted a U.N. agency to pause ship evacuations, threatens to derail the recovery of one of the world's most important shipping lanes. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)

**Trump Threatens 100% Tariffs on Europe Over Tech Taxes**
President Trump has threatened to impose tariffs of up to 100% on European countries in retaliation for their digital services taxes, which primarily affect U.S. tech giants. The threat raises the possibility of a new trade dispute, potentially overriding a recently finalized trade agreement with the European Union. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)

**South Korean Refinery Scrambles After Oil Shock**
A major jet fuel refinery in Ulsan, South Korea, is struggling to wean itself off Middle Eastern oil following the price shock caused by the Iran war. The refinery is a top exporter of jet fuel to the U.S. West Coast, highlighting the global supply chain's vulnerability to regional conflicts. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)

### Science &amp; Advanced Research
**Mathematicians Upgrade 80-Year-Old 'Erdős Method'**
A powerful mathematical technique known as the "probabilistic method," first developed by Paul Erdős 80 years ago to study networks, has been significantly upgraded. Researchers have enhanced the method, making it an even more potent tool for understanding the properties of random structures. (Source: Quanta Magazine)

**Physicists Develop Audacious Experiments to Trap Neutrinos**
Scientists are building some of the most ambitious experimental setups ever conceived in the hunt for the elusive neutrino. The article details the innovative and massive-scale projects designed to track and trap these "ghostly" particles, which are fundamental to our understanding of physics but notoriously difficult to detect. (Source: Quanta Magazine)
