## AI News Digest

### Regulation, Policy, and Geopolitics

**US Government Halts Release of Anthropic's New Models, Igniting Fierce Debate**
The U.S. government forced Anthropic to withdraw its latest models, Fable 5 and Mythos 5, from distribution just days after their release, citing national security risks under export control regulations. The move was reportedly triggered by Amazon researchers discovering methods to bypass the models' safety guardrails. The decision has been met with significant backlash from cybersecurity experts who signed an open letter calling the action a dangerous precedent, arguing that similar vulnerabilities exist in other publicly available models and that restricting access hinders crucial safety research. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, Feed: Artificial Intelligence Latest, Technology - WSJ.com, InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**Anthropic's Data Sharing Policy for New Models on AWS Bedrock Raises Concerns**
Prior to their withdrawal, using Anthropic's Fable 5 or Mythos 5 models on Amazon Bedrock required users to opt-in to a data sharing agreement, sending all prompts and outputs to Anthropic for a 30-day retention period with potential human review. This policy marked a significant departure from previous Bedrock model agreements, which kept all inference data within the customer's AWS environment. The requirement raised privacy and compliance questions for enterprises considering the new models. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**US Grapples with AI's Role in Modern Warfare and Arms Races**
The U.S. is navigating the complex realities of an AI arms race, highlighted by the use of autonomous systems in conflicts like the war in Ukraine and the recent controversy surrounding Anthropic's models. A new executive order and ongoing policy struggles indicate that the government is attempting to establish rules for AI in real-time, balancing national security with the need for innovation and open research. The situation underscores the global challenge of controlling the proliferation of powerful AI capabilities with military applications. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

**US Regulators Fast-Track Grid Connections for AI Data Centers**
The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) has mandated that grid operators prioritize and streamline the process for connecting new data centers to the electrical grid. This "fast lane" is intended to support the rapid expansion of AI infrastructure, which demands enormous amounts of power. However, the ruling does not address the underlying issue of electricity supply shortages, raising concerns about grid stability and the long-term feasibility of meeting the AI industry's energy needs. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**US Scrutinizes Potential Presence of Advanced ASML Chip Tool in China**
U.S. officials have raised concerns that one of ASML's top-tier, export-controlled chip-making machines may be operating in China, a claim ASML has denied. The controversy highlights the intense geopolitical tensions surrounding advanced semiconductor technology and the challenges of enforcing strict export controls. The presence of such a tool would represent a significant leap in China's domestic chip manufacturing capabilities, undermining U.S. efforts to limit its technological advancement. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

### The Rise of AI Agents

**Microsoft Unveils New Windows Security Strategy for AI Agents**
Microsoft has outlined its strategy to make Windows a secure operating system for autonomous AI agents, centered on the new Microsoft Execution Containers (MXC) SDK. The approach emphasizes that critical security features like containment, identity, and manageability must be built directly into the OS layer to safely run powerful agents. This positions Windows as a foundational platform for the emerging agentic computing paradigm. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**Perplexity Launches 'Brain,' a Self-Improving Memory for its AI Agent**
AI search company Perplexity has introduced "Brain," an advanced memory system for its "Computer" agent. Instead of focusing on user preferences, Brain remembers the agent's own actions, building a context graph of what tasks succeeded, what failed, and what corrections were made. The system reviews this graph overnight to learn and improve, with Perplexity reporting early gains in correctness, recall, and cost-efficiency. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Azure Functions Ships Serverless Agents Runtime**
Microsoft has released a public preview of a serverless agents runtime for Azure Functions, allowing developers to build and deploy AI agents more easily. Agents are defined in simple markdown files and can be triggered by events, access over 1,400 connectors via the Model Context Protocol (MCP), and run in a sandboxed environment. Microsoft confirmed the runtime adds no cold start overhead or billing premium over standard consumption plans. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**NVIDIA Introduces SpatialClaw, a Training-Free Agent for 3D Reasoning**
NVIDIA AI has developed SpatialClaw, a novel AI agent that performs complex 3D spatial reasoning tasks without requiring any specific training. The agent operates by writing and executing Python code in a persistent kernel, composing various perception tools to understand and interact with its environment. This code-as-action approach provides a flexible and powerful interface for spatial problem-solving. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**OpenAI Details 'Kepler,' Its Internal Data Analyst AI Agent**
In a presentation, OpenAI's Bonnie Xu detailed Kepler, an internal AI agent built to function as a data analyst for the company's massive 600+ petabyte datasets. Kepler overcomes context window limitations by using the Model Context Protocol (MCP), automated code crawling, and RAG. The system also features a "scoped semantic memory" for self-learning and uses a sophisticated evaluation pipeline to ensure its analytical outputs are robust and accurate. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

### New Models, Frameworks, and Open Source

**Apple Launches 'Core AI' Framework for On-Device Generative AI**
At its Worldwide Developers Conference (WWDC), Apple announced Core AI, the official successor to its Core ML framework. Core AI is specifically designed to enable developers to run large language models and other generative AI tasks entirely on-device on Apple Silicon hardware. The framework supports both custom-converted PyTorch models and a library of pre-optimized open-source models, marking a major push for privacy-centric, on-device AI. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**Cisco Open-Sources FAPO for Automated LLM Pipeline Optimization**
Cisco Foundation AI has open-sourced FAPO (Fully Automated Prompt Optimization), a system that uses Anthropic's Claude to autonomously optimize multi-step LLM pipelines. FAPO evaluates a chain of operations, attributes failures to specific steps, and proposes and validates improvements to prompts, parameters, and the overall chain structure. In Cisco's internal tests, it outperformed existing methods on 15 of 18 benchmarks. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Yandex Releases YaFF, a High-Speed Data Format for Protobuf**
Yandex has open-sourced YaFF (Yandex Fast Format), a zero-copy wire format for the Protobuf ecosystem that significantly speeds up data reading. YaFF changes how data is represented in memory without altering the source `.proto` file, with its "Flat Layout" achieving read speeds within 1.2 times that of a raw C++ struct. The format is already in use at Yandex, delivering 10-20% CPU savings in its advertising recommendation system. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Liquid AI Releases Multilingual Models for Edge-Device Search**
Liquid AI has introduced LFM2.5-Embedding-350M and LFM2.5-ColBERT-350M, a pair of efficient models designed for fast, multilingual search across 11 languages on edge devices. The system combines a dense bi-encoder for initial retrieval with a ColBERT late-interaction model for re-ranking, providing a powerful and lightweight solution for on-device semantic search applications. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**New 3B Reasoning Model 'VibeThinker-3B' Claims High Performance**
A new 3-billion-parameter dense model named VibeThinker-3B has been released, built upon Qwen2.5-Coder-3B using a "Spectrum-to-Signal" post-training pipeline. The creators claim the MIT-licensed model achieves performance on verifiable reasoning benchmarks that is competitive with much larger models like DeepSeek V3.2 and Kimi K2.5. (Source: MarkTechPost)
