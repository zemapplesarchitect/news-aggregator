## AI News Digest

### Corporate Strategy, Funding, and Market Moves

**SambaNova Raises $1B at $11B Valuation in New Funding Round**
AI chip and systems company SambaNova has raised $1 billion in a Series F funding round, reaching an $11 billion valuation. This major funding event, occurring just five months after its last mega-round, underscores the intense investor appetite for AI hardware companies that offer alternatives to NVIDIA and highlights the capital-intensive nature of competing in the AI chip market. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**SK Hynix Pursues US IPO Amid AI-Fueled Memory Chip Boom**
South Korean memory chip manufacturer SK Hynix is set to list on a U.S. stock exchange, capitalizing on the surging demand for its high-bandwidth memory (HBM) chips, which are critical components for AI accelerators. The move will give U.S. investors direct access to another key player in the AI supply chain, though some analysts caution its valuation may already be high. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, Technology - WSJ.com)

**TeraWulf and Anthropic Announce $19 Billion AI Infrastructure Deal**
Bitcoin miner TeraWulf has signed a massive deal to lease an AI infrastructure campus in Kentucky to AI company Anthropic, a contract that could generate $19 billion in revenue. This partnership signifies a major strategic pivot for TeraWulf, leveraging its energy infrastructure for the power-hungry AI data center market, and shows Anthropic securing vast computing resources for future model development. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

**Microsoft Conducts Major Layoffs and Shifts AI Strategy to Cut Costs**
Microsoft has laid off nearly 5,000 employees, or about 2.1% of its workforce, with the Xbox and commercial sales divisions hit hardest, with AI being cited as a factor in the restructuring. Concurrently, the company is reportedly shifting to use more of its own smaller, in-house AI models instead of relying solely on partners like OpenAI, in a bid to control the soaring costs associated with running large-scale AI services. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Nokia Pivots to Supply AI Data Center Boom**
Veteran telecommunications company Nokia is successfully repositioning itself as a key supplier for the AI data center infrastructure market, leading to a roughly 90% surge in its stock price this year. The company is leveraging its expertise in high-speed optical networking to meet the massive demand for connectivity within and between data centers, marking a significant new chapter for the former handset giant. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

**Nvidia-Backed Nscale Secures $900 Million for Data Center Expansion**
Nscale, a UK-based AI infrastructure startup backed by Nvidia, has locked in $900 million in funding to accelerate its data center buildout across Europe, the U.S., and the Asia-Pacific region. This investment highlights the global race to build out the physical infrastructure required to power the AI revolution, with startups like Nscale aiming to provide flexible and scalable compute capacity. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

**Apple Commits $30 Billion to U.S.-Made Broadcom Chips**
Apple announced it will spend $30 billion on U.S.-made chips from Broadcom, including wireless components essential for its devices. This deal is part of Apple's broader commitment to domestic investment and secures a critical part of its supply chain, reflecting the strategic importance of semiconductor manufacturing in the current geopolitical and technological landscape. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

**Amazon to Stop Accepting New Customers for Mechanical Turk**
Amazon is halting new customer registrations for its Mechanical Turk (MTurk) crowdsourcing platform, a service that has been foundational for data labeling in machine learning for nearly two decades. This move signals a potential winding down of the platform, which has faced criticism for low pay, and may reflect a shift in the industry towards synthetic data and more advanced automated labeling techniques. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Key Personnel Departures and Moves Shake AI Landscape**
OpenAI's Chief Futurist, Joshua Achiam, who was with the company for nearly nine years and focused on AI safety, has announced his departure. In another move, former OpenAI product executive Kevin Weil has joined the board of reusable rocket company Stoke Space, indicating a cross-pollination of talent from the AI sector into other deep-tech industries. (Source: Feed: Artificial Intelligence Latest, AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

### New Models and Product Launches

**Tencent Releases Hy3, a Powerful 295B Open MoE Model**
Tencent has open-sourced Hy3, a massive 295-billion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model released under an Apache 2.0 license. The model is highly efficient, activating only 21B parameters per token, and features a 256K context window, making it well-suited for complex reasoning and long-context tasks while competing with other top-tier open models. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**NVIDIA Releases Audex, a Unified Audio-Text LLM**
NVIDIA has launched Nemotron-Labs-Audex-30B-A3B, a 30-billion-parameter model that unifies audio understanding, speech recognition, translation, and audio generation in a single framework. The model is significant for preserving the powerful text-based intelligence of its Nemotron backbone while adding comprehensive audio capabilities, pushing the boundaries of multimodal AI. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**OpenAI Launches Faster, More Capable Real-Time Voice Models**
OpenAI has released GPT-Realtime-2.1 and GPT-Realtime-2.1-mini to its API, designed for building low-latency voice agents. These new models offer improved reasoning and have achieved at least a 25% reduction in p95 latency, making conversational AI interactions faster and more natural for developers to implement. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Meta Launches Muse Image Generator Amidst User Data Concerns**
Meta has rolled out a new AI image generator, Muse Image, which is capable of creating images for advertising, creative projects, and other uses. The launch was met with immediate pushback from users and privacy advocates, as the model was trained on public Instagram photos, and users must proactively opt out to prevent their images from being used in this manner. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, Feed: Artificial Intelligence Latest)

**Anthropic's Claude Cowork Agent Expands to Mobile and Cloud**
Anthropic has updated its agentic assistant, Claude Cowork, allowing it to run tasks in the cloud, independent of a user's local machine. Now accessible via mobile and web, users can initiate a task on their desktop, monitor it on their phone, and retrieve the results later, even if their computer is off, marking a step towards more persistent and autonomous AI agents. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, Feed: Artificial Intelligence Latest, The New Stack)

**Sakana AI Debuts Multilingual Translation Tool**
Tokyo-based Sakana AI has launched Sakana Translate, a new tool powered by its Namazu model series that provides bidirectional translation between Japanese, English, and Chinese. The tool offers distinct "Translate," "Proofread," and "Ask" modes, showcasing the power of specialized, regional foundation models for nuanced linguistic tasks. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Ant Group Open-Sources 1B Vision Model for Spatial Perception**
Ant Group's Robbyant division has open-sourced LingBot-Vision, a family of 1-billion-parameter vision models trained for dense spatial perception. The model uses a novel "masked boundary modeling" technique and demonstrates that a smaller, specialized vision foundation model can match or exceed the performance of larger models on specific perception tasks. (Source: MarkTechPost)

### Open Source, Developer Tools, and Infrastructure

**Liquid AI Open-Sources "Antidoom" to Fix Repetitive Loops in LLMs**
Liquid AI has released an open-source method called Antidoom, which uses Final Token Preference Optimization (FTPO) to combat "doom loops"—a phenomenon where a model repetitively generates the same text. The tool significantly reduces these errors (e.g., from 22.9% to 1% in Qwen3.5-4B), improving the reliability of long-form generation for reasoning models. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**French Startup ZML Releases Free Tool to Speed Up AI Inference**
ZML, a French AI startup endorsed by Yann LeCun, has released ZML/LLMD, a free software product designed to optimize and accelerate AI inference across various types of chips. This could help lower the operational costs of running AI models, making the technology more accessible and efficient for a wider range of developers and companies. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Hugging Face Expands Ecosystem with Major Cloud Integrations**
Hugging Face announced several new integrations to streamline MLOps, including one-click deployment to Amazon SageMaker Studio and support for Microsoft Foundry Managed Compute. Additionally, a partnership with SkyPilot enables zero-egress storage, allowing users to run AI workloads on any cloud while keeping data stored on Hugging Face, reducing data transfer costs. (Source: Hugging Face - Blog)

**Synthetic Sciences Releases Open-Source AI Workbench for Research**
Synthetic Sciences has launched OpenScience, an open-source, model-agnostic AI workbench for scientific research in fields like biology, physics, and chemistry. The platform, which runs on a user's own infrastructure and API keys, comes with over 250 editable skills and access to scientific databases, aiming to accelerate AI-driven discovery. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**AWS Enhances DevOps Agent with AI-Powered Release Management**
Amazon Web Services has expanded its AWS DevOps Agent, introducing AI-powered release management capabilities. The agent can now autonomously assess code changes and test software before it reaches production, aiming to improve code quality and streamline the software development lifecycle for teams building on AWS. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

### AI Safety, Governance, and Societal Impact

**UN Secretary-General Calls for International Ban on "Killer Robots"**
United Nations Secretary-General António Guterres has forcefully called for a ban on lethal autonomous weapons systems (LAWS), labeling them "morally repugnant." This high-profile statement resurfaces the global debate over the ethics of AI in warfare and echoes the concerns that were central to Anthropic's recent clash with the Pentagon over military contracts. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

**China Issues Rules for AI Companions and Claims Vulnerabilities in US AI**
Beijing has drafted new regulations targeting AI companions, aiming to control the development of AI agents designed to form personal relationships with users. Separately, China's national vulnerability database claimed to have found security flaws in Anthropic’s Claude Code, alleging it could send sensitive user data to remote servers without consent, a claim that adds to the tech tensions between the two nations. (Source: AI News, Technology - WSJ.com)

**Privacy Concerns Mount as Google and Meta Use Public Data for AI Training**
Recent changes to Google's privacy policy confirm that user data from its services is being used to train its AI models, prompting guides on how to opt out. This follows backlash against Meta for using public Instagram photos to train its Muse image generator, highlighting a growing tension between AI companies' need for data and user expectations of privacy. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, Feed: Artificial Intelligence Latest)

**Discord Admits AI Moderation Bug Led to Wrongful Bans**
Discord confirmed that a bug in its AI-based content moderation system has been incorrectly banning users for posting harmless images since May. The incident, which saw an additional 200 users banned over a single weekend before being fixed, highlights the risks and challenges of relying on automated systems for sensitive tasks like content moderation and enforcement. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Analysis Reveals Human Role in "First AI-Run Ransomware Attack"**
A widely reported "AI-run" ransomware attack was not fully autonomous, as new details show a human operator was still required to select the victim, set up infrastructure, and supply stolen credentials. While an AI agent executed the technical parts of the attack, the event underscores that fully autonomous cybercrime is not yet a reality, though it demonstrates a dangerous new potential for tool-chaining. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Researchers Warn LLMs Can Be Weaponized to Create Botnets**
Security researchers have demonstrated a technique called "HalluSquatting," which exploits the tendency of large language models to "hallucinate" and invent plausible-sounding but non-existent software packages. Attackers can register these package names, and when developers mistakenly use them based on AI suggestions, it can be used to assemble massive botnets, revealing a novel attack vector. (Source: Biz &amp; IT - Ars Technica)

### AI in Science, Defense, and Enterprise

**AI-Discovered Drug for Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis Enters Phase III Trials**
Insilico Medicine is advancing a drug candidate for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), which was discovered using its AI platform, into late-stage Phase III human trials. This marks a major milestone for the computational drug discovery field, as it provides a critical test case for an AI-generated therapeutic moving beyond early safety checks to large-scale efficacy validation. (Source: AI News)

**Pharma Giants Engage in Multi-Billion Dollar Deals to Bolster Pipelines**
The pharmaceutical industry is seeing a wave of major acquisitions and partnerships, many aimed at incorporating advanced data and AI capabilities. Notable deals include Vertex Pharmaceuticals acquiring Crinetics for $10 billion, Novartis buying Myricx Bio for up to $1.5 billion, and AstraZeneca striking a $1.9 billion licensing deal with Sino Biopharm, all aimed at strengthening drug development pipelines. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

**US-Made Autonomous Ground Vehicles Deployed in Ukraine**
Forterra, a U.S. autonomy company, has deployed over 100 of its self-driving all-terrain vehicles in conflict zones in Ukraine. This represents one of the first major deployments of American autonomous ground vehicles in an active warzone, providing critical data on their effectiveness for logistics and other battlefield tasks. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Consumer Goods Giants Use AI to Accelerate Product Development**
Companies like L’Oreal, Mondelez, and Nestle are increasingly using AI in their research labs to shorten product development cycles and discover new applications for existing ingredients. For example, L'Oreal uses AI to predict how molecules will interact, allowing the company to innovate faster and bring new cosmetic products to market more quickly. (Source: AI News)

**Savi Raises $7M to Protect Consumers from AI-Powered Scams**
A new startup named Savi has raised $7 million in seed funding and launched an app to protect consumers from sophisticated AI scams, such as voice-cloned kidnapping calls. The app is designed to detect and flag realistic-looking deepfakes and other AI-generated fraudulent content, addressing a growing public safety concern. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)
