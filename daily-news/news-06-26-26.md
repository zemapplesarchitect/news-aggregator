## AI News Digest

### Corporate Strategy, Funding &amp; Acquisitions

**Qualcomm Acquires Modular for Nearly $4 Billion**
Qualcomm has acquired Modular, a prominent AI software startup, for nearly $4 billion, marking a significant move to bolster its software capabilities in the AI era. The acquisition aims to integrate Modular's technology, which optimizes AI model performance across different hardware, into Qualcomm's ecosystem to better compete in the AI chip market. (Source: Wired)

**Adobe Acquires Image and Video Enhancement Tool Maker Topaz Labs**
Adobe has acquired Topaz Labs, a company known for its AI-powered image and video enhancement software. This strategic purchase will allow Adobe to integrate Topaz Labs' advanced upscaling, sharpening, and noise reduction tools directly into its Creative Cloud suite, enhancing its AI-driven features for creative professionals. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Agility Robotics to Go Public via $2.5B SPAC Deal**
Humanoid robotics startup Agility Robotics announced plans to go public through a merger with a special-purpose acquisition company (SPAC), valuing the company at $2.5 billion. The deal is expected to generate $620 million in proceeds, which will fund the scaling of its bipedal robots for logistics and warehouse automation. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Patronus AI Raises $50M to Stress-Test AI Agents**
Patronus AI, a startup founded by former Meta AI researchers, has secured $50 million in funding to expand its agent evaluation platform. The company builds "digital worlds" to rigorously test the performance, safety, and reliability of AI agents, addressing a critical need for enterprises deploying these autonomous systems. (Source: TechCrunch)

**General Intuition Raises $320M at $2.3B Valuation to Train AI with Video Games**
AI startup General Intuition has raised $320 million, betting that training AI agents on millions of hours of video gameplay can instill a form of real-world intuition. The company believes that action-oriented data from games is key to developing more capable and adaptable AI systems for real-world tasks. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Amazon Commits an Additional $13B to AI Infrastructure in India**
Amazon is investing another $13 billion into its cloud and AI infrastructure in India, bringing its total planned investment in the country to over $25 billion. This move reflects the escalating race among global tech giants to build capacity and capture the rapidly growing AI market in India. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Google's AI Talent Drain Continues as Researchers Join Anthropic**
Top AI researchers Jonas Adler and Alexander Pritzel have left Google to join rival Anthropic, continuing a trend of high-profile departures from the tech giant. This talent migration highlights the intense competition for leading AI experts and underscores the challenges Google faces in retaining key personnel. (Source: TechCrunch, WSJ.com)

**DeepSeek AI to Double Workforce After Fresh Funding**
Chinese AI startup DeepSeek plans to double its workforce, hiring for numerous technical roles including AI product managers and development engineers. The expansion is fueled by a recent funding round and signals the company's ambition to accelerate growth and compete in the global AI landscape. (Source: WSJ.com)

**Oracle's AI Ambitions Fueled by Layoffs and Debt**
Oracle is undergoing significant restructuring, including laying off an estimated 21,000 employees, to help finance its massive investments in AI data center infrastructure. The company is spending billions to build out its cloud capacity to support the intense demand for AI workloads. (Source: Ars Technica)

**Cerebras Stock Plummets After Misunderstood Margin Outlook**
AI chipmaker Cerebras saw its stock plunge following its first earnings report as a public company. Investors were spooked by a forecast for a narrower gross margin, which the CEO later clarified was a misunderstanding of the company's long-term outlook. (Source: TechCrunch)

**BlackBerry Pivots to AI Opportunities in Embedded Software**
BlackBerry reported better-than-expected quarterly results, driven by growth in its embedded software business, particularly within the automotive sector. The company is increasingly focusing on AI-driven opportunities in IoT and cybersecurity, lifting its long-term financial outlook. (Source: WSJ.com)

### AI Hardware &amp; Infrastructure

**OpenAI Unveils 'Jalapeño' Custom Chip Built with Broadcom**
OpenAI has revealed its first custom-designed AI chip, named Jalapeño, developed in partnership with Broadcom. This application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) is optimized for OpenAI's inference systems, marking a strategic effort to reduce reliance on third-party hardware and control escalating infrastructure costs. (Source: TechCrunch, AI News)

**Nvidia Launches DGX Spark, a $4,699 Mini-PC for Local AI**
Nvidia has introduced the DGX Spark, a compact desktop computer priced at $4,699 that features a data-center-grade Blackwell GPU. The machine is designed to bring high-performance local AI development and inference capabilities out of the cloud and onto the desks of individual developers and researchers. (Source: Towards AI - Medium)

**Memory Chip Boom Continues Amidst Surging AI Demand**
The demand for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) chips, crucial for AI processors, is driving a massive financial boom for manufacturers like Micron. The company reported a quadrupling of revenue to $41.45 billion and a profit surge to $28.2 billion, though the long-term threat of innovation and new workarounds looms. (Source: TechCrunch, WSJ.com)

**Databricks' Former AI Chief Aims to Cut AI Power Bills by 1,000x**
A new startup, Un-0, founded by Databricks' former AI chief, claims its technology can replicate conventional AI systems while reducing power consumption by a factor of 1,000. The company demonstrated an image-generation tool as a proof-of-concept for its highly efficient approach to AI computation. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Europe Pushes Back Against US-Led Chip War on China**
European leaders and companies are expressing resistance to Washington's efforts to further restrict sales of semiconductor technology to China. Concerns are growing that such policies could harm European firms like ASML and disrupt global supply chains without effectively halting China's technological progress. (Source: TechCrunch)

### New Models &amp; Open Source Releases

**DeepReinforce Releases Ornith-1.0 Open-Source Coding Models**
DeepReinforce has open-sourced Ornith-1.0, a family of powerful coding models built on Gemma and Qwen. A key innovation is that the model learns its own reinforcement learning "scaffold" instead of using a fixed one, with the flagship 397B model achieving a high score of 82.4 on the SWE-Bench Verified benchmark. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Baidu Open-Sources 'Unlimited OCR' Model for Long Documents**
Baidu has released Unlimited OCR, a 3B-parameter model designed for efficient parsing of long documents. It uses a novel "Reference Sliding Window Attention" (R-SWA) to keep the KV cache size constant, allowing it to process dozens of pages with flat memory and latency, outperforming existing OCR baselines. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Gradium Launches Real-Time Speech Translation Models**
Gradium has released `stt-translate` and `s2s-translate`, two new models for real-time speech-to-speech and speech-to-text translation across 20 language pairs. The models reportedly beat alternatives from OpenAI and Google on accuracy and latency by collapsing the typical three-stage pipeline into a more efficient two-stage process. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Apple Releases 'container', an Open-Source Tool for Apple Silicon**
Apple has open-sourced `container`, a Swift-based tool that enables running Linux containers as lightweight virtual machines on Apple Silicon. This provides developers with a native, efficient way to work with containerized Linux environments on macOS, simplifying cross-platform development workflows. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Google Introduces OpenRL for Self-Hosted LLM Fine-Tuning**
Google's GKE Labs has launched OpenRL, an open-source project that provides a self-hosted API for post-training and fine-tuning large language models. The tool is designed to run on standard Kubernetes clusters, giving organizations more control over their model customization pipelines. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**Hugging Face Debuts FFASR Leaderboard for Real-World ASR Benchmarking**
Hugging Face has introduced the FFASR Leaderboard to benchmark Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) models on real-world, challenging audio. This initiative aims to provide a more realistic measure of model performance beyond clean, academic datasets, helping developers choose the best ASR systems for practical applications. (Source: Hugging Face - Blog)

### AI Agents &amp; Development Frameworks

**Anthropic Embeds Claude as a Collaborative Agent in Slack**
Anthropic has launched a beta of its Claude Tag feature, allowing users to pull the Claude AI model directly into Slack channels by typing `@Claude`. This transforms the AI from a private chatbot into a collaborative team member that can be assigned tasks, summarize discussions, and participate in group workflows. (Source: AI News, The New Stack)

**Nous Research Adds '/learn' Command to Hermes Agent**
Nous Research has enhanced its Hermes Agent with a `/learn` command that allows the agent to create new skills automatically from conversations, documents, or URLs. This feature streamlines the process of expanding an agent's capabilities, as it can now learn and codify new workflows without manual programming. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Grab Builds 'Palana' Platform for Secure AI Agent Execution**
To manage the risks of autonomous AI, Grab's security team developed Palana, a Kubernetes-native platform for safely running agentic workloads. Palana isolates agents in secure namespaces and uses a proxy-mediated control plane to contain potential threats from unpredictable tool use or code execution. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**Dapr 1.18 Introduces Verifiable Execution for Trusted AI Agents**
The latest release of the Dapr framework, version 1.18, introduces Verifiable Execution, a feature that brings cryptographic trust and tamper-evident logs to AI agents and distributed workflows. This allows developers to prove the provenance and integrity of actions taken by autonomous systems. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**Cloudflare Ships Agent Skills for Zero Trust Deployments**
Cloudflare has released an open-source library of "agent skills" designed to automate the planning, deployment, and migration of Zero Trust network environments. These skills include logic for migrating from competing platforms like Zscaler and Palo Alto Networks, drastically reducing transition times for enterprises. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

After previous restrictions, Samsung Electronics is now providing broad access to ChatGPT Enterprise and Codex for its employees. The deployment covers all staff in Korea and its global Device eXperience (DX) division, aiming to boost productivity and innovation in both technical and non-technical roles. (Source: AI News)

This strategic shift indicates a belief that AI agents will become the primary interface for managing communications, rather than traditional email clients. (Source: Ars Technica)

**SAP Aligns Commerce Data for AI-Powered Personalization**
SAP is introducing new capabilities to unify fragmented commerce data, enabling enterprises to deliver more effective AI-driven personalization. The goal is to solve the common problem where siloed data prevents recommendation engines and other AI tools from anticipating customer needs accurately. (Source: AI News)

**Figma Enhances Platform with New AI Features, Code Layers, and Animation**
Design platform Figma has rolled out a major update that includes more AI features, a new "code layer" for developers, and support for motion and animations. The update also allows users to create custom AI-powered plugins, further integrating intelligent automation into the design-to-code workflow. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Enterprises Begin 'Token Rationing' to Control AI Costs**
Companies are reportedly scrambling to implement controls to prevent employees from exhausting AI budgets on small or trivial tasks. As the use of powerful models like GPT-4 becomes widespread, organizations are moving from an era of unlimited use ("tokenmaxxing") to one of careful rationing and cost management. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Pendo Appoints 23-Year-Old as Chief AI Officer**
Software firm Pendo has appointed a 23-year-old as its chief AI officer, a move the company's CEO says has been an advantage. The decision highlights a trend of empowering younger, digitally native talent to lead AI strategy and reflects the rapid pace of change in the field. (Source: WSJ.com)

### Regulation, Safety &amp; Societal Impact

**White House Asks OpenAI to Slow Rollout of New Model Over Safety Concerns**
The Trump administration has reportedly asked OpenAI to delay the public release of its next major model, purportedly GPT-5.6, due to safety concerns. In response, OpenAI is said to be planning a limited release to a select group of partners instead of a broad public launch. (Source: TechCrunch)

**AI Not Killing Engineering Jobs, Data Suggests They Are Most Resilient**
Contrary to widespread fears, new data from SignalFire suggests that software engineering jobs have been the most resilient to AI-related disruption. While AI is changing workflows, engineers are reportedly making up a larger share of new hires, indicating their continued and evolving importance. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Study Casts Doubt on AI's Ability for Stock-Market Timing**
New research suggests that while large language models may show initial success in stock-market trading, their performance does not consistently outperform the market over longer periods or in changing conditions. This finding challenges the hype around using generative AI for reliable financial market timing. (Source: WSJ.com)

**British Police's Crime-Prediction AI Yielded Untrustworthy Results**
A WIRED investigation into a predictive policing system used by a UK police force revealed that the sprawling AI-driven machine produced results that couldn't always be trusted. The report highlights the significant risks and ethical challenges of deploying predictive analytics in law enforcement. (Source: Wired)

**White House Accelerates Deadline for Post-Quantum Cryptography**
An executive order has drastically shortened the deadline for federal agencies to transition away from cryptography vulnerable to quantum computing attacks. The move underscores the growing national security risk posed by future quantum computers that could break current encryption standards. (Source: Ars Technica)

**MIT Forum Explores AI's Broad Societal Impacts**
Leading researchers at MIT's AI and Society Forum convened to examine the critical influence of artificial intelligence on employment and democracy. The discussions focused on navigating the profound societal shifts and ethical questions posed by the rapid advancement of AI technologies. (Source: MIT News - Artificial intelligence)

### Developer Tools &amp; Platforms

**DFlash Speculative Decoding Promises Up to 15x Higher Throughput**
Researchers from UC San Diego have developed DFlash, a new speculative decoding method that drafts entire blocks of tokens in parallel using a lightweight diffusion model. The technique reports up to a 6x lossless speedup on some models and is projected by NVIDIA to offer up to 15x higher throughput on its Blackwell GPUs. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**MIT Develops 'Murakkab' to Improve AI Agent Efficiency**
MIT researchers have created a new system called Murakkab that optimizes the design and deployment of the complex, multistep workflows that power AI applications. The system aims to significantly improve the speed and energy efficiency of AI agents by streamlining how they execute tasks. (Source: MIT News - Artificial intelligence)

**GitLab Addresses AI Code Governance Challenges**
In response to the flood of AI-generated code, GitLab has released new governance features to help teams manage and validate code they may not have written or fully understand. The tools aim to maintain quality and security standards as AI becomes more integrated into the software development lifecycle. (Source: The New Stack)

**'Code Should Be Regenerated, Not Maintained': The Case for Spec-Driven Development**
A growing movement, exemplified by tools like Codeplain, argues that AI-generated code should be treated as ephemeral and regenerated from specifications rather than manually maintained. This "spec-driven" approach aims to manage the high volume of code produced by AI and ensure it stays aligned with requirements. (Source: The New Stack)

**Hugging Face Simplifies vLLM Server Deployment**
Hugging Face now allows developers to deploy a vLLM (vicuna Large Language Model) inference server on its Jobs platform with a single command. This greatly simplifies the process of hosting and serving high-throughput LLMs for production applications. (Source: Hugging Face - Blog)
