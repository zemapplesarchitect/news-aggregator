## AI News Digest

### Corporate Strategy, Investment, and Legal Battles

**AWS Defends Dual Investments in OpenAI and Anthropic**
AWS CEO Adam Selipsky stated that the company's multi-billion dollar investments in competing AI labs Anthropic and OpenAI are not a conflict of interest. He explained that AWS has a long-standing culture of competing with its partners and that offering customers choice among top models is a core part of its strategy. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Anthropic Faces Legal and Regulatory Scrutiny**
A U.S. appeals court denied Anthropic's request to end a Pentagon blacklisting, leaving its ability to work with the U.S. military in limbo due to conflicting court rulings. The company's refusal to remove safety guardrails for military use has, however, made it an attractive partner for the UK government, which is courting the firm for a London expansion. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com, Wired, AI News)

**Anthropic Secures More Compute Amid Surging Demand**
Anthropic has expanded its compute deal with Google and Broadcom to gain more access to Google's TPUs. This move comes as the company's run-rate revenue reportedly surged to $30 billion, highlighting the intense demand for its AI models and the massive computational resources required to train and run them. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Meta Expands AI Cloud Deal with CoreWeave to $21 Billion**
Meta Platforms has significantly expanded its long-term agreement with AI cloud provider CoreWeave, committing to a deal worth approximately $21 billion through December 2032. This massive investment underscores Meta's deep commitment to building out its AI infrastructure to support its ambitious model development and product integrations. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

**Intel Joins Elon Musk’s Terafab Chip Project**
Intel has signed on to Elon Musk's "Terafab" project, joining SpaceX and Tesla in an effort to construct a new, large-scale semiconductor factory in Texas. While the full scope of Intel's involvement remains unclear, the partnership signals a major effort to bolster U.S. chip manufacturing capabilities, likely focused on AI hardware. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, Wired)

**Nvidia-Backed Data Center Builder Firmus Reaches $5.5B Valuation**
Firmus, an Asian AI data center provider backed by Nvidia, has achieved a $5.5 billion valuation after raising $1.35 billion in six months. The rapid growth highlights the intense investor appetite for companies building the foundational infrastructure needed to power the global AI boom. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Elon Musk Escalates Lawsuit Against OpenAI**
Elon Musk has amended his lawsuit against OpenAI, now asking that any potential damages be awarded to OpenAI's original nonprofit entity. The amended suit also seeks the removal of CEO Sam Altman from the nonprofit's board, intensifying the legal battle over the company's transition to a for-profit structure. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

**Databricks Co-Founder Wins ACM Award, Declares "AGI is Here Already"**
Matei Zaharia, co-founder of Databricks and creator of Apache Spark, has won the prestigious ACM Computing Prize. In accepting the honor, Zaharia controversially stated that Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) "is here already" but is simply misunderstood, arguing that current systems already exhibit general problem-solving capabilities. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

### New AI Models and Major Research

**Meta Launches Muse Spark to Compete with Top-Tier Models**
Meta has announced Muse Spark, its first major model release following an expensive, year-long overhaul of its AI division. The new model's benchmarks suggest formidable performance, positioning it as a serious competitor to offerings from OpenAI and Anthropic and marking a critical test of the company's renewed AI ambitions. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com, Wired)

**Anthropic Previews Powerful ‘Mythos’ Model for Cybersecurity**
Anthropic has debuted a preview of its new, powerful AI model, codenamed Mythos, as part of a new cybersecurity initiative. The model, which was previously revealed through a website misconfiguration, will be used by a select group of partners to explore defensive cybersecurity applications. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial intelligence | TechCrunch, The New Stack)

**Z.AI Releases 754B Parameter Agentic Model GLM-5.1**
Z.AI has introduced GLM-5.1, a massive 754-billion parameter open-weight model specifically designed for agentic engineering tasks. The model is reported to achieve state-of-the-art performance on the SWE-Bench Pro coding benchmark and can sustain autonomous execution for up to eight hours. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Meta AI Releases Compact EUPE Vision Encoders**
Meta AI has released EUPE (Efficient Universal Pyramid Encoder), a family of compact vision models with under 100 million parameters. Designed for on-device and edge applications, these models rival the performance of larger, specialized models across a range of tasks, including image understanding and dense prediction. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Google Research Unveils PaperOrchestra for Automated Paper Writing**
Google AI researchers have introduced PaperOrchestra, a multi-agent framework designed to automate the process of writing AI research papers. The system coordinates specialized agents to handle tasks like drafting sections, formatting citations, and generating tables from experimental results, aiming to streamline the difficult manuscript creation process. (Source: MarkTechPost)

To address the complexity of building enterprise-grade AI agents, Anthropic has launched "Claude Managed Agents." Atlassian is enhancing its Confluence platform with new AI capabilities, including visual AI tools for creating assets directly within the software. A new startup named Poke is working to bring AI agents to a broader audience by making them accessible through simple text messages. **OSGym Framework Enables Large-Scale, Low-Cost Agent Research**
Researchers have introduced OSGym, a new infrastructure framework designed to manage over 1,000 parallel operating system replicas for just $0.23 per day. This system addresses a major bottleneck in AI agent research by providing a scalable and affordable environment for training agents to perform complex computer tasks. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Google Open-Sources Scion for Multi-Agent Orchestration**
Google has open-sourced Scion, an experimental testbed for orchestrating multiple AI agents. Scion is designed to manage concurrent agents running in containers across both local and remote compute, providing them with isolated identities and shared workspaces for complex, collaborative tasks. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**Astropad Reimagines Remote Desktop for AI Agents**
Astropad has launched Workbench, a remote desktop tool specifically designed for monitoring and controlling AI agents, not for traditional IT support. The application allows users to manage agents running on Mac Minis from an iPhone or iPad, providing low-latency streaming and mobile access for agentic workflows. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

## FINANCEANDTECHNOLOGY News Digest

### Global Markets &amp; Geopolitics: The U.S.-Iran Cease-Fire

**Markets React Volatilly to Fragile U.S.-Iran Cease-Fire**
Global markets experienced sharp swings following the announcement of a U.S.-Iran cease-fire. The Dow initially surged 2.8% in its largest gain since last spring, and oil prices plunged on hopes of de-escalation. However, the optimism proved short-lived as the truce appeared fragile, leading oil prices to rebound and equities to fall as investors adopted a more cautious stance. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business)

**Strait of Hormuz Remains a Critical Choke Point**
Despite the cease-fire, commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, a vital channel for global energy supplies, remains severely restricted. Shipowners, insurers, and energy companies are wary of the truce's stability, causing marine traffic to drop and preventing a quick restoration of oil and natural gas production and transport. This bottleneck is a key factor in ongoing market uncertainty and sustained high energy prices. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Markets)

**Energy Sector Outlook Shifts Amid Geopolitical Tensions**
The conflict has prompted a fundamental re-evaluation of the energy sector, with analysts suggesting energy stocks could see sustained interest after years of being shunned by investors. The OECD warned that the war's economic impact will depend heavily on the extent of damage to Middle East energy infrastructure. Reflecting the continued supply risk, Goldman Sachs forecast that crude oil could reach $115 per barrel by the end of the year. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, MarketWatch.com - Top Stories, WSJ.com: Economy)

**Economic Fallout to Linger for Consumers**
Economists caution that the war's economic consequences will not be undone overnight, even if the cease-fire holds. Americans, already concerned about the cost of living, should not expect a rapid decline in gas prices, which tend to fall much more slowly than they rise. The conflict has also created ripple effects that could impact food prices and other consumer goods. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business)

### Economic Outlook &amp; Central Banks

**Federal Reserve Remains Cautious on Rate Cuts Amid New Pressures**
Minutes from the Federal Reserve's March meeting reveal that officials are in no rush to cut interest rates, with the Iran conflict adding a new layer of complexity to pre-existing inflation concerns. The energy shock from the war has scrambled the economic outlook, with some Fed officials now open to the possibility of further rate increases to combat persistent inflation. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Economy)

**U.S. Economic Indicators Present a Mixed Picture**
The U.S. services sector faced its highest inflation pressures in four years during March, largely driven by the surge in energy prices. In contrast, Federal Reserve Governor Philip Jefferson noted that employment data suggests the labor market may be stabilizing after weakening in 2025. Meanwhile, job openings for blue-collar trades have plateaued, narrowing options for some workers. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy, NYT &gt; Business)

**Global Central Banks Hold Steady Amid Uncertainty**
Central banks around the world are navigating the uncertain economic environment by maintaining a cautious stance. The Reserve Bank of India held its key interest rates, citing the fluctuating outlook due to the Middle East war. Similarly, New Zealand's central bank governor affirmed a vigilant approach to getting inflation back to its target range. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon Highlights Key Risks for 2026**
In his annual letter to shareholders, JPMorgan Chase CEO Jamie Dimon outlined a range of significant risks that could impact the global economy and world affairs in 2026. His concerns reflect a broader anxiety among financial leaders about geopolitical instability, persistent inflation, and other potential economic shocks. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets)
