## AI News Digest

### Major Model &amp; Product Releases

**Google Unleashes Gemma 4, a Powerful Open Source Multimodal Family**
Google has released Gemma 4, a new family of four open-weight models under an Apache 2.0 license. These models are natively multimodal (text and audio), feature a 256k context window, and are designed for on-device deployment, representing a significant move to compete in the open-source AI space and empower developers to build local, powerful AI agents. (Source: Hugging Face - Blog, Towards AI - Medium)

**Microsoft's New AI Group Releases Three Foundational Models**
Just six months after its formation, Microsoft's MAI group has released three new foundational models. The new models are designed for transcribing voice to text, generating audio, and creating images, signaling Microsoft's intent to compete directly with other major AI labs by developing its own core model technologies. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Google Lowers Barrier to AI Video with Veo 3.1 Lite and Enhanced Vids App**
Google AI has launched Veo 3.1 Lite, a new, more affordable tier for its generative video model available through the Gemini API, aimed at making production-scale video generation more accessible for developers. Concurrently, the Google Vids application has been updated to allow users to direct and customize AI-generated avatars using prompts. (Source: MarkTechPost, AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**TII Releases Falcon Perception for Advanced Vision-Language Tasks**
The Technology Innovation Institute (TII) has launched Falcon Perception, a 0.6B-parameter model that uses an early-fusion transformer architecture. This model is designed for open-vocabulary grounding and segmentation from natural language, challenging the traditional modular approach to computer vision by more tightly integrating vision and language processing. (Source: MarkTechPost, Hugging Face - Blog)

**IBM Targets Enterprise Documents with Granite 4.0 3B Vision**
IBM has released Granite 4.0 3B Vision, a vision-language model specifically engineered for enterprise-grade document data extraction. Rather than a monolithic model, it functions as a specialized adapter for the Granite 4.0 language backbone, bringing high-fidelity visual reasoning to complex business documents. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Arcee AI Launches Trinity Large Thinking for Open Reasoning**
Arcee AI has released Trinity Large Thinking, an open-weight reasoning model under the Apache 2.0 license. The model is designed to handle complex, multi-step reasoning, long-horizon tasks, and tool use, providing a transparent, open-source alternative to proprietary reasoning models for building advanced AI agents. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Salesforce Overhauls Slack with 30 New AI-Powered Features**
Salesforce has announced a major update for Slack, introducing 30 new features heavily integrated with AI. These enhancements aim to make the collaboration platform more useful by automating tasks, summarizing conversations, and improving overall productivity within the enterprise. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Google Optimizes Gemini API with New Cost-Control Options**
Google has introduced 'Flex' and 'Priority' inference options for its Gemini API, giving developers new ways to balance cost and reliability. This allows for more granular control over API usage, enabling developers to choose lower-cost, variable-speed options for non-critical tasks or pay for prioritized, stable performance. (Source: AI)

### AI Governance, Security, and Ethics

**Anthropic's Week of Errors: Source Code Leak and Botched Takedown**
Anthropic experienced a difficult week, beginning with an accidental leak of its source code and information about unreleased models. The company's attempt to contain the leak resulted in an overly aggressive and erroneous DMCA takedown campaign on GitHub, which it later had to retract, highlighting the challenges of managing proprietary AI development. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, The New Stack)

**Major Supply Chain Attack Hits LiteLLM Project on PyPI**
A compromised version of the popular LiteLLM library was downloaded over 40,000 times from the PyPI repository. The malicious payload was designed to harvest and exfiltrate sensitive information, such as API keys, affecting downstream users like the AI recruiting startup Mercor and underscoring the vulnerability of the open-source software supply chain. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering, AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**New "Rowhammer" Attacks Grant Full Control Over Systems with Nvidia GPUs**
Security researchers have developed new Rowhammer-style attacks, named GDDRHammer and GeForge, that target the GPU memory in systems with Nvidia cards. These attacks can flip bits in memory to gain complete control over the machine's CPU, demonstrating a critical new hardware vulnerability vector. (Source: Biz &amp; IT - Ars Technica)

**KiloClaw Launches to Govern "Shadow AI" in the Enterprise**
A new tool, KiloClaw, has been released to help businesses manage and enforce governance over autonomous agents. The platform addresses the growing problem of "shadow AI," where employees deploy agents on personal infrastructure, bypassing official procurement and creating security and compliance risks. (Source: AI News)

**China Details Ambitious AI Deployment Targets in 15th Five-Year Plan**
China has approved its latest Five-Year Plan, which sets significant national priorities for AI deployment through 2030. The plan groups AI with quantum computing, biotechnology, and energy as strategic technologies, indicating a strong state-led push to advance its capabilities and integration across the economy. (Source: AI News)

**MIT Researchers Develop Framework for Evaluating AI Ethics**
A new testing framework from MIT aims to pinpoint situations where AI decision-support systems exhibit unfairness towards individuals or communities. This tool provides a systematic way to audit autonomous systems for ethical compliance and ensure they are treating people equitably. (Source: MIT News - Artificial intelligence)

**Experian Report Reveals "Fraud Paradox" in Financial Services AI Adoption**
A new forecast from Experian highlights a central tension in the financial services industry: the same AI technologies being deployed to detect fraud are also being weaponized by criminals to create more sophisticated attacks. This paradox complicates risk management and requires a multi-layered defense strategy. (Source: AI News)

**GitHub Deploys AI to Automate Accessibility Feedback Workflow**
GitHub is now using an AI-powered workflow to manage accessibility feedback at scale. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

### Advanced Research &amp; Future Concepts

Two independent research groups have published findings that significantly lower the estimated resources required for a quantum computer to break common online security. The advances reduce the number of qubits and the time needed to crack elliptic-curve cryptosystems, bringing the "Q-Day" threat closer than previously thought. (Source: Quanta Magazine, Biz &amp; IT - Ars Technica)

**Anthropic Research Suggests Claude Model Contains "Functional Emotions"**
Researchers at Anthropic published a paper claiming to have found internal representations inside their Claude model that perform functions analogous to human feelings. (Source: Feed: Artificial Intelligence Latest)

## FINANCEANDTECHNOLOGY News Digest

### US Economic Indicators &amp; Federal Reserve Policy

**Strong March Jobs Report Surprises Markets, Complicates Fed's Path**
The U.S. economy added a much stronger-than-expected 178,000 jobs in March, signaling a rebound from a previous soft patch and suggesting the labor market remains healthy. This robust growth, largely driven by the healthcare sector, has allowed the Federal Reserve to maintain its focus on combating inflation. The news prompted a rise in Treasury bond yields and a strengthening of the U.S. dollar, as traders adjusted expectations for future interest rate decisions. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Economy, MarketWatch.com - Top Stories)

**Beneath the Surface, Labor Market Shows Signs of Strain**
Despite the strong headline job number, some economists point to underlying weaknesses, describing the current environment as a "low-hire, low-fire" market where job-seekers still face challenges. Critics note that while payrolls expanded, finding employment remains difficult for many, and the labor market is in a precarious balance due to factors like lower immigration. Furthermore, the S&amp;P Global services PMI showed its first contraction in over three years, falling to 49.8 in March, as rising energy prices eroded business confidence. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, MarketWatch.com - Top Stories, WSJ.com: Economy)

### Geopolitical Tensions and Market Volatility

**Iran Conflict Roils Global Markets, Spiking Oil and Fuel Prices**
Geopolitical tensions surrounding a conflict with Iran have sent shockwaves through financial markets, causing oil prices to surge and stocks to fall. President Trump's signals of further military strikes created significant uncertainty, leading investors to pull back from equities after a brief rally. The conflict's impact is being felt directly by consumers through gas prices hitting $4 a gallon and by industries like airlines, with United and JetBlue raising bag fees to offset soaring fuel costs. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business, MarketWatch.com - Top Stories)

**US Economy Faces Inflationary Pressure from Mideast Conflict**
While the U.S. is more insulated from energy shocks than in the past, the ongoing conflict in Iran is creating significant economic headwinds, including rising mortgage rates and inflationary pressures. The average 30-year mortgage rate climbed for a fifth consecutive week to 6.46%, weighing on the housing market. Economists are closely watching for the broader impact on growth and inflation, as other nations look for ways to pressure the U.S. economy in response to its global aggression. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Economy)

### Private Credit Under Pressure

**Investors Rush for Exits from Private Credit, Sparking Liquidity Concerns**
The once-booming private credit market is facing a crisis of confidence as wealthy investors are rushing to withdraw their funds. In the first quarter, investors requested to pull nearly $14 billion from a group of private-credit funds, with Blue Owl being a notable example. The firm received withdrawal requests totaling $5.4 billion from two of its funds, forcing it to limit redemptions and raising questions about the true value of these illiquid assets and the potential losses for banks that lend to these funds. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business)

**Trump Proposes Major Budget Shift Towards Military Spending**
The Trump administration is preparing a new budget that proposes a historic increase in defense spending, requesting $1.5 trillion for the military. This sharp increase would be partially offset by significant cuts to domestic programs and social services, clarifying the administration's priorities amid the Iran conflict. The proposal, which includes a total spending hike of $1 trillion for 2025, is expected to face a tough fight in Congress. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, MarketWatch.com - Top Stories)

**Trump Administration Revamps Tariffs and Immigration Policies**
Alongside the budget, the administration has revamped tariffs on metals and pharmaceuticals and continues its crackdown on the H-1B visa program for skilled foreign workers. The new and adjusted levies target industries influential to the president's trade policy. The tightening of visa rules is causing foreign professionals to reconsider their careers in the U.S., potentially impacting the tech industry's talent pool. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)
