## AI News Digest

### Regulation, Safety, and Legal Battles

**Anthropic Disables Top AI Models Under Government Order**
Anthropic has disabled its newly launched, high-performance Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models following a US government export control directive. The order cited national security concerns and a potential "jailbreak" vulnerability that could bypass safety features. Anthropic stated its disagreement with the finding but complied, while all other models, including Opus 4.8, remain operational. (Source: MarkTechPost, TechCrunch, Wired, The New Stack, WSJ.com)

**Court Rules Google Liable for False AI Overview Statements**
A court has ruled that Google can be held legally liable for false and damaging statements generated by its AI Overviews feature. The decision establishes that a company that designs, trains, and manages an AI system must assume legal responsibility for the output it generates, setting a significant precedent for the accountability of generative AI providers. (Source: Wired)

**OpenAI Faces Investigation by Coalition of State Attorneys General**
OpenAI is under investigation by a group of state attorneys general over its activities and impact on users. The company has been served with a subpoena requesting a wide range of documents, signaling increasing regulatory scrutiny from state-level authorities on the practices of leading AI labs. (Source: WSJ.com)

**xAI Sued by Former Engineer Over Grok Safety Concerns**
A former engineer has filed a lawsuit against xAI and SpaceX, alleging wrongful termination. The engineer claims he was fired for raising critical AI safety concerns about the Grok model just days before SpaceX's major IPO, highlighting ongoing tensions between rapid development and safety protocols within the industry. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Google Sues AI-Powered Cybercrime Operation**
Google has filed a lawsuit against a Chinese cybercrime group dubbed "Outsider Enterprise" for using AI to perpetrate large-scale scams. The group is accused of sending 2.5 million fraudulent text messages over two weeks, leading to estimated losses of $1.9 billion and demonstrating how AI is turbocharging malicious activities. (Source: TechCrunch, WSJ.com)

**Grok Continues to Host Sexualized Deepfakes**
An investigation found that xAI's Grok platform is still hosting dozens of nonconsensual, sexualized deepfake images and videos of famous women, including celebrities and a prominent US politician. This occurs despite previous reports, raising serious questions about the platform's content moderation and safety enforcement. (Source: Wired)

### New Models and Architectures

**Google Releases Gemini-SQL2 for Advanced Text-to-SQL**
Google Research has announced Gemini-SQL2, a new text-to-SQL capability powered by the Gemini 3.1 Pro model. The system achieved a state-of-the-art score of 80.04% execution accuracy on the BIRD single-model leaderboard, demonstrating significant progress in natural language database querying. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Moonshot AI Open-Sources Kimi K2.7-Code**
Chinese AI firm Moonshot AI has released Kimi K2.7-Code, an open-source, coding-focused model with a 256K context window. The model reports a 21.8% performance increase on the Kimi Code Bench v2 benchmark over its predecessor and is available under a Modified MIT license via the Kimi API. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Zyphra Launches Zamba2-VL Hybrid Models for Faster Inference**
Zyphra has released Zamba2-VL, a family of open-source vision-language models (VLMs) at 1.2B, 2.7B, and 7B parameters. These models use a hybrid Mamba2 state-space and Transformer architecture, which reportedly cuts time-to-first-token by an order of magnitude while remaining competitive with pure Transformer models. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**NVIDIA Unveils 550B Nemotron 3 Ultra for Agentic AI**
NVIDIA has introduced Nemotron 3 Ultra, a 550-billion parameter open-weight model specifically designed for building AI agents. Unlike models optimized purely for benchmarks, Nemotron 3 Ultra is built to perform well in production environments, focusing on practical application and agentic workflows. (Source: Towards AI - Medium)

**OpenAI's Frontier Models Arrive on Amazon Bedrock**
OpenAI's powerful models, including GPT-5.5, GPT-5.4, and the coding model Codex, are now generally available on Amazon Bedrock. This marks a significant expansion of access beyond OpenAI's exclusive Azure arrangement, with GPT-5.4 becoming the first OpenAI model available in AWS GovCloud. (Source: InfoQ)

Moonshot AI has launched Kimi Work, a local desktop agent for macOS and Windows. The application reportedly runs on the Kimi K2.6 model and utilizes a swarm of 300 sub-agents to perform tasks, drive the user's browser, and schedule background jobs, bringing powerful agentic capabilities to the local machine. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Stack Overflow Launches Platform for AI Agents**
To address the challenge of AI agents sourcing unreliable information, Stack Overflow has created a dedicated platform for them. **Google Advances Agentic Web Interaction with WebMCP Trial**
A new standard proposal, WebMCP (Model Context Protocol), is now available in origin trials for Google Chrome. This standard allows websites to securely expose tools and functions to in-browser AI agents, enabling more reliable and efficient web automation compared to screen-reading or DOM-scraping methods. (Source: InfoQ)

**Google Releases Colab CLI for Terminal-Based Interaction**
Google has launched the Google Colab CLI, a command-line tool that enables developers and AI agents to interact with remote Colab runtimes directly from a local terminal. This facilitates automation and integration of Colab's powerful computing environments into broader development workflows. (Source: InfoQ)

**Angular Team Releases "Agent Skills" to Improve AI Coding**
The Google Angular team has released "angular/skills," a repository of "Agent Skills" designed to help AI coding tools write better, more modern Angular code. By providing AI agents with up-to-date context and conventions, the project aims to improve the quality and relevance of AI-generated code suggestions. (Source: InfoQ)

### Research and Technical Implementations

**MIT Researchers Improve Preference Prediction Models**
Researchers at MIT have developed a significant upgrade to random utility models, a nearly century-old concept for predicting choices. By considering the "power of three" (analyzing preferences in triplets), their new model offers more accurate predictions of people's preferences, with applications in economics, marketing, and policy. (Source: MIT News - Artificial intelligence)

**Pinecone Integrates with Microsoft OneLake for Enterprise Agents**
Vector database company Pinecone has announced an integration between its Nexus knowledge engine and Microsoft OneLake. This aims to change how enterprise AI agents access and reason over corporate data by bringing the agents directly to the data source, improving efficiency and security. (Source: InfoQ)

**Technical Tutorials Showcase AI in Urban Planning and Medical Imaging**
Recent technical articles demonstrate practical AI applications, including using Spatial Graph Neural Networks for urban function inference and leveraging the MONAI framework for 3D spleen segmentation in medical CT scans. These implementations highlight the growing use of advanced AI techniques in specialized fields. (Source: MarkTechPost)

### The SpaceX IPO Dominates Markets

**SpaceX Completes Record-Breaking IPO, Valuation Soars Past $2 Trillion**
SpaceX executed the largest Initial Public Offering in history, with its stock surging on its first day of trading. The blockbuster debut saw the company's valuation climb to as high as $2.1 trillion, with shares rising between 11% and 20% in a remarkably smooth market entrance. The successful IPO is seen as a major win for Elon Musk's vision and is expected to pave the way for a new wave of mega-IPOs from other tech giants like OpenAI and Anthropic. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business)

**Elon Musk Becomes World's First Trillionaire**
As a direct result of SpaceX's soaring stock price upon its public debut, CEO Elon Musk's net worth crossed the $1 trillion threshold, making him the world's first trillionaire. This milestone draws historical parallels to John D. Rockefeller, who became America's first billionaire in 1916. The event underscores the immense wealth creation of the IPO and has sparked broader conversations about wealth inequality, especially as worker wages face pressure from inflation. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Markets)

**Wall Street Celebrates as SpaceX Joins Mainstream Portfolios**
The financial industry played a key role in the IPO, with JPMorgan Chase hosting a lavish party featuring branded steaks and cocktails to celebrate the event. The IPO's success lifted the broader market, with the Dow, S&amp;P 500, and Nasdaq all posting gains. As a new heavyweight in public markets, SpaceX shares will soon be automatically included in many index funds, meaning millions of people will own a piece of the company through their 401(k) retirement accounts. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business)

**Investors Warn of Stock Bubble and Governance Risks**
Despite the rampant enthusiasm, some analysts are urging caution, warning that the mega-IPO frenzy could be a harbinger of a tech stock bubble, with valuations that "defy gravity." Concerns have also been raised about SpaceX's corporate structure, which appears to heavily favor Elon Musk at the expense of other shareholders. The IPO is considered a significant bet on Musk's vision, which presents both a massive opportunity and a considerable risk for investors. (Source: NYT &gt; Business)

### Global Economic Headwinds and Central Bank Responses

**Central Banks Raise Rates to Combat War-Driven Inflation**
Central banks worldwide are tightening monetary policy in response to a resurgence in inflation driven by the Middle East conflict and the disruption of the Strait of Hormuz. The European Central Bank became the first major bank to raise rates, the Bank of Japan is poised for a rate hike to a 31-year high, and the Bank of Korea has signaled its readiness to resume tightening. The U.S. Fed and Bank of England are expected to hold rates for now but leave the door open for future hikes. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**Global Growth Forecasts Dim Amid Geopolitical Tensions**
The World Bank has warned that global economic output could slow to just 1.3% growth if the war and the blockage of the Strait of Hormuz continue. The conflict is already impacting national economies, with the U.K.'s economy contracting by 0.1% in April and Germany's Bundesbank cutting its growth expectations while raising its inflation forecast to 2.9% for the year. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)

**US Consumer Sentiment Remains Sluggish**
In the U.S., consumer sentiment improved slightly in June but remains near historic lows, rising to 48.9 from 44.8 in May. Separately, analysis suggests that while overall consumer credit card debt is manageable, the slow growth in new lending could be a point of concern for the economy. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy, WSJ.com: Markets)
