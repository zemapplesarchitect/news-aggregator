## AI News Digest

### Major Investments, Partnerships, and Corporate Strategy

**SpaceX Inks $6 Billion Compute Deal with Open Source AI Lab**
SpaceX has entered a massive deal with Reflection AI, an open-source AI lab, to provide data center capacity. The deal is valued at approximately $6 billion through 2029, with Reflection AI paying $150 million per month for immediate access to Nvidia's latest GB300 AI chips at SpaceX's Colossus 2 data center in Tennessee. This provides the open-source lab with immense computational power to develop its models. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com, AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Google DeepMind Bets $75M on A24 for AI Filmmaking Tools**
Google DeepMind is investing approximately $75 million in film studio A24 as part of a research partnership to build AI-powered tools for Hollywood. The collaboration aims to explore the future of AI in filmmaking, leveraging A24's creative expertise and Google's technical prowess. This move signals growing interest from major tech companies in applying generative AI to the entertainment industry. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, Technology - WSJ.com)

**AI Chipmaker Groq Confirms $650M Funding Round**
Groq, known for its high-speed LPU inference chips, has officially confirmed a $650 million funding round. The company is re-staffing key executive roles and leaning into its "neocloud" business model after a previous "not-acqui-hire" deal with Nvidia. This significant capital injection will help Groq compete in the crowded AI hardware market. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Oracle Sheds 21,000 Jobs Amid Sharpened AI Focus**
Oracle's workforce shrank by 21,000 employees, or roughly 13%, in the last fiscal year as the company streamlines operations to focus on artificial intelligence. The job cuts reflect a broader industry trend of legacy tech giants reallocating resources toward high-growth AI initiatives and cloud infrastructure. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

**L’Oréal Partners with OpenAI to Bring Virtual Makeup Try-On to ChatGPT**
L’Oréal announced a collaboration with OpenAI to integrate Maybelline New York’s virtual makeup try-on feature directly into ChatGPT. The partnership, revealed at VivaTech 2026, also covers product discovery, advertising pilots, and internal use of AI for research and content production, demonstrating how generative AI is being applied to consumer retail experiences. (Source: AI News)

**Cursor Acquires Open-Source Coding Assistant Continue**
AI-native code editor Cursor has acquired Continue, an open-source alternative to GitHub Copilot. The acquisition signals ongoing consolidation in the AI developer tools market, with companies aiming to build more integrated and powerful coding assistants for software engineers. (Source: Artificial Intelligence News, Analysis and Resources - The New Stack)

**Microsoft CEO Calls for Checks on AI Power**
In a recent interview, Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella offered a critique of the current AI power balance, stating that the industry cannot let a few AI giants "eat the economy." He called for earning society's permission and ensuring that the benefits of AI are distributed broadly, highlighting the ongoing debate about competition and market concentration in the AI sector. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

**WSJ Investigates Sam Altman's Personal Investments and OpenAI Ties**
A Wall Street Journal report details how OpenAI CEO Sam Altman's personal investments in startups like Helion have benefited from deals or explorations with OpenAI. The article raises questions about potential conflicts of interest and the complex web of relationships between AI leaders and the companies they invest in. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

### New Models, Agentic AI, and Frameworks

**xAI Adds Autonomous Task Execution to Grok with '/goal'**
xAI has introduced a new `/goal` mode in Grok Build, enabling long-running, autonomous execution for complex tasks. The feature allows a user to provide a single objective, which the agent then breaks down into a plan, executes with a progress checklist, and verifies until the goal is complete, marking a step forward in autonomous agent capabilities. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Sakana AI Launches Fugu Orchestration Model**
Tokyo-based Sakana AI has released Fugu and Fugu Ultra, a new type of "orchestration model" designed to route tasks across a swappable pool of other frontier LLMs. This multi-agent approach aims to improve performance by selecting the best model for a given task and helps enterprises avoid vendor lock-in by not relying on a single monolithic model. (Source: MarkTechPost, AI News)

**Prime Intellect Releases Framework to Train Trillion-Parameter MoE Models**
Prime Intellect has open-sourced `prime-rl 0.6.0`, a framework for training trillion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models using reinforcement learning. The framework incorporates advanced optimizations like FP8 inference and 3-D parallelism, enabling the training of massive models on agentic workloads with high efficiency. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**The Rise of "Loopy" AI Systems**
The AI industry is seeing a trend towards "loopy" AI, where swarms of agents are authorized to work continuously and autonomously in the background. This concept moves beyond single-shot agentic tasks to create persistent systems that can endlessly iterate, learn, and perform complex workflows without constant human intervention. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Cisco AI Open-Sources FAPO for Automated Prompt Optimization**
Cisco's Foundation AI team has released FAPO (Fully Automated Prompt Optimization), a system that uses Anthropic's Claude to autonomously optimize multi-step LLM pipelines. FAPO evaluates a chain, attributes failures at a step level, and proposes and validates improvements, reportedly outperforming existing methods in Cisco's evaluations. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Linux Foundation to Launch Agent Name Service (ANS)**
The Linux Foundation announced its intent to launch the Agent Name Service (ANS), an open standard for AI agent identity based on the Domain Name System (DNS). The initiative aims to create a verifiable and interoperable way to identify and communicate with AI agents across different platforms. (Source: Artificial Intelligence News, Analysis and Resources - The New Stack)

**Anthropic's Claude Now Handles 95% of its Internal Analytics**
Anthropic reported that its own AI model, Claude, now successfully handles about 95% of its internal business analytics queries. The company credits this achievement not just to model improvements but to strong data governance, clear semantic definitions, and operational discipline, allowing employees to self-serve data insights. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

### Hardware, Chips, and Infrastructure

**AWS Announces General Availability of 192-Core Graviton5 CPU**
Amazon Web Services (AWS) has made its new Graviton5-powered EC2 instances generally available, featuring 192 ARM cores and DDR5-8800 memory. The new chip also includes a formally verified Nitro Isolation Engine for enhanced VM security and offers an estimated 15% better price-performance over Graviton4. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**MIT Researchers Develop Chip for Tiny Robot Navigation**
Researchers at MIT have developed a new chip that combines an efficient algorithm with dedicated hardware to help tiny, power-constrained robots navigate complex environments. The chip can rapidly generate 3D maps using minimal memory and power, a key step toward creating more autonomous micro-robots. (Source: MIT News - Artificial intelligence)

**AMD Reinstates Memory Encryption on Consumer CPUs After Outcry**
Following significant user backlash, AMD has reversed its decision and will reinstate full memory encryption features in its upcoming consumer CPUs. Critics had argued that removing the feature was an attempt to push customers toward more expensive enterprise-grade chips. (Source: Biz &amp; IT - Ars Technica)

**Nvidia's New Cooling System Addresses Only Part of AI's Water Problem**
Nvidia announced a new data center cooling system designed to reduce water usage within the facility. However, critics point out that this does not address AI's largest water footprint, which comes from the water used to cool the fossil fuel power plants that generate the immense electricity required for AI data centers. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial intelligence | TechCrunch)

**MoonMath AI Open-Sources High-Performance Kernel for AMD MI300X**
Startup MoonMath AI has released an open-source HIP attention kernel for AMD's MI300X accelerator that it claims outperforms AMD's own AITER v3 library. The release demonstrates how smaller players can create highly optimized, low-level software to extract more performance from AI hardware. (Source: MarkTechPost)
