## AI News Digest

### Corporate &amp; Financial Maneuvers

**Anthropic Confidentially Files for Public Offering**
AI safety and research company Anthropic has confidentially filed paperwork for an initial public offering (IPO), setting the stage for a major stock market debut. This move positions one of OpenAI's primary competitors to tap public markets for capital, reflecting the intense financial demands of large-scale AI development and the maturation of the generative AI sector. The filing follows a blockbuster year for IPOs and intensifies the race among top AI labs to secure long-term funding. (Source: Wired)

**Alphabet Seeks $80 Billion for Massive AI Expansion**
Alphabet, Google's parent company, announced plans to raise $80 billion to fund a significant expansion of its AI infrastructure. The capital will be used for data center construction and securing computing resources to meet what the company describes as overwhelming enterprise and consumer demand for its AI services. In a significant vote of confidence, Berkshire Hathaway is reportedly set to purchase a $10 billion stake in the offering. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

**SoftBank to Invest up to €75 Billion in French Data Centers**
SoftBank has committed to a massive investment of up to €75 billion to develop and operate new data center capacity in France. The initiative aims to add up to 5 gigawatts of capacity, signaling a major strategic push to build out the foundational infrastructure required for AI growth in Europe. This move underscores the global race to establish regional AI hubs with sufficient computing power. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Tech Giants Link Layoffs to AI Investment Shift**
An analysis suggests that major technology companies have laid off over 142,000 employees while simultaneously committing an estimated $725 billion to AI development. Companies like Meta and Cloudflare have conducted significant staff reductions despite strong financial performance, indicating a strategic reallocation of capital from human resources to AI infrastructure and research. Current labor laws do not require companies to disclose if AI was a factor in termination decisions. (Source: Towards AI - Medium)

**Nvidia CEO Predicts Marvell Could Join Trillion-Dollar Club**
Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang suggested that chip firm Marvell Technology could be the next company to reach a trillion-dollar valuation. Huang's prediction is based on the anticipated surge in demand for specialized AI hardware, driven by the emergence of more powerful and autonomous AI models. This highlights the expected continued boom in the semiconductor sector that powers the AI industry. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

### New Models and Open Source Contributions

**Alibaba Launches Multimodal Agent Qwen3.7-Plus**
Alibaba's Qwen team has released Qwen3.7-Plus, a powerful new multimodal agent model available on its Bailian platform. The model features advanced capabilities including vision for understanding images and video, deep reasoning, autonomous tool use, and self-programming. This release marks a significant step towards more capable and autonomous AI agents from one of China's leading tech firms. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**JetBrains Releases 12B Mixture-of-Experts Model Mellum2**
JetBrains has open-sourced Mellum2, a 12-billion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model released under the Apache 2.0 license. Trained on 10.6 trillion tokens, Mellum2 is designed for speed and specialization within multi-model AI pipelines, making it suitable for targeted tasks in complex workflows. The release provides developers with a powerful, efficient, and open alternative for building specialized AI applications. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**MiniMax Unveils M3 Model with 1M-Token Context and Native Multimodality**
Chinese AI startup MiniMax has launched its new M3 foundation model, which introduces a proprietary MiniMax Sparse Attention (MSA) architecture. This enables the model to support a massive 1-million-token context window, along with native multimodality for processing images and video, and advanced agentic coding capabilities. The large context window is a key feature for handling complex, long-form tasks. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**NVIDIA Open-Sources Cosmos 3 for Physical AI Reasoning**
NVIDIA has released Cosmos 3, its first open omni-model designed specifically for physical AI systems like robotics and autonomous vehicles. The model is engineered for reasoning and action in the physical world, integrating various data types to understand and interact with its environment. This release aims to accelerate the development of embodied AI that can perform complex real-world tasks. (Source: Hugging Face - Blog)

**Arm Open-Sources Metis, an AI Security Analysis Framework**
Arm has open-sourced Metis, an agentic AI framework for autonomously discovering complex software vulnerabilities. Unlike traditional static analysis (SAST) tools that rely on pattern matching, Metis uses semantic reasoning to analyze code and identify security flaws across different components. The framework provides its findings in clear natural language, aiming to outperform traditional security tools. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

### The Rise of AI Agents

**Anthropic's Claude Code Gains Dynamic Workflows for Parallel Agent Coordination**
Anthropic has enhanced Claude Code with "Dynamic Workflows," a new feature for managing complex software engineering tasks. This capability allows the AI to dynamically create orchestration scripts, decompose work into subtasks, run them in parallel, and validate the results. This represents a move towards more sophisticated multi-agent systems where a primary AI can manage a team of specialized AI agents. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**IBM Research Argues for Agent Logic in Enterprise AI**
A post from IBM Research emphasizes that scalable enterprise AI adoption depends on "agent logic" rather than just large language models (LLMs). The argument is that for AI to be truly effective in business, it needs to move beyond simple chat interfaces to become part of a structured, logical framework of autonomous agents that can execute complex, multi-step business processes reliably. (Source: Hugging Face - Blog)

**New Coding Agent from Skiplabs Ships Code Without Human Feedback**
Startup Skiplabs has developed a coding agent named Skipper that is designed to operate autonomously, shipping code without requiring iterative feedback from a human developer. This approach contrasts with most AI coding assistants that act as co-pilots, aiming instead for a fully automated workflow from task description to code deployment. The goal is to dramatically increase development speed by removing the human from the loop. (Source: The New Stack)

**Holo3.1 Introduced as a Fast, Local Agent for Computer Use**
A new agent model, Holo3.1, has been introduced, designed to operate locally on a user's computer to perform tasks. Being fast and local, it aims to provide a more private and responsive alternative to cloud-based agents for controlling applications and automating user workflows directly on a personal machine. (Source: Hugging Face - Blog)

### Safety, Security, and Regulation

**Florida Sues OpenAI and Sam Altman Over Alleged AI Harms**
In a first-of-its-kind lawsuit, the state of Florida is suing OpenAI and its CEO, Sam Altman, alleging the company's products are unsafe. The suit is partially connected to a 2025 mass shooting at Florida State University and ChatGPT's alleged role in the incident, marking a significant legal challenge that seeks to hold AI developers liable for harms facilitated by their models. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**"BadHost" Vulnerability Exposes AI Systems via Web Framework Flaw**
A high-severity authentication bypass vulnerability named "BadHost" was discovered in Starlette, a popular Python web framework with over 325 million weekly downloads. The flaw allows attackers to use malformed HTTP Host headers to bypass access controls, potentially exposing sensitive AI agent infrastructure, LLM gateways, and evaluation systems. This highlights the critical importance of security in the underlying software stack used for AI. (Source: InfoQ - AI, ML &amp; Data Engineering)

**Anthropic Expands Security Program to Protect Critical Infrastructure**
Anthropic is scaling its "Project Glasswing" security initiative, extending access to its powerful Mythos model to 150 organizations across 15 countries. The program specifically targets the protection of critical infrastructure sectors like power, water, and healthcare, where a successful cyberattack could impact millions of people. This represents a proactive use of advanced AI for defensive cybersecurity purposes. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**Study Finds Top AI Models from Google, OpenAI, and Others Fail on Key Attack Vector**
A study from Cisco found that leading AI models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Amazon, and xAI are all vulnerable to a specific type of attack. The research suggests that the safety benchmarks currently used by enterprises to evaluate models may be measuring the wrong things, leaving them exposed to risks that are not captured by standard testing procedures. (Source: The New Stack)

**Red Hat's Official NPM Channel Used to Distribute Backdoored Packages**
Dozens of software packages on the official NPM (Node Package Manager) channel for Red Hat were found to be backdoored. The security breach allowed malicious code to be distributed through a trusted enterprise channel, posing a significant supply chain risk. Users of the affected packages were urged to investigate their systems immediately for compromise. (Source: Biz &amp; IT - Ars Technica)

**Trump Administration Divided on AI Regulation**
The Trump administration is reportedly experiencing internal conflict regarding the regulation of artificial intelligence. President Trump recently canceled a planned executive order on AI just hours before it was to be signed. This has left administration officials and industry executives uncertain about the future of federal AI policy and whether any regulatory framework will be pursued. (Source: Feed: Artificial Intelligence Latest)
