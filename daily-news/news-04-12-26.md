## AI News Digest

### AI Safety, Governance, and Legal Challenges

**Anthropic Withholds "Too Dangerous" AI Model After It Finds Thousands of Cyber Flaws**
Anthropic announced it is not publicly releasing its new model, Claude Mythos Preview, after it autonomously discovered thousands of zero-day vulnerabilities in major software as part of "Project Glasswing." The model, which scored 83.1% on a key cybersecurity benchmark, found decades-old flaws in systems like OpenBSD and FFmpeg, prompting Anthropic to share it only with a select group of tech companies. This move has ignited a debate about responsible disclosure, corporate liability, and whether such powerful tools can ever be safely released. (Source: AI News, Towards AI - Medium, Wired, Technology - WSJ.com)

**OpenAI CEO Sam Altman's Home Attacked With Molotov Cocktail**
The San Francisco residence of OpenAI CEO Sam Altman was attacked with a Molotov cocktail, and a suspect was later taken into custody after allegedly making threats at the company's headquarters. Altman responded to the incident and a critical New Yorker profile in a blog post, highlighting the escalating real-world security threats faced by leaders in the AI industry and the intense public scrutiny surrounding them. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch, Wired, Technology - WSJ.com)

**OpenAI Faces Multiple Lawsuits and Investigations Over Alleged Misuse**
OpenAI is facing growing legal challenges, including a lawsuit from a stalking victim who alleges ChatGPT fueled her abuser's delusions and that the company ignored her warnings. Separately, the Florida Attorney General has launched an investigation into OpenAI's role in a university shooting that was allegedly planned using ChatGPT. These cases represent a new frontier of legal battles aiming to hold AI companies accountable for the harmful use of their products. (Source: AI News &amp; Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch)

**OpenAI Backs Bill to Limit Liability for AI-Caused Disasters**
OpenAI testified in support of an Illinois bill that would limit when AI companies can be held liable for "critical harm," such as mass deaths or financial disasters caused by their models. The move signals a proactive effort by major AI labs to shape legislation and shield themselves from potentially catastrophic legal responsibility as their models become more powerful and autonomous. (Source: Wired)

**White House Proactively Tackling Security Threats from Powerful AI**
A White House group, led by National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross, is working to identify and address security vulnerabilities in powerful AI models from companies like Anthropic and OpenAI before they are released to the public. This initiative reflects a shift in government strategy towards proactive risk mitigation in response to the rapidly advancing capabilities of frontier AI systems. (Source: Technology - WSJ.com)

**Agentic AI Poses Governance Challenge Under EU AI Act**
The autonomous nature of emerging AI agents, which can trigger actions and move data without direct human oversight, presents a significant governance problem for organizations. Experts warn that the lack of clear audit trails for agentic decisions could lead to non-compliance with regulations like the EU AI Act, which mandates traceability and transparency in AI systems. (Source: AI News)

**Researchers Uncover New "Tool Injection" Attack Vector for AI Agents**
A new study, "Les Dissonances," details a novel attack class called Cross-Tool Harvesting &amp; Polluting (XTHP), where an attacker publishes a malicious tool that an AI agent trusts and incorporates. This allows the attacker to hijack the agent, feed users false information, or exfiltrate data without needing to jailbreak the model or exploit a software vulnerability, making the user the primary victim. (Source: Towards AI - Medium)

### Technical Breakthroughs &amp; Research

**MIT Researchers Develop Method to Make AI Models "Leaner" During Training**
A new technique from MIT researchers uses control theory to dynamically prune unnecessary complexity from AI models while they are still being trained. This "in-flight" optimization method promises to reduce computational costs and training time without sacrificing the final performance of the model. (Source: MIT News - Artificial intelligence)

**TriAttention Method Promises 2.5x Higher Throughput for Long-Context LLMs**
Researchers from MIT, NVIDIA, and Zhejiang University have proposed TriAttention, a new KV cache compression method that significantly improves inference efficiency. The technique reportedly matches the performance of full attention while enabling 2.5 times higher throughput, addressing a major memory and speed bottleneck in long-context language models. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Recursive Language Models (RLMs) Emerge as Solution to "Context Rot"**
A growing body of research is focused on Recursive Language Models (RLMs) as a way to solve "context rot," the tendency of traditional LLMs to lose or misinterpret information in very long conversations or documents. By design, RLMs can maintain coherence and recall information more effectively over extended contexts, making them better suited for complex, multi-step reasoning tasks. (Source: Towards AI - Medium)

**Knowledge Distillation Enables Deployment of Complex Ensemble Models**
An analysis highlights the importance of Knowledge Distillation, a technique where the combined intelligence of a large, high-performing "teacher" ensemble of models is compressed into a single, smaller "student" model. This process makes it practical to deploy the accuracy benefits of complex ensembles in production environments where latency and operational costs are critical. (Source: MarkTechPost)

### Global Economy &amp; Geopolitical Tensions

**Markets Rally on Middle East Cease-Fire Hopes, But Headwinds Remain**
U.S. stock markets rallied strongly on optimism surrounding a fragile cease-fire in the Middle East and upcoming U.S.-Iran talks, with the S&amp;P 500 marking its seventh consecutive gain and the Dow Jones Industrial Average turning positive for 2026. However, diplomatic talks in Pakistan later ended without a deal, causing U.S. stock futures to slide and oil prices to climb, highlighting the market's sensitivity to geopolitical outcomes. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, MarketWatch.com - Top Stories)

**Iran Conflict Fuels Inflation and Squeezes Consumers**
The March Consumer Price Index (CPI) registered its highest reading since 2024 and the fastest annual rate in two years, largely driven by the war in Iran. The conflict caused a record monthly percentage jump in U.S. gasoline prices to over $4 a gallon, eroding wage gains and prompting companies like Delta, Amazon, and the U.S. Postal Service to raise their prices. (Source: WSJ.com: Markets, NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Economy)

**Oil Shock and Supply Chain Disruptions Threaten Global Economy**
Iran's control over the Strait of Hormuz is blocking significant oil flows from the Persian Gulf, creating a deeper supply shock than headline prices indicate. European airports have warned of a "systemic" jet fuel shortage within three weeks if the strait remains shut, while the conflict has strained Europe's economic relationships with the U.S., China, and Russia. (Source: NYT &gt; Business, WSJ.com: Economy)

**IMF and Central Banks Adopt Cautious Stance on Growth**
The International Monetary Fund's Managing Director, Kristalina Georgieva, stated that global economic growth is expected to slow, even if a durable peace is achieved in the Middle East. In response to the uncertainty, central banks like the Bank of Korea are holding interest rates steady as they assess the conflict's impact on GDP, inflation, and employment. (Source: WSJ.com: Economy)
