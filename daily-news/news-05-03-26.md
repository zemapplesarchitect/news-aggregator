## AI News Digest

### The AI Giants: Legal Battles, Strategic Shifts, and New Products

**Musk vs. OpenAI Trial Heats Up as xAI's Training Methods Are Questioned**
The ongoing lawsuit between Elon Musk and OpenAI has intensified, with Musk testifying for three days. During his testimony, Musk admitted that his company, xAI, used OpenAI's models to train its own Grok model, a practice he referred to as "distillation" and argued is standard industry practice. Court documents also revealed the significant role of Shivon Zilis, an executive at Musk's companies and mother to four of his children, who acted as a key intermediary between him and OpenAI's leadership. The core of Musk's lawsuit remains his claim that OpenAI betrayed its founding nonprofit mission by transitioning to a for-profit entity. (Source: TechCrunch, Wired)

**OpenAI Focuses on Security, IPO Prep, and Model Safety Amidst Scrutiny**
OpenAI is taking steps to mature its operations, announcing new advanced security protections for ChatGPT accounts, including a partnership with physical security key provider Yubico. The company is also reportedly preparing for a major IPO, with newly appointed CFO Sarah Friar tasked with readying the company for the public market. Concurrently, OpenAI is grappling with the challenges of its powerful technology, restricting access to its new cybersecurity tool, GPT-5.5 Cyber, and addressing reports of its models being used to plan violent attacks. The company also had to intervene when ChatGPT developed a strange "goblin obsession," a bug that arose from attempts to create a "nerdy" personality for the bot. (Source: TechCrunch, WSJ, Wired)

**Anthropic Nears Massive $900B+ Valuation and Launches Security Tool**
Anthropic is reportedly in the final stages of a new funding round that could value the company at over $900 billion, with investors asked to submit allocations within a 48-hour window. This massive valuation reflects the intense investor interest in leading AI labs. In a significant product move, Anthropic has also released Claude Security from its closed preview; the tool is designed to help developers scan codebases for vulnerabilities, positioning Claude as a key player in AI-powered cybersecurity. (Source: TechCrunch, The New Stack)

**Meta Acquires Robotics Startup and Pivots to Proprietary AI Models**
Meta is bolstering its ambitions in physical AI by acquiring the humanoid robotics startup Assured Robot Intelligence, aiming to enhance its AI models for real-world robots. In a major strategic shift, reports indicate Meta is abandoning its popular open-source Llama model family in favor of a new proprietary large language model named Muse Spark. To support its model development, Meta also introduced Autodata, an agentic framework that uses AI models as autonomous data scientists to generate high-quality training data. (Source: TechCrunch, The New Stack, MarkTechPost)

**Mistral AI Launches New Flagship Model and Cloud-Based Coding Agents**
Paris-based Mistral AI continues to challenge its US rivals with the launch of several new products. The company unveiled Mistral Medium 3.5, a new 128-billion-parameter flagship model, and introduced "Remote Agents" in its Le Chat interface. This new agentic "Work mode" allows for asynchronous, cloud-based coding sessions, marking a significant step forward for developers using AI agents and reinforcing Mistral's position as a leading European AI company. (Source: MarkTechPost, The New Stack)

### Enterprise and Government Adoption

**Pentagon Inks Deals with Tech Giants for Classified AI Work**
The U.S. Department of Defense has signed contracts with Nvidia, Microsoft, and AWS to deploy their AI technologies on classified government networks. This move is part of the Pentagon's strategy to diversify its AI vendors and reduce supply-chain risk. The decision comes after a dispute with Anthropic over the usage terms of its AI models, which the DOD had previously declared a potential risk. (Source: TechCrunch, WSJ)

**GitHub Copilot Abandons Flat-Rate for Per-Token Pricing**
In a major shift for AI-powered developer tools, GitHub has announced it will move its Copilot service from a flat-rate subscription to a per-token pricing model, effective June 1, 2026. This change means users will be charged based on their consumption of the AI model, which could have significant cost implications for heavy users and enterprise teams. The move reflects a broader trend in the industry towards consumption-based billing for AI services. (Source: AI News)

**Google's Gemini AI Assistant Rolls Out to Millions of Vehicles**
Google is pushing its advanced conversational AI, Gemini, into the automotive sector, with plans to integrate the assistant into millions of vehicles. This initiative aims to move beyond simple voice commands and provide a more sophisticated, conversational AI experience for drivers, including trip planning, vehicle control, and on-the-go information. The move signals a major push to make generative AI a core part of the in-car experience. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Legal AI Startup Legora Hits $5.6B Valuation Amidst Fierce Competition**
The legal AI sector is seeing explosive growth and intense competition, exemplified by startup Legora achieving a $5.6 billion valuation in its latest funding round. This development heats up its rivalry with fellow legal AI unicorn Harvey, as both companies have raised massive sums and are aggressively pushing into each other's territory with dueling ad campaigns. The high valuations underscore the perceived potential of AI to transform the legal industry. (Source: TechCrunch)

**Stripe Updates Digital Wallet for Use by Autonomous AI Agents**
Stripe has updated its digital wallet, Link, with features that allow autonomous AI agents to make purchases on behalf of users. The system enables users to connect their payment methods and then authorize AI agents to spend securely through predefined approval flows. This development provides a crucial piece of infrastructure for a future where AI agents can autonomously perform commercial tasks. (Source: TechCrunch)

**SAP Argues for Enterprise AI Governance to Secure Profit Margins**
SAP is emphasizing the importance of robust AI governance for businesses, arguing it is essential for securing profit margins. Manos Raptopoulos, a Global President at SAP, stated that enterprise-grade AI must provide deterministic control and reliable accuracy, contrasting it with consumer-grade models that often provide "statistical guesses" and can be unreliable for critical business functions. (Source: AI News)

Cloudflare has developed new infrastructure specifically designed to run large language models efficiently across its vast global network. This approach is designed to handle the high costs and heavy traffic associated with deploying AI at scale. (Source: InfoQ)

**Disneyland Deploys Facial Recognition Technology on Visitors**
In a move that raises new questions about privacy and surveillance, Disneyland has begun using facial recognition technology on its visitors. ### Research, Development, and Open Source

**François Chollet Creates AI Benchmark That Top Models Fail**
Renowned AI researcher François Chollet, who recently left Google, has developed a new benchmark designed to test for true abstract reasoning, a capability he argues is missing from current LLMs. The test, which humans pass with 100% accuracy, proved nearly impossible for OpenAI's latest GPT-5.5 model, which scored just 0.43%. Chollet's work aims to push the field beyond pattern matching and towards more general intelligence. (Source: Towards AI - Medium)

**NVIDIA Research Demonstrates Major Speedup for Model Generation**
A new paper from NVIDIA Research shows significant performance gains using speculative decoding within its NeMo RL framework. The technique achieved a 1.8x speedup in rollout generation for 8-billion-parameter models and projects a 2.5x end-to-end speedup for massive 235-billion-parameter models. This innovation promises to make training and deploying large-scale AI models faster and more cost-effective. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Qwen AI Releases 'Qwen-Scope' for LLM Interpretability**
The Qwen AI team has released Qwen-Scope, an open-source suite of Sparse AutoEncoders (SAEs) designed to help developers understand the internal workings of large language models. The tools turn the complex internal features of LLMs into practical, interpretable data, which can be used for analysis, fine-tuning, and improving model safety and performance. (Source: MarkTechPost)

**Sakana AI's 'KAME' Injects Real-Time Knowledge into Speech AI**
Sakana AI has introduced KAME, a novel tandem speech-to-speech architecture for conversational AI. The system is designed to inject knowledge from a large language model into a conversation in real-time without introducing audible latency. This breakthrough could lead to more knowledgeable and natural-sounding conversational agents. (Source: MarkTechPost)
